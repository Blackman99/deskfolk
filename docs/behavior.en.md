# Behavior

[简体中文](behavior.md)

This records behavior details for [`CONTEXT.en.md`](../CONTEXT.en.md); the glossary defines concepts, while versions, tools, APIs and migration boundaries belong here.

> **P4b scope**: the new work-item, desk, deterministic attribution and lead-routing paths below apply at `engine_level >= 2` (`ENGINE_LEVELS.work_items`), which also raises the database compatibility floor to 2. Levels 0/1 retain message-time organizing, ticket inheritance and redirect paths; that code has not been physically deleted. Level 2 makes no pre-turn organizer call for attribution, but not every pre-turn model call is gone: an old group without a confirmed lead still has participation judgements, and current model routing remains. Quiet-plan handover organizing (settle) remains too.
>
> **Partial P4c implementation (this round)**: `ENGINE_LEVELS.delegation = 3`, raising the compatibility floor to 3 when enabled. Level 3 has structured delegation, Bot-pair × plan direct reuse, non-waking ordinary private messages, nonterminal `send_message`, read-only delegation views and a domain ending function wired to the engine. Implemented and pending parts are separated below. Levels 0–2 retain old private-message/sending/ending paths; level 3 has not yet retired settle, plan-watch or report_back.
>
> **P4c supervisor (level 4)**: `ENGINE_LEVELS.supervision = 4`, raising the compatibility floor to 4 when enabled ([ADR 0045](adr/0045-supervisor.md)). Level 4 has the [supervisor](#supervisor): ball holder, call-backs to tickets nobody moves, picking up work that needs attention, I6 checks, restart resume by cause, the effect ledger, and the [work question card](#work-question) for blocked work; it no longer runs plan-watch/plan_nudge or report_back. Settle and the closing regexes remain at every level.
>
> This does not complete P4c: real implicit submission is still missing; the full ending contract is still under scoped acceptance. P4d polling, P4e `submit`/`review`, reviewer assignment with approved delivery/part review and P5 model policy/learning are outside this round. New-job card checks passed without certifying whole-phase acceptance.

<a id="direct"></a>
## Direct

Your direct with a Bot is reused. Bot↔Bot directs remain read-only for you: viewing work and existing safety controls does not make you a third speaker. **Level-3 delegation** reuses a non-archived direct with exactly those two present Bots for an unordered “Bot pair × plan”; reverse handoffs on that plan reuse it, different plans do not. No new conversation type exists: a collaboration thread is still a direct, distinct from a quoted reply to one transcript message.

At level 3 ordinary Bot↔Bot private messages, including prose endings and mentions of the present partner, create only `peer_note` mail with `wakes=0`. An existing segment can read it; an idle Bot is not woken. A note is not a delegation result and does not satisfy a wait. Naming an absent third Bot, an unknown name or everyone in such a direct returns `use_delegate` before posting. Use [Delegation](behavior.en.md#delegation) for actual work. `create_direct` is no longer offered at level 3 and returns `use_delegate` if called; the legacy implementation has not been physically deleted.

Levels 0–2 retain one fresh Bot↔Bot direct per initiation, ordinary replies/messages waking the other side (with the old nod-to-nod check retained), and quiet-thread report_back. Level 3 still has report_back/plan-watch/settle, so ordinary-message non-waking semantics are not a claim that every legacy quiet-thread callback is gone there; from level 4 report_back and plan-watch give way to the ball-holder [supervisor](#supervisor), while settle remains.

Quoted replies remain one level deep in the main transcript: replying to a Bot can mention it automatically, without duplicating existing mentions/everyone and without auto-mentioning the user; the parameter remains `parent_id`.

<a id="delegation"></a>
## Delegation

**Wired at level 3**: `delegate({to,ask,expects,ticket?,parts?,requirement_ids?,continue?})` requires a running work segment exactly bound to its work item and not held; a desk/read-only answer cannot impersonate it. `to` is an id or exact name of another non-archived Bot present in the segment's conversation, the work item's home or the plan's home. Tickets/parts must belong to the plan and cited requirements must apply to that work. Unknown targets do not relax these checks.

The app records the request, sender/recipient work items and originating turn, creates/reuses the recipient's work on the same plan/optional ticket and the exact Bot-pair direct, and queues priority-3 waking request mail. Unless `continue:true`, the sender becomes waiting with a `delegation_wait` record and its segment ends. `continue:true` creates no wait and keeps the segment going, while the delegation remains recorded. The sender may later finish independent work as answered/idle; a delayed answer still queues/wakes the eligible original sender without inventing a wait. Recipient admission/hearing/queueing still obeys I1/I1b, capacity and holds; being delegated does not duplicate a Bot's live work on the plan.

**A delivery approved answers its delegation (from level 5)**: a delegation that expects a deliverable (`expects: deliverable`) is answered by the app when the hand-over the recipient made for it is approved (by a review, by the app on its checks, or by your 放行): the result says who handed in what, the submission id and its files, and the delegating Bot is woken at once, out of its wait. One that names parts is answered once the approval covers them all; one that names none, once the ticket is through. Nothing answered such a delegation before: the delegating Bot waited for good and never heard (the three in your database were each cancelled only when their group was cleared). A rejection answers nothing — the recipient still owes it. While the recipient's hand-over for it is being checked, reviewed or waits on your 放行, the delegation is not counted as owed by the recipient, so its segment ends once it has handed in. It used to count, so `submit` answered "carry on" and the Bot rewrote and handed in again over the one under review (2026-10-03); sent back, the ticket is in rework and owed again. A request for a deliverable on a ticket the caller owns, naming no part, is refused: what the recipient hands over for it would be the whole ticket's hand-over — on 2026-10-04's real-model run the poster group's lead, without laying the job out, sent the slogans straight to 文案 on the job's one ticket, and 文案's 宣传语.txt went to you to approve as the whole job, so 放行 would have delivered it with no poster. The refusal names the two ways on: give the piece its own ticket with `plan_items` (the recipient as owner, the caller as reviewer) and delegate on that, or ask with `expects: "answer"` and hand the ticket over yourself. On the same run the lead took the second: 文案 answered with the three lines and the lead made and handed in the poster.

**A reply in words covers answer requests, and review requests with nothing handed over to review**: the recipient calls `end_turn({reason:"answered",answer:"…"})`, which resolves the open delegations exactly bound to it: every one with `expects="answer"`, and one with `expects="review"` only once it has read the request and nothing within the request's reach (its ticket, or the whole plan when it names none) is being checked or waits for review (checking/submitted/in_review) — say, a pre-review of a script or storyboard. A review request with a submission waiting goes through `review` ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-submissions-and-reviews.md)); `expects="deliverable"` has no reply in words. Ordinary closings or `send_message` are not results. The result commits once as priority-2 durable `delegation_reply` mail, marks replied and voids that delegation's wait, then queues/wakes an eligible sender. A held sender's result remains held. A reply in words only hands the verdict to the Bot that asked: it approves no submission and moves no ticket stage, and a prose claim cannot stand in for an approved review.

When a segment read such a request and ends answered without an answer (typically after posting its verdict with `send_message` in the thread), the ending bounces (`unanswered_request`) naming the request, who is waiting, and that the reply goes in answer; it shares the two-bounce budget, so running out ends needs_attention instead of quietly leaving a request nobody closes. On a done or prose ending, the unfinished-obligations bounce also says how to answer such a request it read.

**Event waits are not timer check-backs**: due scanning excludes `delegation_wait` at every level. Only this delegation's reply/cancellation resolves it. Level-3 timers replace by exact work item/dedupe key, not another job or event wait of the same Bot/conversation. A hold suspends/restores the matching records without an unrelated newer timer clobbering an event wait. With no real due time the view's `due_at` is `null`, not an internal placeholder displayed as a countdown.

**Hold cascades** follow still-open delegation edges whose plan/ticket bindings match, captured as downstream Bot × plan targets when the hold is made. They do not trust one `delegated_by` parent: one recipient item can have several senders. Stopping the owner's job covers its helper on that job while the helper's other plan continues. Later delegations do not quietly widen an existing snapshot, and only user actions lift holds.

Archiving/deleting a Bot or clearing/deleting a related conversation cancels affected open delegations and records the result. Archiving the recipient gives the still-existing eligible original sender one waking cancellation result; archiving the sender does not wake itself. Conversation-clear/delete cancellation results all have `wakes=0`, never restarting work because a chat was cleared. Requests/results remain recorded; cancellation is not completion. Archiving also closes that Bot's work items, ends live execution and aborts it after commit. The side-effect guard rechecks Bot eligibility, a nonclosed work item and execution authority, so an in-flight completion cannot keep writing after archival.

**Read-only views**: local and remote-whitelisted `GET /v1/tasks/:id/delegations` and `GET /v1/sessions/:id/delegations` return persisted `DelegationView` facts: who asked whom, the expectation, open/replied/cancelled, request/result, waiting/held and a real nullable due time. Live `delegation.changed` plus catch-up updates them. The pair direct shows structured delegation/result/wait records, not inferred prose status. These are read-only projections, not new user delegation buttons. Components are wired, and the parent confirmed synthetic-scenario checks in two browsers. That verifies the read-only view/interaction, not a paid real-model collaboration-loop acceptance.

**Speaking differs from ending**: level-3 successful `send_message` does not end the segment, allowing a note followed by more work. A segment may send at most 3 user-visible progress messages without `parent_id`; the fourth returns `progress_limit`. Currently `parent_id` is the exemption marker, with no implemented semantic classification of a real question: an arbitrary quoted reply is not a proven answer exemption. Group mentions can still wake; ordinary pair-direct messages do not. Files written after an earlier progress post remain tracked as uncited paths and can attach to the closing message; the earlier send does not lose later artifacts. This is message citation, not implicit submission.

**Ending is wired, but 24b is not complete**: level-3 `end_turn` reasons are done/answered/nothing_new/blocked/gave_up. It validates running status, exact work-item/plan/ticket binding and holds, records dispositions for read user/annotation mail and writes `turns.end_reason` / `work.ended`. Unacknowledged user mail bounces, except on a prose ending: the lines of yours the segment read and gave no word about are recorded as answered by that prose. Prose first passes retained closing/run-evidence checks; a bounce writes no premature work.ended, and the ending contract runs afterwards. Prose in a desk/readonly answer is answered; work prose uses the same contract. With unfinished To do/In progress/In review tickets, open delegations or waits and no valid wait, done/prose bounces once and then ends as nothing_new, not a submission. Two no-progress endings with obligations can become blocked and notify (in your interface language, naming the job and the Bot). With nothing owed on the work (no ticket of its own to do, no request, no wait), when the segment's last word to you (the closing itself for prose, else its newest message) says it is still working (正在编写, 结论随后, "I'll put the storyboard together and post it", "I'm now drafting", as read by [line reading](#line-reading); by the words of `later-words.ts` when it cannot be read) and names no Bot to take it, done/answered/nothing_new bounces once (`promised_later`) with its own words: do it now, or book a check-back and end with nothing_new, or name who takes it. The next ending goes through, and a line in the conversation (in your interface language) says it said "…" but its turn has ended and nobody is carrying on with it, and to @ it to have it go on (in your direct with it, to tell it: whatever you say there reaches it; the two-endings-without-progress line does not ask you to @ it there either). On 2026-10-03 视频导演 ended as done eight seconds after saying it was writing the new EP01 setting bible and storyboard; the plan's one ticket still read as handed over from the earlier run, nothing woke it again, and the group showed no sign it had stopped. A segment a line of yours opened in your direct that is about to end with no word to you (no closing reply about to go out, no message of its own with words in it there, no words handed over; files alone are no reply — files it wrote, a line that only carries files, files handed over) bounces once too, on done/answered/nothing_new (`said_nothing`): what goes into `end_turn` — `answer` (only for a Bot that asked), note, inbox — never reaches you, and files are not a reply, so it is told to reply in the conversation; the next ending goes through with a line in your direct (in your interface language) saying it ended its turn without replying and to say it again to have it answer. On 2026-10-07 通识 put its whole answer into `answer`, and your line got nothing; the same day 视频导演 answered 「水印去不掉吗」 and 「它支持传参考图吗」 with files alone. In such a segment `submit` does not end the segment either, and tells the Bot to reply first (no bounce counted). Lines in a group, read-only answers and segments a routine woke are not covered. When the end contract sends a pure-text closing reply back (files of an approved ticket changed, a ticket not yet handed over…), the reply has already gone out: the bounce is about the work, not the words, and only the closing check holds the words back; the bounce opens with "your reply has gone out as written; do not say it again", and the same words go out once per segment. Held back before, the reply never went out once the Bot handed its files over as told and `submit` ended the segment — on 2026-10-07 视频导演's written answer to 「这个视频生成跟 Grok 的比哪个好」 was lost that way, leaving a folder card. An actually observed ticket-stage change is authoritative progress recorded as ticket.stage_changed with the matching turn id: a productive current segment's first ending is not counted as empty cycling, while later quiet endings accumulate. Unacknowledged-inbox/unfinished-obligation bounces and filing refusals share the two-bounce budget, ending as needs_attention when exhausted. In a desk segment, thrown malformed `work_on`/`end_turn` arguments count too (an unknown reason, blocked without needs_from_user, gave_up without a note), suppressing later effects in the same hop. Once the segment is bound, a refused `delegate`, `work_on` or `end_turn` does not count: it is an ordinary failed call the Bot reads and retries, three failures of one tool in a row step the job up, and hop limits bound the segment; `delegate` binds a desk before it runs, so its validation never counts. Before, these counted too, and working segments that had done their work were cut off with the desk's line 「这句话需要先选归属，尚未执行有副作用的工具」.

A work item with a real timer/delegation wait stays waiting. A completed ticket or desk can close; other answered/nothing_new work may idle. Blocked needs `needs_from_user`, gave_up needs a note and records a notice/event. Blocked is only for what you alone can give: for a wait on another Bot, the tool description, the system prompt and the unfinished-obligations bounce all tell the Bot to delegate to it or end with nothing_new, rather than turn the wait into a question to you. **Gaps remain**: at level 3 blocked produces an app system line; the persistent answerable card is level 4's ([work question card](#work-question)); I5 now has level-3 database triggers: a bound completed/stopped/interrupted/redirected row needs a nonblank end_reason on INSERT and every UPDATE, without restricting it to a reason enum. Legacy levels 0–2 are exempt; historical terminal rows get a one-time status backfill without overwriting explicit reasons. Old closing-check regexes still run. `implicitSubmission.implemented=false` only lists metadata for cited artifacts under the ticket directory: no existence/new-hash validation, bound checks, stored submission or submitted/approved transition. Supervisor 24c (orphan thresholds, periodic I6 checks, hourly recovery budgets, automatic restart policy, retiring old timers) is implemented at level 4, see [Supervisor](#supervisor); level 3 has none of it.

<a id="check-back"></a>
## Check-back

A check-back is a Bot's timer reminder to itself, not a delegation's event wait. Existing `check_back(after_minutes,note)` remains one minute to seven days; this round does not rename it into the planned `wait_for(minutes>=5)` or enable external-job polling. When due it creates a private trigger for that Bot under the original plan/ticket, without changing plan stage itself. Holds suspend it until a user lift permits firing.

**Level-3 scope change**: timer replacement/restoration uses exact work-item/dedupe keys, not “one appointment per Bot per conversation”, and cannot overwrite `delegation_wait`. Levels 0–2 retain legacy conversation-scoped dedupe, while no level fires delegation waits through due scanning. At levels 0–3 the old quiet-plan/direct plan_nudge/report_back remain; from level 4 they no longer run, ones booked before and still pending are voided when they come due, and the [supervisor](#supervisor) wakes the ball holder instead. In the dispatch queue a Bot's own appointments and routines come last (priority 4, after your lines 1, delegation results 2, supervisor wakes and delegation requests 3).

<a id="supervisor"></a>
## Supervisor

From level 4 ([ADR 0045](adr/0045-supervisor.md)), on every 15-second scheduler tick, before due check-backs, the supervisor does three things in one write; levels 3 and below do none of it. Each call-back or pick-up is a fired `check_backs` row (`kind = supervisor`, its `wait_spec` saying why, which attempt, which progress cycle or restart), and repairs, restarts and notices go to the work log as `supervisor.*`; every time is read from those records, so a restart loses no deadline and repeats no wake. Delegation replies and cancellations, your answer on a question card, and lines queued into an inbox still queue in their own write and dispatch at once, without waiting for the tick.

- **I6**: a waiting work item whose object is gone (the request cancelled, the other side's work closed, the check-back gone) needs attention (`supervisor.wait_invalid`), and in the same tick gets a Bot-only line saying that wait no longer stands. A wait a stop covers is a valid wait.
- **Idle work with mail in line**: an idle work item with queued mail that would wake it is queued again (`supervisor.requeued`), so the dispatcher sends it. Only queued work is dispatched, and the mail in line keeps the supervisor from calling the Bot back, so the two would wait on each other for good. A segment's end does the same: mail queued on its work while it ran, unread by it, is the work's next step, and the work stays queued instead of going idle. On 2026-10-04's real-model run the copywriter was reviewing the poster when the lead handed in a newer version; the new review request was queued on the same work, the review segment then ended nothing_new and set the work idle, and the newer version was never reviewed.
- **Work that needs attention**: a segment interrupted (a restart, a drain), failed (failed again after one retry on the same model, stuck past its limit, a runtime error), out of its two ending-contract bounces, or with its wait gone puts its work item in `needs_attention` (an interruption or failure in the same write as the segment's end; a running item with no live segment whose last segment was interrupted or failed is caught up by the tick; one whose last segment ended any other way — a Stop whose hold you lifted with its button, a closing the contract did not see — and that no stop covers goes idle on the tick, where the call-backs below find it). The supervisor picks it up: from that segment's own interrupted or failure line, as its Continue would — same conversation, same stops and slots, the line marked continued and its notification resolved — or, with no such line, with a Bot-only line saying what happened to the last segment, its last step and which attempt this is. **At most 3 times an hour per work item**; then one line in a conversation you are in, with a notification, and no more automatic pick-ups. A failed turn of work it will pick up gets no "turn not finished" notification of its own (from 2026-10-04): you are told by its line and notification once it stops retrying, or when the last step has no known outcome; a failure it does not take up (a turn on no job it watches, levels 3 and below) is notified as before. When the engine cannot continue from that line (the conversation archived, the job already under way elsewhere), the pick-up is recorded as `supervisor.pickup_refused` and voided: no attempt, no hourly budget spent, and the next tick picks the work up by a queued wake rather than that line again. The same Bot working on another job in the same conversation is no refusal any more (see [Work item](#work-item)). Not while a stop covers it, the ticket is closed, or another request's recipient holds the ball; work a restart cut off follows the restart's cause first (see [Interrupted](#interrupted)). Mail in line on the work is no reason to wait (work that needs attention is not dispatched: on 2026-10-04's real-model run a review request queued on work whose segment had just failed made the two wait on each other); the segment that picks it up reads that mail too.
- **Tickets nobody moves**: an open ticket whose [ball holder](#ball-holder) is its owner, the plan's lead or an unanswering request recipient, where that Bot has no live segment, no valid wait and no queued line on the job, and no work cut off or failed on any ticket of the job that has not been taken up again (a Bot has one live segment per job, and the cut work comes first: while a restart notice said it would not go on by itself and waited for your Continue, or after you chose not to continue, the Bot used to be called to the next ticket of the same job two minutes later — on 2026-10-04 it redrew the cut ticket's frames under the next ticket), in an active, non-dormant, non-routine plan whose dependencies are done, is called back once it has been quiet past the threshold, with only the facts (which ticket, how long quiet, owner/lead/which request it owes) and the ending contract's choice. The threshold is **2 minutes** from the job's last activity (lines filed to it, its segments' activity, its work log); **10 minutes** when, among conversations you are in, the job's last line is a Bot's and came after your last line in the job, counted from that or from your last line in that conversation, whichever is later — whether it asked anything is not read. **At most 2 call-backs per progress cycle**; then one line "This has stopped" in a conversation you are in (the plan's first), with a notification, once per cycle.
- **Progress** is only what the work log records: a ticket's stage changing (board, organizer and observed work all record `ticket.stage_changed`), a check passing for the first time (`check.first_passed`; a model-judged seams check must pass twice in a row), a new content hash in the ticket's folder (written files are hashed, streamed, when the segment ends and recorded as `artifact.changed`; reserved folders and files over 2 GB are left out), and deliveries and reviews once P4e writes them. Editing a description, a changed `updated_at`, or words do not count.
- **From the moment it is on**: when the database reaches level 4 the work log records `engine.level_raised`. Tickets already quiet, and work already waiting or needing attention before then, are not all woken or picked up on the first tick (even with dozens of old plans open at the upgrade); once anything happens in them (a line of yours, a board change, a new segment) they are the supervisor's. Work cut off by the restart that did the upgrade is not "before", and goes on by the restart's cause.
- **Its lines**: the app's system lines, out of every Bot's context, with `control` `{kind: "supervisor", code: "stalled" | "retry_budget" | "unknown_effect", task_id, ticket_id, work_item_id, offer: []}` and no buttons, plus a notification (`failure`, titled "stopped"). The line says how to go on: @ the Bot, press that segment's Continue, or change the ticket on the board.
- **Effect ledger** (`tool_executions`): `write_file`, `delete_file`, `shell` and non-read-only MCP calls record a start before acting (once per call; a repeat does not run and returns `repeated_effect`) and an outcome when done: succeeded, refused, failed or unknown — unknown when aborted, when an MCP call errored after it was sent, on a timeout, or when still running as the segment ended. The supervisor treats an unknown shell or MCP call as an external call with no known outcome: that segment does not go on by itself, and a line tells you (unless a restart notice already did). An older segment with no ledger rows but shell/MCP runs in its record counts the same when an interrupted segment would be picked up.
- **Submissions (from level 5)**: each tick (outside your stops and dormant plans) also takes on [submissions](#submission) checked, with no reviewer, waiting longer than 15 seconds, by their checks as they are now: one whose ticket has a reviewer by now goes to it, an organizer's reading included; a gate that ran and failed sends it back to its producer (a line wakes it), one not finished yet is waited for while the app runs it; an organizer's reading and an answer are never approved on their checks alone, period: a card asks you to approve it or send it back; a file hand-over (explicit or implicit) is approved on its checks only when at least one check you wrote or confirmed is bound to it and every gate passes — the organizer's checks still send it back when they fail, but their pass backs nothing, since one may only ask that a file exists (`submission.approved`, `by: no_reviewer`) — with none bound at all it ends on the same card, which opens to the files themselves. A required item (said twice or more, or about the picture) with no passing check you confirmed behind it is yours to look at too: it is listed on that same approve/reject card, with what the app measured on it, and approving counts it as met, the ball yours — one card per hand-over (from 2026-10-07; before, such items got a card of their own first, another for each one read while it waited, and only then the approve/reject card). A routine's runs never end on a card: they are approved once their gates pass (`by: routine`, see [Submission](#submission)). Nor does a file hand-over made in one go (2026-10-06): on a job with one ticket not dropped, which no approval or required-items card has ever asked you about, handed over by the only segment since the job opened (or was last approved), it is approved (`by: one_go`), required items with nothing behind them included (from 2026-10-07); segments that start after the hand-over (your next line while it waits, say) do not count. A job of several tickets, of several segments, or once on your card still asks you. While a hand-over waits on you, card up or not, a line of yours on its job that its Bot is at work on (a segment opened by or hearing it still running, a question to you unanswered, a generation job of its own awaited, the line still queued) holds it: no card, and one already up comes down saying 「你又说了这件事，等X按你说的做完再问你。」; what the Bot hands over for the line supersedes it and is what you are asked about, and if the work ends with nothing new handed over, this one comes to a new card that says so first (from 2026-10-07; segments that only read, another Bot's work, lines about another ticket and a 放行 you already pressed do not count; from 2026-10-08 a segment that heard the line counts on whichever ticket of the job it was). A hand-over that needs your look comes to its card only once its Bot has stopped working on the job: no segment of its running there (one waiting on your answer or approval, or only reading, does not count), no generation job of its own still out, nothing just queued that wakes it, and its last segment there ended more than a tick ago; a large job's sample counts its own ticket only. What it hands over meanwhile supersedes the older one, which never came to a card; each hand-over held this way records `submission.card_deferred` once (from 2026-10-08, `stillAtWork`). A submission waiting on your card is taken up again each tick against what is true then: a card waiting only on its required items (a check you confirmed backs the rest) approves it with no resubmission once they no longer hold here or a confirmed check passes on them, and says 「已放行。」; any other approve/reject card waits on your press alone, never flipped by a later tick on its own — pressing Approve while a gate has not run yet runs it first and takes the press up again once it has, not a bare approval and not a failure either: meanwhile the card reads 「等检查跑完再放行…」 with only Send back left, and a second Approve is refused; a gate added while it waits is run at the next tick before deciding; the card does not say Approved until it actually is, and a failure shows as sent back with what failed. A card a newer hand-over took over, or one your status change on the board voided, loses its buttons and says which. A ticket in review that nobody moves calls back its reviewer; **a reviewer's approve does not always approve it either**: not an organizer's reading or an answer, which nobody actually made, nor a file hand-over no check you wrote or confirmed backs when the reviewer runs on the producer's own model (a model nobody recorded counts as the same) or gave no evidence on a requirement that applies to it (a made-up requirement id does not count) — one Bot passing another's claim. Then its approve only moves the submission to awaiting you, the card showing the reviewer's own verdict (name, model, whether it ran on the producer's own, its notes); its reject still sends the ticket to rework as before. The tick an organizer reads a ticket as done, if its producer still has a segment running on it, or a work item that is not idle, that reading is skipped rather than raced ahead of real work; the organizer tries again next time it reads the ticket as done.
- **Not done**: a failing plan-level check bound to no ticket calls nobody back (levels 0–3's plan call-back would; one bound to a ticket sends the ball back to its owner); external job polling (P4d), listed as `unsupported` in every tick's result (level 4 also lists reviewer assignment, which [submissions](#submission) take on from level 5); the board does not show "ball with: …" yet.

<a id="ball-holder"></a>
## Ball holder

Derived for each open ticket in order; the first that holds is it:
1. An open request on it whose recipient can still take work: the ball is with the recipient's work item. Not a request for a deliverable whose recipient's hand-over is in its checks or in review (waiting on your 放行 included): the ball is then the reviewer's or yours, as in 5, and approval answers the request. It used to count as owed by the recipient, so while your 放行 waited the supervisor called the recipient back every three minutes to "answer" it, and it handed the same work in each time (2026-10-04 walkthrough: five hand-overs in twelve minutes).
2. A question awaiting your answer: a live turn's ask card, or the [work question card](#work-question) a blocked work item left.
3. A blocked work item: the ball is yours.
4. A stop of yours covers it, or a ticket it depends on: yours (paused). Held work never counts as stalled and is not reassigned.
5. Awaiting review: yours — unless a check bound to this ticket (its `ticket_id`, or a path/folder inside its folder) failed when it last ran, which sends it back to the owner. From level 5 this reads the [submission](#submission) instead: while its approval waits on your answer to a card, it is yours; in review, the reviewer holds it; handed over with no reviewer, the app (taking it on at the next tick); rework is the owner's; a ticket left in review from before level 5 with no submission is still yours.
6. Otherwise the ticket's owner, then the plan's lead, and with neither, unclaimed (yours). External jobs (handed to the poller) arrive with P4d.

The **owner** is `tickets.owner_bot_id`, written with `worker`; at the upgrade it is imported once from a worker that can still take work, else the Bot with the most turns on that ticket that can. The **plan's lead** is read, not stored: the stored `lead_bot_id`, else the Bot that opened the plan, else a group lead you confirmed in one of the job's groups (the home's first, then where you spoke last), else the Bot with the most turns in the plan — each only while it can take work and is not archived. Storing it would change who gets your unaddressed group lines, so it is not stored. Able to take work means not archived or deleted and in one of the job's conversations: its home, or one you spoke about it in. A job often opens in a direct and goes on in a group (on 2026-10-03 《一拳超人》 opened in your direct with 审片员 and went on in the group once 审片员 was archived): counting the home alone, no Bot doing it in the group could take work and its tickets were nobody's. A holder with no work item is woken in one of the job's conversations it is in (where you spoke about the job last first), and the supervisor's notices go where you last spoke about the job, never to the direct of a Bot you archived or deleted.

**Dependencies** (`tickets.depends_on`) come only from you: `PATCH /v1/tickets/:id {depends_on: [...]}` (local and remote), other tickets of the same plan only, never itself, never a loop, never a newly added parked one, at most 39; while any is not done (a parked one does not count as waited for) the ticket is not called back, and while one is held the ball is yours. Whichever field changes, a ticket's owner is never its reviewer. On the board's ticket list, the picked ticket's "Depends on" setting opens checkboxes to change them, and the line under each ticket's title says what it waits for.

**The ball on the board**: from level 4, in an active plan that is not dormant, each open ticket on the list says who has the ball — with a Bot, waiting on the lead to hand it out, on a delegated part, waiting on someone's review, approved by the app at the next tick, waiting on a render, or waiting on you (an answer, something stuck, a stop, acceptance or an approval, the capability ceiling, nobody on it); at level 4 a ticket waiting for acceptance says to mark it done on the board, and only from level 5, with an approval card, does it say approval. In a parked, done or dormant plan nobody waits, and nothing is said. A dependency cannot be a parked ticket (it would never end) or one that makes a loop.

## Work question card

From level 4, `end_turn({reason: "blocked", needs_from_user})` leaves a question card (`control.kind = "work_question"`, with the work item, plan and ticket) where the segment asked (when you are in it and it is not archived), else where you last spoke about the job, else the job's conversation, else the plan's, else your direct with the Bot (it used to go to the job's conversation first: a job opened in a group and taken up in your direct asked you to sign off in the direct while its card landed in the group, 2026-10-04); its author is the Bot, it shows as the app's card, and it comes with a "waiting for your answer" notification. The work item is blocked with `waiting_on = {kind: "user"}`, and the ball is yours. The same question from the same segment leaves one card.

`POST /v1/messages/:id/work-answer {body}` (on the phone too) records your words once: the same request id sent again queues nothing more, and answering an answered card under another request is refused. The answer goes into the job's inbox as a line of yours (priority 1; the words also become a [user quote](#user-quote)), the work item queues and dispatches at once, and the card records the answer and when, its notification resolved. When a stop covers the job, or the conversation the segment ran in, the answer is only held — saved all the same — and no stop is lifted by it; the work goes on after the lift. A card already answered, superseded by a later question, whose segment has not ended yet, whose work is closed, whose Bot is archived, or whose conversation is archived or cleared refuses the answer, and the draft stays on the card. When the job goes on without your answer (a later question, a segment woken by another line ending, the work closing), the card records `superseded_at` and reads "This work was waiting for your answer", saying it takes no answer any more, with no answer box; its notification is voided (`superseded`) and no longer counts toward the Dock badge. Opening the database does the same for the cards an older build left behind. An answered card is left as it is. At levels 3 and below blocked posts a plain line.

**Nothing goes up that only asks your OK** (from 2026-10-04, [ADR 0058](adr/0058-the-app-asks-only-when-it-needs-you.md)): from level 3, a blocked ending's `needs_from_user` and an `ask_user` question are read once by [line reading](#line-reading); read as only asking your OK to go on with what you asked for — a sign-off on work in progress, whether it may start the next step — the `end_turn` is sent back (`asks_go_ahead`, one of the ending contract's bounces) and `ask_user` returns an error (the same code, work log `ask.go_ahead_refused`), with no card and no notification. The Bot hears that what you want is already said: not to wait for an OK or for you to look first, but to go on, as you look at what it hands over (and can stop it at any time); and to stop only for what you alone can give (an account, a permission, something only you know) or a choice between options that is yours, saying which. Once per segment: stopped or asked again, the question reaches you, unread; and when the contract's two bounces are already spent it reaches you too, never ending the work as needing attention. On 2026-10-04 the video director handed in the keyframe board for 《一拳超人》 and stopped blocked on 「请确认关键帧板（board.jpg）与设定集是否符合预期，确认后将正式启动视频片段生成与后期剪辑」; the card waited two and a half hours for your 「确认」. With no model to read lines, an error or no reading, the question goes to you as before.

<a id="when-the-app-asks"></a>
## When the app asks you

The app itself (not a Bot) asks you for something only at three kinds of moment: when you have to let an action or a piece of work through; when it needs what only you can give (a permission, a credential, something only you know, a call only you can make); and when it has stopped and will not go on by itself. What it decided for you and you can change at any time, what you have just said, and what it will go on with by itself, it does not ask you about ([ADR 0058](adr/0058-the-app-asks-only-when-it-needs-you.md), 2026-10-04). "Asks" here is a card, a line with buttons or a "waiting on you" notification; Bots' own replies and routine results are not counted.

It asks you:
- **Approval**: before a dangerous action, for the one step it lets through.
- **A Bot's question, and the [work question card](#work-question)**: the Bot needs your judgment, or something it cannot get. Whether to ask is the Bot's call; the app only puts it where you are, and one that only asks your OK to go on is first sent back once for the Bot to go on.
- **The approve/reject card** (from level 5): a hand-over nothing backs — no reviewer, no check you wrote or confirmed — or a requirement you said twice, or about the picture, with nothing behind it ([Submission](#submission), [Review](#review)). One card per hand-over: such requirements are listed on it, and approving counts them as met; it goes up once the Bot that made it has stopped working on the job (see 2026-10-08 below). Not for a routine's runs, nor for work made in one go.
- **The capability-ceiling card**: one requirement failed again and again; it stops to ask you for another way, another plan, a relaxed requirement or the work as it is ([Review](#review)).
- **The lesson card** (from level 8): a checklist item or check a Bot proposes after a missed review or the ceiling, in force only once you take it up ([Quality event, Lesson](#learning)).
- **The replacement card of a check from your words**: a number you gave later differs from a check in force ([Check from your words](#check-from-your-words)).
- **The line saying it stopped**: the supervisor called a Bot back twice with no progress, used its three pick-ups an hour, or met a last step with no known outcome ([Supervisor](#supervisor)); the end contract saw two endings with no progress, or a promise of more and then an ending ([Delegation](#delegation)); a desk segment failed to choose its job twice ([Desk segment](#desk-segment)); a restart cut off work that will not go on by itself ([Interrupted](#interrupted)).
- **A failure nothing takes up**: the "turn not finished" notification for a failed turn whose work the supervisor does not pick up (on no job it watches, levels 3 and below).
- **Notes about models**: a pinned model no endpoint lists, one that cannot see the pictures its work needs, a job stepped up as far as it goes ([Model](#model)) — each said once, with no buttons.
- Replies to what you just did: the receipt of a stop you said or chose from a stop menu, 「已转回返工」 ("Sent back to rework") with its Undo under a line of yours that found fault, the line an annotation batch makes. "Did it stop?", a status question and a go on go to the Bot, which answers itself (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)).

It no longer asks you (2026-10-04):
- The default-model card [Keep it] [Use the endpoint's default]: the default is used; pin a model to change it.
- Work a restart cut off that goes on by itself (or that your stop holds): no line, no notification, and its turns' own notifications are voided.
- A failed turn the supervisor will pick up: no "turn not finished" notification.
- Checks from your words: no card for an offer, for "You have said it N times", for an offer a gate went back to, or for a gate finding its file; offers are confirmed on the flow board.
- The requirements ledger's "Did you say these?" card for old rules, and the card offering to make a requirement standing.
- The approve/reject and required-items cards for a routine's runs: approved once their gates pass.
- The receipt of a Stop on a turn's card: it stopped the turn you pressed it on, and your next line lifts it.
- A Bot's question (an ask or a work question card) that only asks your OK to go on: sent back once for the Bot to go on; asked again, it reaches you.

Since 2026-10-06 it no longer asks you either:
- The "New job: …" card of a newly opened job (with Undo and Merge): the tag under your line already names the job it opened, and a click on it changes that; to stop or drop the job, say so or press Stop. None of the four ever posted was pressed.
- The approve/reject card of work made in one go: on a job of one ticket that never asked you, or that had a hand-over approved, done and handed over by the only segment since it opened or since that approval, it is approved. It is already in front of you; if it is wrong, say so: a line read as finding fault sends it back to rework at once (see [Review](#review)). From 2026-10-07 a job that once asked you counts once you approved it: each follow-up made by one segment used to need another press (five cards in half an hour). After one you sent back, the next still asks you.

Since 2026-10-07 it no longer asks you either:
- The required-items card "nothing backs these" (Confirm the check, Count them as met, Stop requiring them): those requirements are listed on the hand-over's approve/reject card, and approving counts them as met; a requirement read while the card waits adds no card. A hand-over used to ask about its requirements and then for approval: a picture of a cat on a balcony brought three cards in 26 seconds, and each of the six "met" presses in the database was followed by another press on an approval card.
- A hand-over made in one go with a requirement about the picture (or said twice) that nothing backs: the line that opens a picture job is always filed as one, so pictures made in one go still came to a card; now they go through like any other work made in one go.
- The approval card of an old version while the Bot works on what you said since: none is put up, and one already up comes down; the new version is what you are asked about, or this one again if nothing new is handed over. Seven seconds after 「把猫改成橘猫」 a card still asked whether to let the unchanged picture through.


Since 2026-10-08:
- **Approval cards while the Bot is still at work**: when a hand-over waits for your 放行, no card goes up while its Bot is still at work on the job — a segment of its running there (on any ticket; one waiting on your answer or approval, or only reading, does not count), a generation job of its own still out, something just queued that wakes it, or less than one tick (15 s) since it stopped — the card goes up once it has stopped. What it hands over meanwhile supersedes the older one, which never came to a card. A large job's sample is the exception: the rest waits on it, so only its own ticket counts, and it still comes to you while the Bot prepares the next parts. A card already up does not come down because the Bot starts again. While 视频导演 reworked a sample, every stop to wait for its video jobs put the files it had just cited on an approval card, and a run of tickets put a card between its progress lines after each one ([ADR 0058](adr/0058-the-app-asks-only-when-it-needs-you.md) §16).
- **Half-made files from a failed segment**: a segment that ends failed (an endpoint error and the like) no longer hands over the files it had cited; a card used to ask about those scripts 15 seconds after the supervisor said the outcome was unknown and it would not go on by itself. A version the Bot handed over itself with `submit` is no longer superseded at the same segment's end by the clips and frames it cited besides: the card listed the clips, and 放行 approved them rather than the master (§17).
- **What you said, heard in the Bot's segment on another ticket**: what counts is the ticket your line is filed under, not the ticket of the segment that heard it; your line about the second part, heard while the Bot re-rendered the sample, took that part's card down and 12 seconds later it was back, saying nothing new was handed over (§18).

From 2026-10-09 it no longer asks you either, nor answers for a Bot ([ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)):
- **The complaint card** "You said "…" — send it back to rework?": the app asking about what you had just said. A line the reader reads as finding fault with work handed over sends it back to rework at once, and under your line it says 「已转回返工：……」 ("Sent back to rework: …") with an Undo ([Review](#review)).
- **The status line**: a line that only asks where the work stands goes to the Bot — read at its next step if it is working, without cutting it off, or waking it if not — and the Bot answers. That morning you asked 「最终成片呢」 ("where's the final cut?") twice in 视频导演's direct, and both times the answer was a status block the app put together under its name, while the Bot heard neither line; it was not working at all, so the block could only say nobody was.
- **The status line in place of a read-only answer that said nothing**, and **a go on's receipt**: a stopped Bot says itself that the stop still holds; one that goes on says so itself ([Hold](#hold)).

Cards of these kinds already out still take their buttons.

**The retrospective** (from level 8, 2026-10-06) does not ask you either: the memory and skill changes it makes are only listed in that job's Plan view on the trace, each one undoable ([Quality event, Lesson](#learning)).

<a id="work-item"></a>
## Work item

The work identity is “Bot × plan × optional ticket”; without a plan, a desk work item is “Bot × conversation”. The new path binds each execution segment to a work item: neither a turn nor a ticket is a work item. A work item has at most one live segment (I1), and a Bot has at most one on a plan (I1b), including Running, Waiting approval and Waiting ask. Plan-level and ticket-level items cannot bypass the second constraint; other Bots can still work on their own items in that plan.

Once a Bot is working on a job, a later line filed under it goes to that segment's durable inbox: another conversation, another ticket or a user mention in a group does not redirect it or open a copy. A different plan opens its own segment, subject to slots and holds. **Current slot scope**: each Bot defaults to at most two work segments globally. A desk uses no work slot; at most one live desk exists per Bot × conversation, with no global one-desk-per-Bot constraint. Code honors a valid positive integer in `bots.parallel_limit`, falling back to 2 when absent/invalid; this change adds no interface for changing it. Mail and work items beyond the work slots persist as queued, and the conversation says “Queued, at position N”. When a slot is available the dispatcher tries in priority and inbox order, including a cold queue with no live target. A held or dormant target does not start. Clearing the transcript does not resume work automatically; once you explicitly resume the target and it is eligible for admission, queued mail with a cleared source can create a Bot-only system trigger from its body snapshot, still passing slots and holds. This is not automatic restart supervision.

**State after a segment ends**: when a segment ends (a closing, Stop, interruption, failure), its work item does not stay `running`. Level 2 has no end contract, and from level 3 an ending the contract did not see (Stop, a closing without end_turn) is the same: the engine settles it at the segment's end, after the mail it did not read is handled — a desk's is closed, one on a plan goes idle, for the job's next line, its queue or the supervisor to take up; with unread mail still there it is queued. At level 4 a segment cut off (interruption, failure) still leaves `needs_attention`. After a crash, the next boot settles whatever the dead process left running the same way (at level 4 only desks: jobs on a plan are the supervisor's to repair by how their last segment ended).

**Continue is not limited per conversation**: from level 2, Continue on an interrupted or failed segment is limited only by one segment per job (I1b; for a desk, the conversation's desk) and the work slots. It works while the same Bot works on another job in the same conversation, instead of `bot already has a live turn`; a live segment on the same job answers `409 already_working`.

**Typing while it waits on your answer**: from level 2, a line of yours that reaches a segment waiting on your answer to its question (waiting_ask), said in the conversation where it asked and carrying no files, is your answer to that question — text of your own rather than one of its choices — and the segment goes on with it, instead of waiting in an inbox the waiting segment never reads. A line with files, said elsewhere, or a Bot's line still goes to the inbox.

If `work_on` selects a job another segment of this Bot already occupies, that segment receives this segment's actual trigger message, not just a summary, and this segment ends as `merged`. Tickets retain distinct identities without allowing the Bot to do the plan twice concurrently. Unread mail adopted by a new segment retains its body snapshot and disposition audit; correcting already-read attribution does not rewrite the old segment's history.

<a id="desk-segment"></a>
## Desk segment

When no reference decides attribution and the reading did not place the line either (read as new, as no telling which, or not read; see [Attribution](#attribution)), the called Bot opens its own `mode=desk` segment: its first hop, not an organizer's side-call guess. While that desk segment is open in the conversation, the next unfiled line for that Bot (by mention, lead, `@everyone`, a join judgement or a fork in your direct alike) is heard in it at its next hop; no second desk segment opens on the same work item, so no uniqueness refusal loses the line. If the segment ends before reading it, the line opens the next desk segment as its trigger instead of staying queued in the inbox. The candidate-id set is captured at admission, the same set the reading was shown (the conversation's candidates and the jobs of the six lines before the trigger). The desk prompt shows each captured plan's id/title, last activity, up to 3 recent artifact paths and its latest non-erased user quote, clipped to 300 display characters; absent artifacts or quotes are omitted. This evidence can refresh each hop without recomputing or widening the selectable ids. An unavailable captured candidate is marked unavailable, not replaced by a new one. `work_on` can choose only a plan in that snapshot, or open a new job by quoting a real user message from this conversation. A plan added later cannot quietly enter the old segment's choices.

Before choosing, the segment is read-only: reading files, listing directories, no-effect queries and MCP tools marked `readOnlyHint`, plus replies, `ask_user`, `work_on` and `end_turn`. The first call asking for a side effect resolves attribution before execution — unless the line was read as something done in place:
- **Read as done in place** ([ADR 0057](adr/0057-the-job-a-line-is-about-is-read.md)): however many candidates, the situation says the app read the line as something done right here, the reply saying how it went (a question, a look-up, a setting, a small action); effects run as usual (refused under a stop, cards as ever, commands from the workspace root) and open no job, and the reply ends it as answered. A Bot that finds there is something to hand over after all opens a job itself with `work_on({new})` first. Whether a job opens is the reading's call, not the tool's: on 2026-10-08 「你能根据已经配置的模型补一下它们的上下文大小配置吗」 opened a job at `update_endpoint`; the window was set and the Bot said so, the ticket stayed To do, the supervisor called the Bot back ten minutes later and it said the same thing again, and the second ending with no progress stopped the job with a notice. A line no model read follows the rules below.
- **0 candidates**: only a user request can automatically open a plan with a produce ticket for this Bot; the call then runs. Since 2026-10-06 no “New job: …” card is posted: the tag under your line names the new job. The new job's title leaves out the Bots the line names (「@Alpha 做一个 logo」 opens 「做一个 logo」), unless the names are all there is. A Bot or system line cannot invent a job through this fallback.
- **Read as new** ([ADR 0057](adr/0057-the-job-a-line-is-about-is-read.md)): however many candidates, the situation says the app read the line as new work, about none of them, and the first call with an effect opens a job for it as with 0 candidates, unless the Bot chose one with `work_on` first. A reading never opens a job by itself: answering or chatting leaves none behind.
- **1 candidate**: the situation already names the default job; if unrelated, split it first with `work_on({plan:{new:{title,quote_message_id}}})`. The call runs only after binding.
- **2 or more candidates**: refuse the call and list the choices, saying `{new}` quoting your line is one of them, waiting for `work_on` or your answer; no writing files or running commands before filing.

Choosing does not grant a work slot: with work capacity occupied (two segments by default), a desk can still answer, but selecting a third job only queues durable work, ends the desk segment and executes no requested effect. Work starts once a slot is available; the desk exemption never becomes a third work segment. Continue on interrupted work also obeys capacity: it can queue, ending the admission segment with `end_reason=queued`, while actual work starts later.

`work_on` can name a ticket of that plan (or open one), and `also` adds this Bot's still-open work items in the captured candidates as message targets. The job the segment is already on need not be a candidate: a desk segment whose `plan_items` opened the job and bound it there goes on to its own first ticket with `work_on`. That step used to be refused as no candidate of the segment's while its ending was sent back to go on, so a lead could not start its own part at all (2026-10-04 walkthrough). Going from the whole job down to one of its tickets is the segment's own step, not a reading of your line: a line filed to the whole job stays with the job rather than moving to that ticket (a lead starting on the poster had your request for the poster and three slogans tagged with the poster alone). A Bot cannot drop locked targets or targets you corrected; a default can be split into a quoted new job before the first effect. The attribution refusal budget is two in a desk segment; exhausting it ends as `needs_attention` with the line "This line needs a job selected before work can proceed.", suppressing later effects in that same hop too. Once the segment is bound it no longer spends this budget.

**Working directory (I8)**: the public constraint is at most one change per segment, with effects allowed only after attribution; typically no desk directory → ticket directory. A misfiled default may split a fresh request once before acting, not authorize repeated changes while working. A work segment bound to another directory, having written files or run calls, cannot use `work_on` to switch jobs and keep writing. Another ticket of the same job is the exception: a segment that has done nothing yet (no file written, no command run, its directory unused) can `work_on` another ticket of its job — a lead woken with the slogans approved goes on to its own poster; it used to be refused and wait minutes for the supervisor to open a segment on it (2026-10-03). Going from the whole job down to one of its own tickets is no change of directory either, even after acting: what the segment did, it did in the job, which holds the ticket's folder. On 2026-10-04's real-model run, after a stop, 「宣传语改成英文的，海报改横版」 was filed to the whole job and the lead's segment opened at that level; it made the poster, then could neither `submit` (no ticket) nor `work_on` ticket 01 (it had acted), and gave up with the job undelivered. Another job, or one ticket to another after acting, is still refused. A segment on the whole job that writes into the folder of one of the job's tickets it owns is bound to that ticket by the app (`work.bound`, by write), so its end hands over and owes as that ticket: on the same real-model runs the lead, woken at the job's level by the approved slogans, drew the poster in 03's folder and ended with nothing_new, nothing handed over. A command that ran once you approved it counts too (it used to be only commands that ran at once: on 2026-10-04 the lead rendered its poster with a python3 command you approved, the segment stayed on the whole job, submit asked it to bind first, and it gave up). And when a segment on the whole job hands in with `submit` files that all sit in the folder of one of its own tickets, it is bound to that ticket first, so files a tool (an image-generating MCP, say) or another command wrote, unseen by the app, still go in. Continue after interruption preserves desk mode, captured candidates and the used directory-change count, and still passes attribution and hold guards; it does not turn a desk into an ordinary writable turn. A held Bot's `readonly` answer is not a desk and cannot take on work through `work_on`.

**New-job card actions**: since 2026-10-06 this card is no longer posted ([ADR 0058](adr/0058-the-app-asks-only-when-it-needs-you.md)'s addendum); one already out still acts as below. A level-2 new job used to display one persistent “New job: …” card, recording its opening segment and quoted user message, with Undo and Merge. A new job queued behind full work capacity can have a card without opening a third work segment. Actions remain available while the new job is active or delivered, including a quickly finished job. Once you have accepted it or it is abandoned, the old card returns `409 plan_changed`: another explicit human action is required, not reopening through an old card. A successful card action is applied once; a repeat does not execute it again.

Undo explicitly holds the new plan and stops its live execution before marking it abandoned; it does not erase past execution. Files, old turns' execution records, quote text and requirements remain, and the quoted message stays filed under the abandoned job. Nothing is automatically cleaned up or deleted; lifting the hold does not automatically set abandoned back to active. Started effects such as writes, commands and MCP calls are honestly retained, without a promise to undo past external operations. Later effects are not let through after the stop.

Merge likewise stops and abandons the new job, but refiles **only the user message quoted by the card**: its target on the new plan moves to an offered active/delivered plan of the same project (home conversation). Its other targets stay, and the quote text is not erased. This follows message-correction inbox/source rules; it neither changes old turns' plan or directory, moves artifacts, merges whole plans nor lifts target holds. A quoted message already corrected away from the new job returns `409 attribution_changed`; a target not offered, outside the project or no longer in an eligible stage is rejected.

Edit on an attribution tag (a click on it) only corrects message relationships, without stopping or abandoning the original plan. Merge on a new-job card includes a separate explicit user action to stop/abandon this new job; they are not interchangeable. Cards use the existing local/remote `POST /v1/messages/:id/control`: `{action:"undo_plan"}` or `{action:"merge_plan",task_id}`. Scoped checks cover stopping live work, retained files, quickly delivered jobs, queued cards, correction and stale protection. They do not certify full P4b phase acceptance or later P4e submission/review completion.

<a id="attribution"></a>
## Attribution

`message_filings` holds all targets of a message (plan, optional ticket and optional part) and their provenance; legacy `messages.task_id`/`ticket_id` project only the first primary target, not the whole relationship. `filed` has targets, `undetermined` has not been decided, and `none` is an explicit unfiling or an app control line outside attribution. A line of yours that only **might** be a stop (marked `possible_control` with buttons: read as a stop beside other words, such as 「先停，把第三镜换成夜景」 "stop, and make shot 3 a night scene", or as both a stop and a go on) is filed like any line: its quote, paths and reading all count, and the segment at work on that job hears it. A line read as a go on (「继续」 alone included) is filed like any line too: which stops it lifts depends on the job it is filed under (see [Hold](#hold)). One line can concern several jobs or several parts of one ticket; multiple targets do not relax the Bot's plan-level execution uniqueness.

What the line itself points at comes first (the locked signals), in this order; distinct targets accumulate, deduplicated and retained:

| Order | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An explicit choice on the interface/card | Locked |
| 2 | Sent annotations, inherited from the artifact path and delivering message/turn | Locked |
| 3 | A quoted reply, inheriting all targets of the referenced message | Locked |
| 4 | An attachment or prose path under a known plan/ticket directory | Locked |
| 5 | An existing work-item/wake binding; a Bot's output uses its own current work item | Locked; level-3 delegation bindings are wired, not future job/submission tools |

These are references (chosen, annotated, quoted, a path written out, bound work), not guesses at what the line means. With none, which job the line is about is read by a model ([ADR 0057](adr/0057-the-job-a-line-is-about-is-read.md); see "Where a line belongs" under [Line reading](#line-reading)): read as about some of the jobs, it is filed there (a default, correctable, with a ticket and parts when the reading names them, `filed_by = reader`); read as about none of them (new work, chat, another question), as no telling which, or not read at all (no model, the call failed), it stays unplaced and the Bot it wakes chooses at its [desk](#desk-segment). There is no "the one candidate left", "the group's one job" or "the job of the Bot's line just before" default any more, and nothing reads how the line opens or what numbers it carries — the three times new requests were glued onto old jobs on 2026-10-02 and 10-03 were all such defaults (「制作《一拳超人》动画」 went under 「让审片员回复视频导演…」).

The jobs a reading is shown: non-dormant active/delivered plans of the current conversation and of a Bot↔Bot thread's origin conversation, plans of the called Bot's (with none named, the conversation's Bots') open work items and its segments in the last 24 hours. A plan of another conversation comes in only through those two: being a member of that group does not count, so a line in your direct is never filed under a group plan the Bot never touched. The jobs of the six lines before it are in too while not accepted or abandoned — a routine's standing plan or a dormant job included: said three minutes after the morning brief, 「标题跟 LOGO 没有对齐」 is read with today's brief among the jobs and the brief said three minutes ago (on 2026-10-03 rule 6 filed it under a three-day-old address job). A desk segment captures the same candidates at admission, so what the reading could choose the Bot can. Explicit references or annotations can add an accepted or dormant plan.

Parts are only those the lead declares with `plan_items` (or you add on the board). A line's parts come from the reading (only the ones it asks to change, faults or asks about, not one it praises) or its references (an annotated file that is a part's current file); file names make or pick no part, and no shot number in the words is matched by pattern. This only records identities and artifact relationships, not approved part stages.

A message's attribution is one compact tag under it: the first job's title (a job is often named after the sentence that opened it, so a long title is cut to the line, in full in the tooltip), then its ticket and part, and "+N more" when there are several; a message with none says "Unfiled · Choose". The whole tag is the way in — there is no separate Change button. A run of consecutive messages in a conversation filed under the same thing shows the tag only on its first message, and every message's right-click menu has "Change attribution…", so one whose tag a run folded away can still be changed. The tag or the menu item opens a dialog rather than unfolding in the stream: the first two lines of the message under the title, so a dialog reached from a menu still says which line is being filed; a search box (Enter takes the first match, Arrow Down goes to the list); what is chosen on top (a job with tickets then asks which ticket, and once a ticket is chosen the part field appears only when you press "Set a part"); then the jobs this conversation has already used — the latest first, each saying "Last used …" and its ticket count; the rest of the workspace's jobs folded behind "Show N other jobs", all of them searchable the moment you type. Save and Cancel stay in view; on a phone the dialog is a page and Cancel is the back arrow; Save is off until something changed, and "File under no job" clears the attribution explicitly; under a line of yours there is also "A new job": when the line is about none of the jobs listed, choose it (choosing it lets go of the rest), and Save opens a job from the line — named after it, without the Bots it names, with a ticket of the same name, made by the Bot of a direct or laid out by a group's lead — and files the line there, as your own correction does; the job it was on is left as it is. A Bot's line has no such row. A ticket named as its job is not said twice on the tag ("X › X"). A job that has not loaded, or is gone, reads as "A job" on the tag, never as its id; a line filed under a job this conversation's list does not have yet (the first line of a new conversation, a job just opened) loads the list again from that line, once, and the tag names it without a reload. `GET /v1/messages/:id/attribution` lists plans/tickets for manual correction (including untouched or dormant plans, without waking one merely by reading; a routine's standing plan is listed too, its latest run's ticket first, so the brief's tag reads 「每日AI重点新闻简报 › 2026-10-03」 and you can file a line under today's run by hand). `PATCH` accepts `{filings:[{plan_id,ticket_id?,part_key?},…]}`; an empty array explicitly unfiles, and the old single-target `{plan_id,ticket_id?,part_key?}` remains supported. Both are local and on the remote whitelist. The API creates the user-action identity; strength cannot be forged, and a wrong plan/ticket/part rejects the whole correction, leaving no partial write.

Your correction may change locked attribution and records `attribution.changed`. Unread queued/held mail moves to the new work item and remains held if the target's holds cover it. Already-read mail and its execution segment remain unchanged; Bots that actually read it get separate “This line was refiled to …” correction mail, queued even with no live target. Multiple targets get distinct mail. Explicit unfiling supersedes unread mail and does not roll back work. A correction neither lifts holds, moves files nor changes an old turn's working directory. A segment still running that read the line, on a job the line is no longer filed under (or at its desk, on none yet), reads an app note at its next step: where the line was refiled, that it is done there in a segment of its own, and not to act on it here — it used to hear nothing and finish the line in the wrong job (2026-10-03). `PATCH` also takes `{new_plan:{title?}}`, which is "A new job": only a line of yours, nothing beside it, local and remote.

**Persistent quotes are plural too**: `user_quote_filings` relates one verbatim quote to every target, so a secondary job can still read it after the transcript is cleared, rather than keeping only the old scalar primary target. Requirement sources derived from that line move with a correction; a current ledger entry still has a single scope and is retargeted through the primary target. This is not a claim that one requirement already fans out into independent review obligations for every job. Changing a line you sent does not refile it (use Change attribution for that), and the quote of the words you changed follows the line's filing; the "You changed this line" a Bot that read it receives is written into its inbox in the same write as the change, as a refile's correction is, without a wake (see [User quote](#user-quote)).

<a id="group-lead"></a>
## Group lead

At level 2 an unaddressed user line in a group goes first to that job's present lead, then to the group lead you confirmed. With neither, the group retains legacy participation judgements (the existing per-Bot calls, not a claim that one call now decides the whole group; each runs on the Bot's own model, or the one chosen for Judgement under Settings → Roles). Mentions/`@everyone` still decide recipients, and an existing segment on the same plan hears the line rather than being redirected. A held lead does not cause another Bot to be picked to continue quietly. When such a line, reaching the lead (or the Bot already at work there) only by default, is filed under a ticket of another Bot that can still take work, the woken Bot starts on the whole job: it can hand that ticket out or go on to its own, but it does not work under someone else's ticket; a Bot you name, or talk to in your direct with it, takes the line's ticket. On 2026-10-04's real-model run 「宣传语改成英文的，海报改横版」 after a stop was read as about the copywriter's slogans ticket; the lead's turn landed on that ticket, and the poster it drew was handed in as the slogans, pushing the copywriter's English lines aside.

The last **7 days** of handoffs only suggest a lead: the count includes real turn-sent group messages mentioning another member (everyone included), and the first real line in a Bot↔Bot direct opened from that group. Ordinary chatter, user mentions, empty directs and subsequent discussion do not add handoffs. Only present, non-archived, non-deleted Bots qualify; no evidence or a tie at the top gives no suggestion. Only your confirmation writes `session_participants.is_lead`; no Bot has an appointment tool. You can choose another present Bot or clear it with `null`.

`GET /v1/sessions/:id/lead` and `PUT /v1/sessions/:id/lead {bot_id,confirmed:true}` are local and on the remote whitelist; in the group's settings, each present Bot's row in the member list is the entry: one click on "Make lead" confirms it (to change, click another Bot), the current lead carries a "Lead" badge, and "Remove lead" on its row clears it; an archived Bot cannot be made lead, but can still be cleared if it is the current one. A suggestion appears only as a hint on that member's row ("Suggested: N handoffs in the last 7 days"): it takes no room in the conversation, needs no dismissing, and changes nothing until you click. Confirmation records `group_lead.confirmed`; a new top count never replaces your lead automatically.

<a id="mention"></a>
## Mention

`@Bot` names a Bot (one in the roster but outside the group still joins through existing Pull-in before receiving); `@everyone` names only Bots already in the group except the author and pulls in nobody outside it. A short name with one present match names that Bot. An unknown name wakes nobody: a Bot's `send_message` is first refused once for correction, then a still-unknown name leaves a system notice. An `@` in a path, URL or Markdown link target is no mention; one glued to an ASCII letter, digit, `_`, `-` or a path separator is none, while `@A/@B` names both and one after Chinese text or punctuation still counts.

A Bot's line in a group that names another Bot and, the names aside, is only a bare acknowledgement (「@Beta 收到，已对齐」) wakes nobody when its turn was itself woken by such a line from the other Bot and neither turn ran a command or MCP tool (at every level; the same nod-to-a-nod rule as in Bot↔Bot directs), so two Bots cannot keep naming each other to agree forever. A question, a file, a path or a name nobody here has makes it more than a nod.

At level 2 a mention chooses recipients, not the end of an old turn: an existing same-plan segment hears it, while another plan opens or queues its own. Your group mentions do the same; Stop/holds interrupt immediately. Level-0/1 compatibility can still redirect a user's group line, carrying old written files, recent calls and unread mail, while Bot mentions and check-backs try to enter an existing segment. Old wake/ending semantics of ordinary Bot↔Bot directs and `send_message` remain at levels 0–2; level 3 uses explicit delegate and non-waking ordinary private messages, under [Delegation](behavior.en.md#delegation).

<a id="turn"></a>
## Turn

A level-2 segment belongs to a work item; live states include running/waiting_approval/waiting_ask. One Bot × plan has one segment, not one Bot across the entire group forever. Different plans use the work slots (two by default), desks count separately (see [Work item](behavior.en.md#work-item)); an unrelated new request goes to a desk, not the old job. Same-plan mail is read between tool calls: a user change defers remaining unexecuted calls in that hop, a question does not, and a long command completes its current call unless a hold stops it.

A prose answer or `end_turn` can end the current segment. Existing inbox dispositions still record adopted, answered, declined (with why), deferred or unacknowledged. A disposition for an id that is no mail of this segment's (none at all — often the line that woke it — or mail another segment read) records nothing and sends nothing back: only a wrong word on mail it did read, or a line of yours left without one, bounces the ending (before 2026-10-03 a segment with no mail at all was bounced twice for naming the line that woke it, ended needing attention, and was picked up again). Ending is not an approved submission. Level-3 reason/obligation checks have a domain function and engine integration; see [Delegation](behavior.en.md#delegation) for implemented parts and gaps. Ball-holder supervision is still absent. Completion failure differs from interruption; existing 40-hop stuck reminders and tool withdrawal at 160 hops remain, not a new supervisor. The transcript interleaves in time and spend is per call.

At old levels a user's group mention/join judgement can redirect: end the old segment and start from the new message, carrying files/calls/unread mail without reversing past effects. Peer lines and check-backs enter an existing segment. Legacy direct forks and one-Bot-one-turn across a group are compatibility, not promises in addition to level-2 plan uniqueness.

**What a turn ran stays under its reply**: each shell command is recorded as it finishes (`turn_runs`): the command (whitespace folded, clipped to 300 characters), its exit code, whether it succeeded, how long it ran, and the last 4000 characters it printed (stdout, then stderr; nothing when it printed nothing), the same for Bots on your endpoints and Claude Agent Bots; MCP calls are recorded too but are not commands. A turn keeps its first 80 calls. Once the turn has ended, its last reply carries the same command card (while it runs, a line it sent on the way carries none: its commands are in its working message), on the reply's last line with its tag, time and reply time, wrapping only when they do not fit, read from that record (`GET /v1/turns/:id/commands`, when the reply comes near the screen); at the moment it ends the card shows what this page saw, until the record is read. Clearing or deleting the conversation deletes these records with it. Earlier builds kept no time or output, so turns from then have the command and exit code only.

**Lines in a row** ([ADR 0063](adr/0063-edit-a-line-and-lines-in-order.md)): your lines in one conversation are taken in the order they came. A line waits until the one before it is read, filed and routed before it is filed and routed itself — the turn that one opened is then there, and this line goes into its inbox, read at its next step — and its filing read sees where the line before it went. The wait counts from when the line ahead started and lasts at most 45 s; shutting down, nobody waits. What a line means is read without waiting: the reading starts as it arrives (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)), and a line read as nothing but a stop is carried out at once, ahead of the lines still being read; a go on or a status question waits its turn like any line and goes to the Bot. A Bot's lines do not queue. So a direct's Send no longer greys out while your last line is read (an older daemon still locks it), the hint says "Enter to send — it reads it at its next step", and a Bot already at work is no longer drawn replying under each line you send meanwhile.

<a id="plan"></a>
## Plan

**Name**: a plan is named after the first words of the line that opened it (at most 40 code points; a line with no words, only a picture or a file, names it after its first file, as in "screenshot.png and 1 more", and one with nothing at all "(attachments only)" — never blank). The app, the organizer and the Bots never rename it; only you do, in the plan's 要点 (Plan) on the board, and its folder keeps its name. Renaming changes the name, not the goal the Bots work to: once a new name is saved, a line under it says what the goal reads now, with an "Edit the goal" button, until you edit it or leave — on 2026-10-04 you renamed a job 「做《一拳超人》动画」 and its goal still read 「制作一部未来世界题材、时长超过2分钟…的短片」, further down the panel, where 视频导演 read it as the job. The turn's picture says so too: when you renamed the job after its goal was last written, the plan's line is followed by "the user renamed this job after its goal was written: where the goal disagrees with the name or with their own words, their words win", until you write the goal on the board. The board's heading, the job switcher, the stop menu, the attribution tags and every turn's picture all call it by that name (the goal is in the spec, and stands in only when the name is empty). A rename is an edit of yours: "Last change" says so, a dormant plan wakes, and `map.md` carries it. Before 2026-10-03 a name could not be changed, and 《一拳超人》 stayed under the line that opened it, 「让审片员回复视频导演，说明未回复原因并给出审片意见。」.

**Current level-2 path**: a job can span conversations, and a conversation can hold multiple non-dormant plans; the “current slot” must not guess a message's job. Code retains the historical `tasks`/`task_id` names. [Attribution](behavior.en.md#attribution) places a request in an existing plan; uncertainty opens a [desk](behavior.en.md#desk-segment), and a quoted request or the first permitted effect opens a new job. Opening it does not park another job still being worked on. This change adds no new “all tickets approved → plan delivered/accepted” submission state machine: old active/done/parked and ticket statuses coexist, and importing done as delivered does not mean an approved submission exists.

Dormancy is separate from holds: it neither changes stage nor lifts a hold, removes default candidacy and suppresses automatic call-backs. Clearing the home conversation sets prior plans dormant. Opening a new plan (a desk opening a job, or `work_on` opening one from a quoted line) sets an older one aside only after two hours with no user activity or recent segment, no non-idle open work item and no registered pending job. When a database goes up from level 0/1 to level 2 or above, old plans parked by another job that have no live segment and none of your words in the last two hours go dormant too (work log `plan.dormant`, cause `legacy_parked`); otherwise they would all read as active candidates, and a desk with two or more candidates refuses to act. A delivered plan also ages into dormancy after 72 hours without user activity. Legacy done plans with no user quote in the last 24 hours import as delivered + dormant. The pending-job condition only checks a table that exists with the columns it reads (an abandoned draft's table of that name, without them, is set aside at open: dropped when empty, renamed `external_jobs_draft_<time>` otherwise), not a claim that the P4d poller is active. Explicit references, your correction or a continue can reactivate one, still subject to holds.

**The following describes level-0/1 migration compatibility and existing plan presentation, not level-2 message attribution; its single current slot, organizing before a turn and parking on a new job do not apply at level 2:**

The one thing currently being moved forward in a session, pieced together by the app itself; you do not have to define it first. The code keeps the `tasks` table and `task_id` (historical names; comments call it a plan). It is written up as **the plan**, with these fields: Kind, Goal (what exactly is to be done now), Done when (how completion is judged), Rules (the standards, constraints, and improvement notes you have set), Process (the process and who does what), Progress (done / to do / stuck), and Status (in progress / done / parked); it also keeps the verbatim text of the request that opened it (the original request). The organizer updates the plan after each of your messages and after the plan goes quiet (that second pass only files the handover; see [Organizer](#organizer)), except Done when and Rules, which only you write, on the flow board: the organizer no longer writes those two, and what your lines ask is kept in the [requirements ledger](#requirements-ledger). Every revision leaves a record (which message or turn caused it, or that you made it), and you can also edit it directly on the flow board. A session has only one current plan at a time: when the organizer judges that you have moved on to a different thing, it opens a new plan and parks the old one; when it judges that you have returned to an earlier thing, it picks that plan back up; when it judges the plan done or parked, the slot is left empty. No clock switches things for you. When you stop the work, the plan is parked, and it goes back to in progress only when you say to continue (or change it on the flow board). Once the engine level has holds, the hold keeps it there (see Hold): a line that says it has not stopped ("you haven't stopped") is read as a stop again and carried out as one, never reaching the organizer (one that also asks for something else is filed as usual; one the reader cannot read goes to the Bot), and the organizer no longer parks a plan on its own reading of a line. Below that level the organizer does it: a line pushing for it to stop is never read as continue, and the filing after the plan goes quiet does not set it back to in progress. Nor does a turn a check-back or annotations wake working in it: opening a turn never changes a plan's status. Every turn's situation carries the plan, what you said in the job (see [User quote](#user-quote)), the requirements ledger's entries in force for it (see [Requirements ledger](#requirements-ledger)), the ticket list and the ticket for this turn, and so does a turn handed off to another Bot or another session, woken by a check-back or fired by a routine, so the original goal and what you asked do not depend on whether that line is still in the transcript window. Looking up precedents of the same Kind is gone: neither the situation nor the organizer reads them. The head of the flow board says how long ago the job last changed and what changed ("Last change 3 min ago (ticket 02 "Rough cut": review)", the newest of the plan's latest version, a ticket's status, a check's run, a turn and a change to its requirements), and a dormant plan is marked "Dormant". The first turn is marked in the situation; it is the only turn that can still align on the request before starting work. When a session's history is cleared or the session is deleted, its plans no longer close; they go dormant: the closing time is still written, so an older build that knows nothing of dormancy still reads them as closed. Nothing touches a dormant plan: it is not filed (a filing already out when it went dormant lands nothing) and nobody is called back into it; clearing or deleting drops its pending filing and look-again, so nothing fires into the cleared session. It wakes when it is put back in its session's current slot (the organizer judges that a line of yours returns to it, you lift the hold that parks it, or an older build picks it back up); when a line of yours, or your answer to a question, is filed under it, from whichever session (it then stays closed unless that filing also puts it back in the slot); and when you edit its fields or its tickets on the flow board, any edit (checks alone do not count). A Bot's turn working in it (a check-back, a call-back, a turn named into it) only moves its closing time and leaves it dormant. When a session is deleted, plans nothing refers to are deleted with their tickets as before; the others go dormant and closed and belong to no session from then on, one the group's hold parked gets a hold of its own, and an edit on the flow board wakes one but can never put it back in a session's slot. A routine's standing plan only closes and does not go dormant, and the routine keeps adding tickets to it. On a clear, a plan stays as long as it still has a quote of yours you did not erase, or a requirement on it or on one of its tickets; only with neither, and no other turn or message of the transcript belonging to it, is it deleted with its tickets as before.

**One-question jobs in the switcher, and a board that has ended (2026-10-10)**: in the switcher under the board's title, a job that was one question and its answer (closed, no tickets, one turn at most) folds into a single row, "N one-question jobs"; press it and it opens in place, and the arrow keys walk through it. The job on screen never folds. An older daemon does not report each job's turns, and then nothing folds. With no job asked for, the board opens on the newest job that was not a one-question one (the newest of all when every one was). When the job is done, parked or dormant, the ticket board wears a matching label beside its title and, while tickets are still open, a line under it: "Settled: no one will pick up the tickets left open" ("Dormant:" and "Parked:" for those); the cards still open fade back and lose their accent. Dragging and the status menu still work, and nothing says the job goes on because of it — as with the supervisor: in a done, parked or dormant plan the ball is nobody's (see [Ball holder](#ball-holder)).

<a id="ticket"></a>
## Ticket

**Level-2 work identity**: a ticket narrows a work item within its plan. `work_on` can explicitly select one or open a produce ticket for a new job; the app validates that it belongs to the selected plan. Neither the waking turn's ticket nor “the Bot has exactly one unclosed ticket” decides the execution directory. A Bot's output uses its own work-item attribution. Different tickets of one job still obey I1b. Existing To do/In progress/In review/Done/Parked can still be used by the current interface, observed progress and settle. In review is not P4e submitted/approved, and materializing a part from a filename is not a passed part review.

**The following fields, artifacts and observed progress remain; organizer-created tickets and worker-based inheritance describe only legacy compatibility:**

The smallest unit with a deliverable under a plan: number, title, description and Done when (what to hand over and how it is judged done; no progress notes and no don'ts a Bot set for itself), status (To do / In progress / In review / Done / Parked), who is on it (filled in first from "Process" when the organizer opens the ticket, then updated from what is observed; it is a record, not an assignment), and artifacts. Both the organizer and you edit tickets. The organizer refers to existing tickets by id and writes only the fields that changed (leaving out the title, status, or who is on it means that field is unchanged); it opens new tickets as new-N; it cannot delete a ticket, only mark it Done or Parked. You change status on the flow board. The app itself also moves a ticket forward one step based on what it sees, without calling a model: when a turn filed under the ticket starts writing files or running commands, To do becomes In progress; when that turn hands over files, it becomes In review; if no one is on it, that Bot is recorded. It only moves forward, and it never marks anything Done or Parked on anyone's behalf. The organizer waits until the plan goes quiet before organizing, so this step keeps the flow board showing truthfully who is doing what even while several Bots work back to back. Each ticket has its own folder `NN-<title>/` under the plan folder; turns filed under the ticket work in it by default, and turns not filed under any ticket land in the plan folder. When a Bot is woken by a handoff, if the ticket handed over belongs to someone else while the Bot is on exactly one ticket in this plan that is not yet closed out, the turn lands on the Bot's own ticket instead of following the waking turn into someone else's ticket; if the handed-over ticket is its own, or no one is on it yet, the turn follows the handoff. Artifacts belong to tickets: a message carries a `ticket_id`, and the files it references are that ticket's artifacts; no files are moved. The ticket list sits beside the flow board, and clicking an entry lights up its cards. Each ticket is a title line whose status label opens to change the status; under it, one line says who is on it, who has the ball and what it waits for, then the description and links to its files and latest turn; the reviewer, model and dependency settings unfold only under the picked ticket. Tickets and the plan are linked: every ticket has to meet the plan’s Done when lines, rules and what you asked for (a ticket folder’s `ticket.md` is the ticket’s description followed by the plan’s Done when and rules), and some requests and checks hold for one ticket alone. The picked ticket lists what it has to meet, and “See in the plan” opens the plan read against it: the ticket is named at the top, and the requests and checks held to other tickets dim. In the plan, a request or check held to one ticket names it, and pressing the name switches to the ticket list with that ticket picked; above Progress, a row counts the tickets by status, and pressing a status opens the list on it — the tickets’ own states are the ones to go by, and the Progress below is the organizer’s written account.

**Stages from level 5** ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-submissions-and-reviews.md)): a ticket also has a `stage`: to do, in progress, submitted, in review, rework, approved, dropped, with its old status written alongside (submitted and in review write In review, rework writes In progress, approved writes Done, dropped writes Parked). Only a [submission](#submission)'s checks, a [review](#review), the supervisor, and your board edits of the status write it (the trigger `tickets_stage_follows_status` brings the stage along with yours), each move recorded as `ticket.stage_changed`. From level 5 the organizer writes no status for existing tickets, opens new ones as to do, and marks a plan done only when it has no ticket to approve; its "done" on a ticket nothing was ever handed over on becomes that ticket's submission (`organizer`, no files): with a reviewer set it goes to that reviewer like any other hand-over; with none, the next tick never ignores it or approves it on its word alone either — once everything else is clear, a card asks you to approve it or send it back (see [Supervisor](#supervisor)). When the producer still has a segment running on the ticket, or a work item that is not idle, this reading is skipped for the tick so it does not race ahead of real work still in progress; the organizer tries again next time it reads the ticket as done. The old "handed files over → In review" step is gone ("started working → In progress" stays). Changing a ticket's status on the board supersedes its undecided submissions, so one still in review cannot move it past you. The board's status label shows the stage (In review, Rework, Submitted, Approved), with how many parts passed beside it ("11/12 approved") and a Reviewer menu under the picked ticket (an unpicked one with a reviewer says "Reviewed by …" in the line under its title) — all only at level 5. A Bot's situation names the stage in its ticket list and "This turn's ticket" too. **Parts** are those the lead declares with `plan_items` (or you add on the board), and a hand-over names the ones it covers in `submit`'s `parts`; one that names none, or that the app hands over for the Bot (implicit), is the whole ticket's — file names make and pick no part ([ADR 0057](adr/0057-the-job-a-line-is-about-is-read.md)). A Bot's situation lists its ticket's parts and their stages. Each submission counts an attempt and records its current file, and a part's stage follows submissions, checks and reviews: in progress → submitted → approved or rework. A ticket's **reviewer** is the one you pick in the picked ticket's Reviewer menu (which lists only Bots of the plan's conversation, never the owner; or `PATCH /v1/tickets/:id {reviewer_bot_id}`, local and remote), never the ticket's owner; else the recipient of an open request expecting a review (`delegate` with `expects: review`); never the producer. When every ticket of a plan not dropped is approved and its plan-wide gates (checks on no ticket) last passed, the plan is delivered by the path a done plan always takes (its spec says done, it closes as a done plan does, under a stop it still reads parked), with `stage = delivered`. A delivered plan one of whose tickets leaves approved — handed in again, or sent back — is active again (`plan.reopened`): delivered means every ticket approved, and the supervisor chases only active plans; it used to stay delivered, so the new hand-over a lead asked for after your complaint was never chased (2026-10-03). It is delivered again once that ticket is through. On the board an approved ticket says who made it ("By X") and no longer "waits for #NN"; a parked or dropped ticket says nobody's. A segment that ends with its ticket approved or dropped closes its work item; otherwise the item goes idle, since a rejection can bring it back. An approval usually happens in another Bot's reviewing segment, when the producer's item is already idle, and there it stays. Changes after delivery: a segment that wrote in the folder of an approved ticket and handed nothing over with `submit` has its ending sent back once, telling it what you approved is the old version and to hand the change over with `submit` for you to decide (an automatic hand-over never reopens an approved ticket); the paths it wrote are all it wrote in the segment, not only those no line has cited yet. And once a job is delivered a settle no longer calls it in progress again: from level 5 hand-overs and approvals decide when it is done. On 2026-10-04's real-model run, after 「第二句不够有画面感，换一句」 the lead redrew the poster and 文案 rewrote the slogans, both tickets still read approved with the old versions, the job still read done and no card came; and once it was handed over and approved, a settle 19 s later marked it in progress again.

**Dropped and set aside, and the totals (2026-10-10)**: a ticket its lead dropped (it has a reason) reads "Dropped" on the board, with a muted "Dropped: <reason>" line on its card, in the Parked column all the same; a ticket you set aside yourself on the board has no reason and reads "Parked" as before. Neither is work still to do: in the ticket list's "N open · M in all · K dropped/set aside" the M leaves them out (the K part is left off when it is 0), and the completion percentage is a share of M alone; with no ticket counting at all there is no percentage or progress bar. The job's header and the switcher rows count the same way. In a large job, or one that already has a sample, a picked ticket that is not through and not the sample has "Make it the sample", which sends `PATCH /v1/tickets/:id {sample: true}` (see [ADR 0053](adr/0053-plan-items.md)).

<a id="plan-items"></a>
## The lead lays out tickets (plan_items)

From level 5 ([ADR 0053](adr/0053-plan-items.md)) the plan's lead can lay the plan out as tickets with `plan_items`: each with a title, who makes it, who reviews it (optional, never the maker), which tickets it waits for, and its parts ("Shot 07", "C07" and the like keyed by shot number as one key, `shot_07`, which a hand-over names in `submit`'s `parts`). The lead is the plan's stored one, the group lead you confirmed, or the Bot of a direct — never whoever ran most; a group lead you confirmed counts in any group you spoke about the job in, not just its home (the home's first, then where you spoke last), so a job opened in a direct and taken up in a group is laid out by that group's lead, and owners and reviewers may be any Bot of those conversations. A title the plan already has is that ticket, so rework opens no second one, but it is only filled in: an owner or reviewer already set stays, dependencies and parts are only added, and a ticket that is done, parked or approved is not touched; if any item is invalid the call changes nothing. Other Bots cannot use it and are told to delegate to the lead. It wakes nobody — work is still handed out by delegation — and you can change any of it on the board. The ticket of the job's own name made when it opened (a desk's first effect opening it, or you making a job of a line) is dropped when the layout leaves it out and nothing was done on it (no hand-over, no file, no command, no open request), and the calling segment goes on the whole job: left standing it was a third ticket nobody handed in, which the supervisor chased and which kept the job from being delivered (2026-10-03). What was filed under it goes up to the whole job with it: your line's filing and quote, and a requirement read from that line and held for that ticket — left there they held for a ticket nobody works on, and the line sat under the dropped ticket in its 归到哪件事 (2026-10-04). The attribution dialog's ticket list leaves dropped tickets out, unless the line is filed under one now.

**Laying the job out again when its direction changes (2026-10-10)**: when you turn down the job's direction (2D to 3D, say), the lead lays it out again with the same `plan_items`. `drop` lists the tickets the old direction left, each with a one-line reason: they are dropped (the board reads "Dropped" with the reason, and they no longer count toward the totals or the percentage), reminders still pending on them are voided, open delegations cancelled, queued work no longer dispatched; another Bot at work on one is told without being woken, and from then on generating, handing over, `work_on` and delegating on it are refused; an external job (a video render, say) still running on it reports to the whole job when it finishes, instead of waking the Bot back onto that ticket. A ticket approved, or with a hand-over waiting on review or your card, cannot be dropped. The item of the new direction marked `sample: true` becomes the sample (`resample_reason` may say why): the old one stops being it, the checks held to it come down (their runs kept), and the other tickets wait for the new one; you approve the new sample as any, and the standard checks against it are made once you do. "Make it the sample" on a board card does the same. The ticket the job opened with (named after the job) is folded into the job by such a layout even when work was done on it (its files stay where they are), unless a hand-over of it waits on review, a delegation is open on it, or another Bot is at work on it — the result then says why it was left. After the IG MV job went from 2D to 3D, 7 of its 14 tickets belonged to the abandoned 2D line and every 3D ticket was held to the 2D sample, which "passed" four versions you sent back.

<a id="large-job"></a>
## Large jobs: laid out first, a sample first

From level 5 ([ADR 0060](adr/0060-large-jobs-sample-first.md)). A job that is only done well in several parts with one shown to you first — a video of several minutes, a whole episode, a book or report of many chapters, a many-page site, a batch of dozens of like things — is a **large job**. Two ways find one: when a line of yours is filed under a job not read yet, the reader reads it with all your words about the job (what is asked for, not wording; unsure is no; unread stays unread); and when a job not read, not laid out and with nothing approved has ended 3 or more segments, the supervisor has it read again with those facts, and marks it large itself when nothing can read. A reading only ever marks an unread job, never over yours. Under the plan panel's Name a large job reads "A large job: laid out in parts, with a sample made first for you to approve before the rest" with what showed it, and "No need" beside it; once you say no need nothing reads it as large again, and "Treat as large" marks one yourself.

Until a large job is laid out, a segment on it is refused side-effect MCP calls (generating images or video) and `submit`, and files left at its ending are not handed over; reading, notes, scripts and local commands go on, and its situation says to lay it out first. The lead does: one `plan_items` item marked `sample: true` is the **sample** — made first, to the whole job's standard, for you to approve. A job has one; a large job's layout stands only with a sample and something waiting for it. Once a sample is set, every ticket but the sample and what it depends on (however far back), not through, waits for it, and so do tickets laid out later.

**Dependencies hold from level 5.** A ticket whose dependency is not through does not start: the supervisor wakes nobody for it (the board reads "Waits for you to approve sample #02" or "Waits for #01 to be handed over"), `work_on` refuses it, a deliverable delegation of it is refused, and generating calls and hand-overs on it are refused. Through means approved for the sample, and handed over at least once (submitted, in review, approved, or back in rework after a hand-over) for any other ticket, so the next one does not wait on your card. A dropped or parked ticket holds nobody. A ticket nobody has started, all it waits for through, has its owner called at the next supervisor tick rather than after the usual quiet window: the next part starts as the one before is handed in.

**The sample and the last one are yours to approve.** The sample's hand-over always comes to your approve/reject card, with a reviewer (who reviews first) or checks of yours behind it all the same. The card says it sets the standard for the rest and what it took — segments, minutes, model spend, generating calls (their cost is the service's) — with a rough sum for the remaining tickets. The last unapproved ticket of a laid-out large job (approving it delivers the job) comes to you too. The ones in between go as usual: approved without you when checks or a review back them.

**Held to the sample.** Once you approve the sample, each ticket waiting for it gets a check, "Holds to the standard of sample #02 …". At each hand-over the app gathers the evidence itself and has the picture judge's model (the one chosen under Settings → Roles, the default model if none) compare: for video the longest file's length, size, sound, how many different pictures its keyframes show and how often one comes back, plus 5 evenly spaced frames; the first pictures; the opening, middle and end of a text. Content differs, as it should; only craft and form count — still pictures under camera moves where the sample moved fall short — and unsure passes. It is a gate: falling short sends the hand-over back with what falls short; keeping the standard backs an approval with no reviewer like a check of yours. A judgement it could not make (nothing handed over, no model, your stop, the day's picture budget spent) holds nothing, and the hand-over comes to your card. A comparison that sends pictures needs a chosen endpoint model to be marked "takes pictures" in its attributes, and otherwise runs that once on the default model; a chosen Claude model is always used; one that compares only words is not held to this. Picture judgements are a reference elsewhere ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-submissions-and-reviews.md)); this one gates, since the bar is the sample you approved. It runs only when a hand-over is made or decided, never on a settle's rerun of every check, and once its ticket is approved or dropped it holds the plan open no more. The plan lists these under Done when as "Held to the sample": the sample's line, each ticket's check under it, failures first and passes last; a failed one is also named under the goal, in "Needs a look", with its ticket and its first reason.

<a id="submission"></a>
## Submission

From level 5 ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-submissions-and-reviews.md)), one hand-over of a ticket's work, kept in `submissions`: which files (by content SHA-256), parts, the Bot's note and claims, the checks the app ran, and the reviews it got. An explicit submission takes files of the plan's folder (its `deliverables/` and root too) but not of another ticket's folder, plus the workspace's shared `assets/`; an implicit one only the ticket's own folder; never a folder the app keeps for itself; a newer submission of a ticket supersedes the undecided ones, and one that failed its checks stays on record.

- **Explicit**: `submit({artifacts, parts?, claims?, note?, continue?})`, paths relative to the workspace or to the Bot's work dir. The app hashes the files, posts the ones this segment has not cited yet on a line of the Bot's (so a check from your words can find the master it measures), then runs the submission's checks at once and waits: a failing gate comes back to the Bot with its details and the ticket does not move (back to rework if it had been handed over); passing, the ticket is submitted and queued for its reviewer if it has one. The segment ends with it unless a line of yours it read is not yet answered for, a line of yours in your direct opened the segment and no word of it has reached you, a request it sent is still open, or it is waiting on something — then it says why (no bounce counted). With no word to you yet, it replies after handing over; files that line already carries are not attached to the reply again. `continue: true` goes on with other parts. Files byte-for-byte the same (paths and hashes) as the ticket's last approved or still-open hand-over are refused (`unchanged`): there is nothing to decide. Writing into a job, a path in a folder shaped like a ticket's (`work/<job>/NN-…`) that no ticket has and that is not there yet is refused by `write_file`, with the job's real ticket folders. On 2026-10-04's real-model run, asked after delivery to change the second day's theme, a Bot built a folder name from the ticket's title (`01-…每天一个主题`; the real one is the title cut short, `01-…每天一个`), wrote the new version there and handed in the untouched one, and the card asked you to approve a plan that had not changed.
- **Implicit**: when a Bot does not call `submit`, files this segment's lines cited, in the ticket's folder, whose content hash is new since the ticket's last submission are submitted for it (`origin = implicit`) with the same checks — for the ticket's **producer** only: its owner (or worker), a Bot a request asks for a deliverable on it (`expects: deliverable`), or anyone on a ticket nobody owns. What the ticket's reviewer, a Bot asked to review it, or a segment woken by a request to review writes are review notes, never submitted. `end_turn(done)` posts the files the segment wrote and has not cited first, then submits (a failing check comes back as the reply to that `end_turn`); a pure-text closing that hands over files of the ticket's folder is posted, then submits — a failing check voids that closing and the Bot hears why and goes on; passing, it is weighed by the ending contract — and any other segment that ends normally (not blocked, gave up, or needing attention) submits when it ends, a failing check queuing a line that wakes the producer. The same bytes are never submitted twice; an approved or dropped ticket gets no implicit submission. A Bot that is not the owner and leaves files in an owned ticket's folder hears once at its ending (`end_turn(done)` or a closing reply) that they were not handed over for it, and to use `submit` if they are the ticket's work.
- **Words handed over**: on a ticket no file was ever handed over on (topics, a conclusion — work that is words), only its producer may hand those words over, and only through an explicit `end_turn(done, answer)` — a plain-text closing reply is never inferred as a hand-over any more; that inference once let "the master is cut" approve a ticket nobody had checked. The text itself must still read as the deliverable: at least 2 code points after trimming (not a real floor — this kind of hand-over always ends on your approve/reject card regardless, so length is not the safety line), not an empty remark, not a promise of more to come, not a question, not a bare ack (好的 / 收到 / 明白 / 了解, "got it" / "sure" / "noted"), and not a bare claim of being done, mid-progress, unable to or waiting (done / finished / completed / working on / waiting) with nothing else behind it, however it is dressed up — padded out long enough it may slip past this filter, which is not a bypass: it still lands on your card for a send-back. Failing that, the call is refused and the Bot is told to hand over the words themselves — a title, a line, a list, a paragraph — as the answer; if the work is a file, to keep working until that file exists (no longer suggesting "write it to a file and submit that": that suggestion was once used to route around this very check — see "no reviewer" under Submission below). Passing, the words are handed over (`origin = answer`, kept as `content`); checks and review follow as for files, the same words are never handed over twice, and a Bot that is not the ticket's producer hears once that only the owner hands over words.
- **Checks**: a submission is held to the plan's checks on its ticket, those reading one of its files or anything in the ticket's folder, commands run there, and [checks from your words](#check-from-your-words) bound to its files (synced first, so a new master gets bound). Only runs started after the submission count. Gates are checks you or the organizer wrote and checks from your words you confirmed; unconfirmed ones are measured and shown, never holding back a submission nor an approval.
- **No reviewer**: a checked submission with no reviewer waits as submitted for the [supervisor](#supervisor)'s next tick, which reads the checks as they are then (see there); a gate not finished yet is waited for.
- **A routine's runs**: what a ticket of a routine's standing plan (the one each due time opens) hands over gets no approve/reject card and no required-items card: it is approved once its gates pass (`submission.approved`, `by: routine`), or on a reviewer's approve when one is set; a failing gate bound to the run still sends it back (a plan-wide check binds no ticket a later run opens, so none holds a routine's run back for now); when a result is wrong, saying so still sends it back to rework when the reader reads the fault. A routine runs unattended by design, and on 2026-10-03 and 10-04 the morning brief waited for your Approve both days, never once sent back (from 2026-10-04; a card left waiting from before is approved and put away at the next tick, against the checks as they are then).
- Details at `GET /v1/tasks/:id/submissions` (local and remote); the board shows stages, parts passed and the reviewer. Levels 4 and below have no submissions: handing files over moves a ticket to In review, as before.

## Review

**The approval card**: a card asking you to approve or send back a hand-over carries its files, to open and look at before 放行 / 退回. On a card the app posts, the first five files are each a small chip whose icon follows the extension (video, image, JSON, Markdown…; a picture shows its thumbnail), and after them always comes the same entry a Bot's message ends with, reading "N files", which opens every file of the card in the preview beside the chat. A chip opens its file over the whole app, the way a picture is enlarged: that one file, with no file tree, and annotations work as usual; Escape, a click outside, the close button or the phone's Back puts it away, asking first about unsaved changes (2026-10-06; the card used to list every file with one page icon, and a chip opened the preview beside the chat). The card says in plain words what came in and why it is yours to decide — the reviewer runs on the same model as the Bot that made it (a model passing its own kind of work does not count), the reviewer gave no grounds on what you asked, or there is no reviewer and no check you confirmed. It used to name the files only as paths in its words, with nothing to open, and give its reason as "a hand-over no check you confirmed backs" and "(fixture, the same model as the producer)" (2026-10-03). 退回 first opens a box under the card asking what should change (optional), and only "Send it back" in it sends it back: anything written there goes word for word to the Bot that made it (its rework turn reads "要改的地方，原话：「…」"), and the card then says "已退回：「…」". It also counts as your own words on the job, as an answer on a question card does: read into the requirement ledger, measured for numbers to check, and listed among your words in every later turn of the Bot (it used to reach the Bot once, as its rework note: on 2026-10-04 「一集时长20分钟，不是这么短的视频，而且要有剧情走向」 sent back with a 《一拳超人》 hand-over never reached the ledger, and the rework came back as a 27-second cut); `note` on `POST /v1/messages/:id/control` goes only with that press and is refused on any other. Before, 退回 was a bare button and the Bot was told only that you had sent it back, left to guess what was wrong (2026-10-04). On the phone neither button could be pressed until then: the phone's allowed buttons were written out in the relay route and had not kept up with the cards added since (from 2026-10-04 they come from the same list in the protocol). The situation's ticket list leaves dropped tickets out (but this turn's own), says who made an approved one, and nobody on one set aside — the opening ticket folded by the lead's layout read "parked; 设计师 on it" to the Bots, the approved slogans "approved; 文案 on it" (2026-10-04). Up to 6 files on the card are listed one by one (pictures with thumbnails) rather than folded into one folder as on other messages; where files do fold into a folder, it is named after the folder they are actually in (a ticket's folder, say), not the "work" every job's files share (on 2026-10-04's real-model run the card read "work · 3 files", and the poster was a folder away).

**A card that waited**: while its Bot is still at work the card is held back and goes up when it stops; when that is more than 10 minutes after the hand-over and the Bot has sent other files in the job since, the card's first line says when this version was handed over and how many files came after it (and the latest one's name), which this approval does not cover. On the IG MV job the card for the 2D quarter-final cut handed over at 00:39 waited three hours and came up right under the Bot's new 3D sample shot; 放行 was pressed on the old cut.

**Your approval is logged as yours**: an approval you press on a card is recorded as yours (`submission.approved` with `by: user`, the ticket's stage change with `source: user`); before 2026-10-10 every one was recorded as the app approving on its own (`by: no_reviewer`), so for history the press recorded on the card is what tells.

**Where cards go**: the cards about one Bot's work — approve or send back, items waiting on you, the capability ceiling, a reflection's proposal — go to where you last spoke about the job: a group, or your direct with that Bot, never your direct with another of the job's Bots; past one you have left or archived, to the one before; with none, to the job's conversation. A line that sends work back to rework posts no card: what it did shows under that line. They all used to go to the job's conversation: on 2026-10-04 a job opened in a group and taken up that morning in your direct with the video director asked you to sign off in the direct while its card landed in the group. When you send work back, send it to rework or answer a ceiling card in your direct with the Bot that made it, it redoes the work in that direct (queued work otherwise runs where it began; a delegation in its own conversation): sent back from your direct with the copywriter, the slogans used to be redone in the group the job was opened in, and the new version landed there (2026-10-04, real-model run).

From level 5, another Bot judges a submission with `review({submission_id?, verdicts, outcome, note?})`; "approved" in words moves nothing. With a reviewer set on the ticket only it may review; otherwise only a Bot in the plan's conversation; the producer never reviews its own. A review needs a ticket that is submitted or in review. Without `submission_id` it takes the newest submission waiting on its ticket, its plan, a ticket it reviews, or a plan of the conversation it runs in. When a submission passes its checks and the ticket has a reviewer, a line asking for the review is queued in the reviewer's work item (`source = review`, priority 2) and dispatched at once.

- **Required items** are the requirements in force on the job that you said twice or more, or whose category is about the picture (picture, shot, character, colour and the like). They need a pass with evidence to approve — unknown, n/a, no verdict or no evidence will not do; the rest may be n/a.
- **Before an approval the app runs the checks again**, and refuses the approval, changing nothing and returning each reason to the reviewer (`review.refused`), when a gate fails, a required item has no pass or no evidence, or something about the picture passed without this segment having read, after the submission, an image in this job's folder with `read_file` (one `artifact.viewed` per path and segment, outside the 80-row command record, and never counted as having run a command). An unconfirmed check from your words is not among them: it never blocks an approval.
- **Waiting on you**: when the reviewer runs on the producer's own model and passes something you said twice or more that no passing check you confirmed, standing on its words, backs and you have not confirmed, the approval waits on you (`review.awaiting_user`) — an unconfirmed proposal that passes does not count, since a misread number can pass a wrong cut: it is refused with what the app measured, and the hand-over's approve/reject card (placed as under Where cards go above, with a notification) carries the reviewer's verdict and names the items and the measurements beside the ledger's own words so you can compare them: **Approve** counts them as met and the reviewer's approve with them; **Send back** sends it to rework. One card per hand-over (from 2026-10-07; before, a card asked about the items alone — Confirm the check, Count it as met, No longer require it — and was often followed by an approval card to press as well). The ball is yours; meanwhile the producer is told nothing and never sent to rework to a number you have not confirmed, and the submission takes no other review. Making the item not hold here on the board instead, or confirming the check on the flow board when it then passes, lets the supervisor's next tick approve it when the card waited on these items alone (a check you confirmed backs the rest); a confirmed check that fails sends it back to its producer at the next tick. A same-model review is marked `same_model` whatever its outcome.
- **A rejection** always records: the ticket and its parts go to rework and the producer's work item wakes with the review's note. **An approval**: the ticket and parts are approved, the producer reads it when it next wakes (it is not woken for it), and the plan may be delivered — unless this submission is words or an organizer's reading (nobody actually made either), or files no check you wrote or confirmed backs while the reviewer runs on the producer's own model (or either model is unknown) or gave no evidence on a requirement that applies: then a reviewer's approve does not approve it; it moves the submission to awaiting you, the card carrying that verdict (see [Supervisor](#supervisor)).
- In F-f: you said "about 2 minutes" twice; 审片员, on the same model as the director, passes it on the 107-second master and approves. The app runs ffprobe again and gets 107.00 s — the proposal is not confirmed, so it blocks nothing — but a same-model pass has no passing check behind it, so the approval waits on you: the refusal and the card both say "107.00 秒，要时长 108–132 秒", and the director hears nothing. You send it back with your reason, or confirm the check on the flow board: it is a gate, measures a failure, and at the next tick the master goes back to the director, now against a number you confirmed, the card saying what failed.

- **Your complaint sends it back at once** (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)): a line of yours filed (by the rows or by you, not by a Bot's pick) under a ticket — or some of its parts — that is submitted, in review or approved (or filed only under the plan, when the plan's submitted, in-review or approved work is a single ticket whose maker is still here, neither archived nor deleted; with several, which one is meant is a guess, and nothing moves), in which [line reading](#line-reading) (a model) quotes a clause objecting to the work (a fault, dissatisfaction, a redo or a start over asked for, what came before scrapped; not praise, a redo turned down such as 别重做 or 不用从头再做, a question, a condition, another time or version, or something settled), sends that ticket back to rework there and then: the parts the line is filed under (else the whole ticket), never shot numbers picked from the clauses; a hand-over still waiting on you is superseded and its card taken down, every review that approved the current hand-over counts one miss (`review.miss`), a delivered plan is active again, and the Bot that made it is woken with your words — a Stop you pressed on its work in that job goes, as at 退回 on a card, even when the line was said to another Bot. Under your line it says 「已转回返工：……」 ("Sent back to rework: …") with Undo for a misreading — while no newer hand-over has come and the ticket is still in rework, Undo puts everything back and the misses no longer count, and the Bot's call to rework is dropped if unread, or it is told the rework was taken back; when one line sent several tickets back, Undo puts back only the last. A line the reader cannot read (the word lists read it) sends nothing back; an annotation, a part-level entry from the scribe, or a mere reply to the line that carried the files no longer counts as a complaint by itself. The line still wakes whom it wakes (the lead, in a group with one), and that turn's situation says the line reads as a complaint about the handed-over ticket, which has gone back to rework with its maker told in these words, and not to hand the fix to anyone else. Before, the lead was not told and could hand the fix to someone else as well, the work twice (2026-10-04). It used to post a card first — "You said "…" — send it back to rework?" [Send back to rework] [Leave it] — the app asking about what you had just said; a card left from then can still be pressed. Approval stays a press on the card: no text ever approves.
- **Calibration record**: a reviewer's situation lists its approvals you overturned in that conversation (newest five, with your words and when), to judge the same kind of thing harder.
- **Capability ceiling**: when a hand-over fails (its checks, a review's reject, or your send-back on a card), each part it covers (else the whole ticket, counting only hand-overs of no part) that, since you last answered a ceiling card about it, has failed the same requirement three hand-overs in a row, or failed more than six hand-overs, is stuck: the part reads Blocked, nothing more is handed over for it (`submit` gets `ceiling_reached`), a hand-over of no part is refused while a part is stuck, the ball is yours, the supervisor wakes nobody for it, and the producer only hears that you are being asked; a card asks — Another way, Change the plan, Relax this requirement (only when stuck on one; pressing it is your confirmation, and the requirement no longer holds for the plan), Take it as it is. The first three send it back to rework, tell the producer what you chose and restart the count; Take it as it is approves the part, and the ticket once all its parts are approved or waived. When the ticket closes another way (approved, your board edit), a card still asking says why and loses its buttons.
- **Pictures are a reference**: when a seams check hands pictures to a model (video frames, images), that result is a reference only — shown in a hand-over's checks as judged by a model looking at pictures, it neither blocks nor backs an approval, and a plan-wide one does not hold the plan's delivery back. No pictures are judged while a stop covers the plan, nor once today's picture judging has cost $5; that run reads as not judged. A Bot's situation labels it as for reference only too. A text-only seams check is unchanged.

<a id="external-job"></a>
## External job

From level 6 ([ADR 0047](adr/0047-external-jobs.md)), a media server's paired `submit_<x>` / `check_<x>` (westlake-cpa's `submit_video` / `check_video`, for one) is waited on by the app:

- **Submitting**: a submit that gets an id back is registered, and the Bot reads that the app polls it and wakes it when done. An answer in JSON is read as JSON; one written as `key: value` lines is read too (grok-imagine answers `request_id: …` so, a note in brackets at the end of a line not counting) — before, no such job was ever registered, and the Bot checked on it again and again inside its own turn. The same server, tool and arguments within half an hour get the job already started (`deduped`) without reaching the server, whatever the filing. When the prompt names exactly one part number and that part has a job running, or a finished result not handed over yet, a new submit is refused (`job_pending`); to submit again anyway, `resubmit_reason` says why (kept on the job, not sent to the server).
- **Checking**: a Bot's check on a registered job reads what the app last heard (`cached`) without reaching the server, and the Bot becomes a waiter.
- **Polling**: on each 15-second scheduler tick the daemon asks about the jobs due, outside any turn, every 30 s, 1, 2, 4, then 5 minutes. Done or failed, the Bot that submitted it and every Bot that checked on it are woken in their own conversations with the result (url, duration, cost; an answer written as lines is passed on whole, grok-imagine's "url is TEMPORARY — fetch promptly" included); one still pending after three hours is given up as lost and they are told so. While a job runs, the supervisor treats the ball as the job poller's and calls nobody back to poll it; a job a Bot submitted on the job as a whole, on no ticket yet, counts on the tickets that Bot holds.
- **Check-backs**: from level 6 a Bot's own check-back is at least 5 minutes, at most six an hour per job, and refused when its note names a job being polled.

## Quality event, Lesson

From level 8 ([ADR 0050](adr/0050-quality-events-and-lessons.md)), no model call reviews a chain afterwards and writes its verdict into a memory; the one exception is the retrospective after a job you accepted is delivered ([ADR 0062](adr/0062-retrospective-after-delivery.md)), in which the Bots that made it check their own conduct, judging no model:

- **Quality events**: the work-log events that say something went wrong, filed by their type — checks failed, a review's reject, your send-back, a complaint's rework, a command timing out are `execution` (a check defined, or a requirement first said, after the hand-over is `unclear`); a reply that loops, declines, hits its cap or comes back incomplete is `model`; a failed render or a part at the capability ceiling is `pipeline`; an approval you overturned is `review_miss`; a stall, a crash, a stop broken is `orchestration`. Each carries the Bot and model it is about. Clearing a history keeps them and only drops where they came from.
- **Mark as the model's problem**: a board card's model choice can mark that turn as the model's problem (press again to take it back); it feeds the model report only. The card also says why the turn ran on its model.
- **The report**: `GET /v1/quality/report` gives per Bot × model × plan kind the hand-overs, approvals, rejects, send-backs, checks failed, complaints, failure shapes, review misses and the spend per approval.
- **Lessons**: a search that walks a tree (`grep -r`, `rg`, `find`…) killed by the timeout makes that kind of call (program, place; no pattern, nothing quoted) a lesson of this workspace for every Bot; a render or a build running long is not one. The first call of that kind in a turn is then not run, and the Bot is told what happened and how to narrow it; repeating it in the same turn runs it; timing out again after that turns the lesson into a block, and that kind of call no longer runs. A folder one level down is another kind of call. Settings' Lessons page shows them and switches each between warn and block, retires it or brings it back.
- **Reflection** ([ADR 0051](adr/0051-narrowed-reflection.md)): only after an overturned approval or a capability ceiling, once a day per Bot and ticket, at most $2 and twenty a day; under a stop it waits for the lift. The Bot involved (the reviewer or the producer), on its own model (or the one chosen for Reflection under Settings → Roles, at `high` thinking), reads the ticket's work log, requirements and review verdicts, and may propose one checklist item (before reviewing, handing over or generating) or one check on a file (exists, contains, matches; never a command), or nothing. The proposal is a card (placed as approval cards are, see [Review](#review)), Adopt / No, and a check proposal spells out what it checks: an adopted checklist item is read in that Bot's situation; an adopted check is added to the ticket, sends work back when it fails, but is not yours — it backs no approval nobody reviewed until you edit it on the board.
- **Project skills** ([ADR 0052](adr/0052-project-skills.md)): on the Project skills card in a Bot's Skills tab you can share one of its skills with every Bot (confirmed once more). What is shared is a copy as it was then: every Bot's catalog shows it, every Bot can read it, none can change it; when the owner edits its skill, the card says so, and "Update the shared copy" shows the body as it is now before you confirm. At most 20 are on at once; you can turn one off or stop sharing it; a deleted owner's copy stays, marked as from a deleted Bot.
- **The quality report**: a "Quality, last 7 days" table at the bottom of the spend page, per Bot × model × plan kind: hand-overs, approvals, turn-backs, complaints, failed replies, review misses and the spend per approval.
- **Retrospective** ([ADR 0062](adr/0062-retrospective-after-delivery.md)): once a job is delivered (every ticket approved) and has stayed quiet for 30 minutes — a complaint right after an approval reopens it, and the retrospective waits for the next delivery — each Bot that handed something over in it looks back once (the three that handed over most), for deliveries within 7 days, never a routine's, waiting out a stop. When nothing went wrong for that Bot in the job (no hand-over sent back, rejected or failed by its checks, no complaint's rework, no requirement first said after a hand-over, no render lost, no search timed out, no ticket handed over more than once) it is recorded as skipped with no model call; delivered again after a reopen, it looks only at what went wrong since the last one. On its own model (a Bot run as a Claude Agent goes through the endpoint; one chosen for Retrospective under Settings → Roles is used instead, at `high` thinking) it reads the job's record: your words, every hand-over and what became of it (what you said sending it back, review verdicts, checks that failed, its own hand-over note), complaints after an approval, the requirements ledger (how often you said each, whether only after a hand-over), its quality events and tool calls that kept failing, all its memories and skills, project skills and checklist items, and what its earlier retrospectives wrote and which of those you undid; never the transcript. It answers with where it tripped up, which of its ways made you send work back and what to do again (at most five each), a verdict on the earlier conclusions this job tested (still holds, happened again, no longer holds), and a few changes: memories written, rewritten or merged (at most 3), memories deleted (at most 3), one of its skills changed or one made (at most 2, at most 1 made). The app checks each before writing it, and one refused takes no other with it: a memory follows the tool's rules (40-character subject, 160-character body, one deleted or merged away first when 20 are enabled); one you turned off, a project skill and another Bot's are not touched; a skill only gains a sentence after a passage, a passage at its end, or one wrong sentence corrected, and must keep every name in backticks, file name, link and environment variable it had — a passage rewritten whole is not written at all. An answer cut off, or not one whole JSON object, writes nothing. It posts no message, shows no card and sends no notification: the board's side panel lists it under Retrospective — who looked back, the summary, the three lists, the changes (opened to show before and now; for a skill only the lines that changed; one not made says why) — with an Undo for each change, which works only while the change is still as the retrospective wrote it; changed since, it is new. A memory a retrospective wrote is marked "Written by the retrospective of …" in the Bot's profile; rewritten by the Bot in a turn or its text changed by you, the mark goes. At most $2 and 12 a day, billed as its own "Retrospective" line (under organizing).
- Chains are no longer reviewed, and your replies no longer count as feedback on the model choice.

## User quote

**P4b addition**: at level 2 quotes follow all message targets, not just the primary (see [Attribution](behavior.en.md#attribution)). `user_quote_filings` relates one verbatim body to several jobs, retaining secondary targets after transcript clearing. “Filed by the turn or organizer” below describes legacy paths; level 2 uses deterministic signals and desk choices. Numeric replacements in the ledger still obey ADR 0042: proposed, changed only by user confirmation, not the draft design's automatic numeric replacement.

What you said, kept as it came, apart from the transcript. Kept are: each line you send (except in the file drop, which has no Bot); each answer you give a Bot's question (the options you picked and what you wrote yourself); for a batch of annotations, the line you added when sending it (none if it only names the Bots) and each annotation itself; and what you write on the flow board yourself — the clauses of the goal or a ticket description you changed (what you only kept is not, since it may be the organizer's), each Done when line and rule you add (dragging a ticket's status is not writing). Each is stored in the same write that makes it: a line that did not go out is not kept either. One holds at most 2000 code points; a longer one keeps its head and its tail, with how much was left out marked between. It is filed under the plan and ticket it belongs to: your line is filed after it lands (by the turn it opens, or by the organizer), and the quote follows that filing, and any later refiling, an older build's included; an annotation is filed under the plan and ticket of the delivery it is about; an answer under the job that asked; what you write on the board under that plan (or ticket), in no session.

Clearing a session's history deletes the transcript and the turns, and keeps every quote, only nulling the id of the message it points at; deleting a group nulls which session it was said in as well. Both can be asked to erase them too (the box "Also erase what you said here" in the clear-history or delete-group confirm, off unless you tick it; `erase_quotes` on the clear or delete request, taken on the local and the remote API): the quotes said in that session have their text emptied and are marked erased, the rows stay, and a requirement standing on one loses its words and no longer applies; what you wrote on the board is in no session and is not touched. What happens to the session's plans: see [Plan](#plan).

The first time a database is opened after updating to this version, the lines, answers and sent annotations still in the transcript are each kept once, filed as the transcript files them; what was cleared before is gone. From then on each is kept as it is written, never caught up again; lines an older build sharing the data folder writes meanwhile are not added later.

Every turn that works in a job — in a group, a direct, a Bot↔Bot direct, woken by a check-back, fired by a routine, all alike — has a section "What the user said in this job" in its situation: of your quotes filed under the job and not erased, the first 4 and the latest 6, verbatim (up to 400 code points each, a longer one cut with "…"), each with when (this machine's time, month-day hour:minute) and where you said it (the group, whose direct, answering a question, an annotation, or on the flow board), and how many were left out between; under 10, all of them. It reads the quotes, not the transcript, so a Bot reads the same after the session is cleared, in a Bot↔Bot direct where no line of yours appears, and forty lines past the transcript window. The job's first turn does not list them: its trigger is the request. A turn a hold covers reads them as they stood when the stop was made: what you said after it, and the requirements noted from that, reach its situation only after the lift, so a turn that may not act is not handed a fresh instruction first.

**Changing a line you sent** ([ADR 0063](adr/0063-edit-a-line-and-lines-in-order.md)): a plain line of yours can be changed after it went out — Edit on the bubble (its hover bar, or the right-click or long-press menu), or ↑ in an empty composer for your newest line. The bubble turns into the editor: Enter saves, Shift+Enter starts a new line, Esc cancels, and the Enter that confirms an input method's candidate is no save. A Bot's line, your answer to a question (a question is answered once), a batch of annotations, and a stop the app carried out itself cannot be changed (one you undid, handed on to the Bot as a line, can again); nor can a line in an archived conversation, a Bot↔Bot direct, or a direct whose Bot is gone. There is no time limit. A changed line says Edited beside its time, which opens what it said before; that history goes with the transcript when the conversation is cleared. Your words gain and never change: the clauses you changed (read as a board edit is, so what you left alone does not count) are kept as one more quote, pointing at the line's first quote and filed where the line is; taking words out keeps nothing new, and the old words are still there. The scribe reads every quote of the line, oldest first and each once, so it reads only what changed; checks from your words count a line's quotes as one source, so a number you left alone is not said twice. Ledger entries on the old quote stay: a changed number makes a replacement awaiting your confirmation, a gate in force whose number changed gets a replacement card, and an entry on words you deleted stays in force. From 2026-10-10 the scribe reads every change again, one that only took words out included: it is shown the line before and after, and the entries that stand on the line's earlier words, that you did not say again in another line and that are still in the ledger, and names those your change took back; they are listed first in the plan's "What you asked for" as "Words you took out", each with "No longer holds" ("Not a requirement" for one waiting to be confirmed) and "Keep it". Until you press, they stay in force. Who hears of it: a Bot that has not read the line yet gets the new words in its inbox and reads only those; a Bot that has read it (an item read, or a turn the line opened) gets a line of yours, "You changed this line. It now reads: “…” (it was: “…”)" — at its next step while it works, by a new turn on its work in the job once its turn ended; after you pressed Stop on it, the change is your next line: the Stop lifts and a new turn goes on from the change; under a stop that stays until you lift it (Stop all Bots, the board, the tools menu) it is held and delivered when the stop lifts — and must dispose of it in end_turn. A read-only answer under a stop has not read it: lifting the stop hands the line back as it then reads. A changed line is not carried out as a stop or a go on, not judged again as a complaint, not refiled, and wakes nobody it now names; a line nobody took up (every Bot in a group sat it out) wakes nobody when changed. In a Bot's transcript a changed line is marked "（发出后改过）", and in its situation's "What the user said in this job" the change reads "changed a line in X".

**Taking back, or inserting, a line not read yet** ([ADR 0069](adr/0069-take-back-or-insert-a-line-not-read-yet.md)): while no Bot has read a line of yours — it waits in a working Bot's inbox, or a stop holds it — a row under it says where it waits: "Queued · read at its next step" (its Bot is at work), "Queued · read when its turn comes" (it waits for that Bot's next turn or a free slot), "Held by a stop · read once it lifts"; once read, the row goes. Beside it are two buttons. Take back: no copy of the line in any inbox is delivered, the line is kept out of every transcript a Bot reads, your words kept from it are erased (the ledger entries made from them are waived), and work queued for it alone goes idle; the transcript shows "You took this line back · Edit again", its words go back into an empty box, and Edit again adds them after what you typed; a line taken back is not changed or refiled — send it again. A line a Bot has read, or one that opened a turn, cannot be taken back, which the row says; you can still change it. Insert now (for a queued line, and for one a Stop of yours holds — the Stop lifts and the Bot opens a turn on it; not under a stop that stays until you lift it, which the row says): a Bot run by Claude Code is interrupted — as Esc does in Claude Code, a command it runs stops — and the lines waiting for that turn go in at once, in order, telling it the step was stopped for them and to run again what was cut if it still needs it; the cut step's own end is neither a reply nor a failure. A Bot on the app's own loop has the model call in flight cut and the hop's waiting tool calls postponed, a command already running finishes, and the next hop opens by reading the lines. With no step to cut right now (it is wrapping up, or the turn ended), the row says "No step to cut right now; it reads this at its next step". Once a stop lifts, every line it held goes back to the Bot to read. Send back on a hand-over's card after Stop lifts that Bot's Stop on the job too, and it reworks with your note. The endpoints are `POST /v1/messages/:id/withdraw` and `POST /v1/messages/:id/insert`, on the phone too. A line a Claude Code turn read after a batch of calls no longer opens one more turn when the segment ends.

<a id="requirements-ledger"></a>
## Requirements ledger

What you have asked of a job, one entry at a time, kept apart from the plan's fields. Each entry stands on your own words: its quote is a piece of what you said (at most 300 code points), beside a restatement, a category, whether it is a must or a must-not, what it holds for — one ticket, the job, or the session the job lives in (every job in that session from then on) — and how many times you have said it, with the line each time. A number you gave (a running time, a resolution, an aspect ratio, a frame rate, with "about", "at least" or "at most") is read out of the quote by fixed rules and kept on the entry, only when the quote gives exactly one. A moment, a stretch or a place in the picture ("at second 3", "at 12 s", "the first 3 seconds", 「logo 放在 3 秒」 "put the logo at 3 s", 「字幕定在 5 秒」), a range ("3-5 minutes", "a minute or two"), a time to answer or finish in ("reply within 5 minutes", 「限你 3 分钟弄完」 "you have 3 minutes to finish", "give you 10 minutes"), how long something else takes ("wait 5 minutes", "the render takes 20 minutes", "3 minutes ago", 「半小时左右能好吗」 "ready in about half an hour?", "ETA 30 minutes"), a count of words ("2k words") or a number you said no to ("not 4K", "not 3 minutes") gives none; a number right after a word setting the work to it reads whatever follows it (「剪成 90 秒发给我」 "cut it to 90 seconds and send it to me" is 90 seconds), and so do 「压在 90 秒以内」 and 「时长定在 90 秒」. A ratio is read only with a word for the picture's shape in the line (画幅, 比例, 屏, "aspect"), never after a score (「比分 2:1」); a bare 「24 帧」 is a count of frames, and a frame rate is written 「帧率 N」, "N fps" or 「每秒 N 帧」; "90s 港风" ("90s Hong Kong style") is a decade. "Don't go over 3 minutes", "no less than 2 minutes" and 「至少要 2 分钟」 read as at most and at least, and 「能不能改成 90 秒」 ("could it be 90 seconds") or 「要不要 4K」 ("4K or not?") asks rather than says no; with no ratio given, portrait (竖屏) and landscape (横屏) count as an aspect (横幅 and 竖幅 are as often a banner, and do not); a bare 分 or m is minutes only right before a number of seconds ("1分30秒", "2m30s") or after a word for a running time.

An entry is in force, to confirm, replaced, no longer applying, not a requirement, or an old rule (source unverified). It only grows: an entry is added, or raised again. It goes out of force only one way: by your own action. A later line of yours giving the same number a new value (first "about 2 minutes", later "make it 3 minutes") is no exception: the scribe can only make a replacement for you to confirm, pointing back to the old entry, which stays in force meanwhile; any other change a model gives does the same. The database holds to two things: an entry's quote must be a piece of the line it names as its source (compared the same folded way), whoever writes it, and one that is not, or whose line was erased, cannot be written; and an entry becomes replaced only by one standing on words of yours said after the old entry's (kept for the day you confirm a replacement). The one delete is your purge.

For now two things write it: the scribe, from your words (see [Scribe](#scribe)), and the fallback capture. The fallback capture: when the scribe notes nothing for a line (no model set, a failed call, an answer that is no patch, no requirement found, or every item it gave failing its check), and the line was said after the job had delivered something (a Bot's line filed under it carried a file, or a ticket was in review or done when you said it, even if the line's filing or a turn it woke has sent that ticket back since) and has a word for something being wrong (fake, wrong, jumpy, breaks the shot, reversed, too short, redo, no good, broken…), the app keeps the line whole (cut at 300 code points) as an entry to confirm, under the ticket the line was filed under (else the job), never a gate.

Editing a plan's Rules or Done when on the flow board edits the ledger: each line you add becomes an entry of the job in force, standing on the quote the board kept of it (when the same words are already an entry for the job as a whole — its own, its session's or a standing one; one that holds for a single ticket does not count — you have said it again; an entry only proposed, or an old rule, is taken up as yours, and one you had set not to apply to this job applies again); each line you take out lets go of every entry with those words that holds for the job, whichever job first wrote it down: the job's own no longer applies, and an old rule with no source found is marked as no requirement; one that holds for the whole session, or a standing one, is set not to apply to this job only while another job still holds those words (the line is on its board, you said it in that job, that job first wrote it down, or you widened it yourself with "For the whole conversation" or "Make it standing"; a job that set it aside does not count), and the other jobs keep it (that covers the one entry an old rule several jobs of the session shared was taken in as, and a session entry your line only said again); when no other job holds it, the line you took out was its last hold, and it goes the way the job's own would: it no longer applies, or is marked as no requirement, so later jobs of the session neither inherit it nor ask you whether you said it. Each step (added, said again, confirmed, restored, no longer applying, no requirement, not for this job) is in the work log with the version of the plan your edit made. A change you later say to one ("make the robot arm a right arm") is kept as a replacement to confirm like any other: an entry written on the board has no category, so nothing tells that the scribe's change is about something else, and it is not dropped.

Where an entry holds: one the scribe says is about one ticket, on that ticket; one the scribe reads you saying every job should keep to ("from now on", "every film", "this series always"), on the session the job lives in; the rest on the job, even when it sounds like general craft or taste. Until 2026-10-10 a video job (one that delivered a video file or called a video tool) widened requirements about craft, choices of look and a series' constants to the session whatever the scribe read: the IG MV job's 19 such entries ("restore the real match shots", "the opponents", "you can't tell which champion it is") showed up in the plan of "Connect to ACE Studio" in the same direct, and in the situation of the Bots working there. Entries already widened are not moved; "Not for this job" on the board still narrows them per job; for the next job to inherit one, say "from now on", or press "For the whole conversation" on the board. A bare "consistent", "style", "pacing", "font" or "quality" (一致, 统一, 风格, 节奏, 字体, 画质) is no craft ("keep the numbers consistent with last month" is about that one job); it counts only with what it makes one (画风统一 "one art style", 色调一致 "a consistent colour grade"); a choice is the same, and "a cold grade" is that film's (or that series') choice, not craft. Which kind an entry is goes by the nature the scribe marked when it noted it (craft, a choice of look, a series' constant, other; see [line reading](#line-reading)); only an older entry without one is judged by the words above. An entry of the session holds for every new job opened in that session from its first turn, marked "inherited from" the job that set it; when one does not fit a job, "Not for this job" on that job's board takes it off for that job only, the others keep it, and "Restore" brings it back. When you say the very words of an entry you had set not to apply to a job again in that job, the scribe counts it as said again and it applies to the job again, rather than a second entry. A job's own entry can be widened with "For the whole conversation". When one kind of craft requirement (by the scribe's category) has come up in two or more video jobs of the same session (counted by the jobs the lines saying it were filed under, only those of this session that are video work; a job is video work once it delivered a video file or called a video tool), no card offers to make it standing any more (from 2026-10-04): an entry holding for the session is inherited by every new job of that session already, and widening it to other sessions is not something you have to decide. A card from before — "You have asked for "背景连贯" in 2 jobs: "背景要连贯". Make it hold for every video job from now on?" (one entry at a time when their words differed) — still takes its buttons: "Make it standing" widens only the entries that still meet the card's terms when you press it — holding for the session or one of its jobs, in force, craft, of the card's kind; never a ticket's or another session's, a number such as a running time, a choice (EP01 graded cold, EP02 warm), or what stays the same across one series (the left arm is that series' own). A standing entry holds for every video job (a job that has delivered no video does not read it yet).

The first time a database is opened after updating to this version, the Rules and Done when lines every plan already had are taken into the ledger once: what you typed on the flow board stays in force (standing on the board's quote of it, when there is one); a line found in your own words about the job (compared folded the same way) stays in force, standing on those words; the rest — mostly the organizer's — becomes "Old rules (source unverified)", for reference only, never a gate. In a video job the craft ones, the choices and what stays the same across a series go to the session; the rest (these too, in a job that is not video work) stay with their plan; a line several jobs of one session share is taken in once (so is a job's line in the same words as a session entry it inherits). Nothing is taken in after that. No card asks you whether old rules are yours (from 2026-10-04): they are for reference only, and you take one up on the flow board's "Old rules" group. Until then, the first time a line of yours was filed under a job bearing old rules nobody had asked you about, the app posted a card — "This job has N old rules from before the requirements ledger with none of your words found for them: … Did you say these?" — with "All mine" (all of them in force) and "Go through them" (opens the flow board, where each has its own buttons); the one of 2026-10-03 was never pressed. Cards already out still take their buttons. Once none of a card's rules is unverified any more (taken up or turned down on the board, or by a rules edit, whoever made it), a card nobody pressed reads "Gone through one by one on the board" with no buttons, and an "All mine" pressed from a page still showing them does nothing; a card pressed "All mine" keeps saying what was pressed.

Where the Bots read it: every turn's situation has a "User requirements" section listing the entries in force that hold for the job (not those set not to apply to it): nearest first (this turn's ticket, the job, other tickets, the session, standing), then by how many times you said it and how recently; every entry said twice or more is listed, the rest up to 20 lines and 2000 code points, with "and N more" past that. Each line reads "R-31 | "前三镜背景严重跳跃" (restated: 前三镜背景要连贯) | the user said it 7 times (across 3 plans) | holds for: ticket 01 Shot 01–03 | check: Running time 108–132 s (waiting for the user to confirm)": the number, your words, the restatement, how many times and in how many jobs you said it (nothing for once), where it holds (an inherited one says from which job), and the check from your words on the same number (in force or waiting). Replacements and complaints waiting for you are a section of their own, "Waiting for the user to confirm (not in force; do not act on these as requirements)", and old rules with no source found another, "Old rules (source unverified; for reference only)", 10 lines each at most.

On the flow board the plan has a block "What you asked for" listing the same entries: the job's own, those it inherits grouped by the job that set them, those waiting for you, the old rules (source unverified), and those set not to apply to this job. Each shows its number, your words, the restatement, where the words came from (you said, your answer, your annotation, you wrote on the board, old rule, your words kept whole as a complaint; one still in the transcript jumps back to that line), how many times you said it, where it holds, and the buttons it takes right now: "Confirm" and "Not a requirement" on a proposed one or an old rule, "Not for this job" on an inherited one, "Restore" on one set aside, "No longer holds" (not on the lines you wrote in the board's Rules or Done when: taking the line out is how) and "For the whole conversation" on the job's own. Each takes two lines: your words and how many times you said them (twice or more is an amber badge), then the restatement, the source, where it holds and the buttons. The job's own list shows the 10 said most often and "Show N more" for the rest (all of them while a ticket is picked). An entry in force shows its buttons when the pointer is on it, always on a touch screen; entries waiting for you get a line under the goal, in "Needs a look", that scrolls to them. The local API is `POST /v1/requirements/:id/action`, also on the remote whitelist; an entry that has moved on since answers 409.

Clearing a session leaves the ledger alone; deleting a group only makes entries that held for that session no longer name it. Erasing a quote empties the words of an entry standing on it and it no longer applies (see [User quote](#user-quote)).

<a id="scribe"></a>
## Scribe

Once a line of yours is filed under a job (the turns it opens have opened, the judgements it needs are made; for a line filed only later — a desk opening a job for it, `work_on` choosing one, your correction — at that moment), or you answer a Bot's question, the app makes one call in the background to the scribe's model (the one chosen under Settings → Roles, else the default model of the default endpoint; with one chosen, thinking at `low`): tool-free, at most 800 tokens of answer, 60 seconds. It reads your line (with the question, for an answer), which session it was said in, the job's goal and ticket list, and the entries already in force for it (the job's, its tickets', its session's and the standing ones, at most 60, each with its id and category), and proposes a patch to the ledger: what to add, what you are saying again, what you changed. There is no delete and no whole list: what it leaves out stays as it is. It holds no turn back: your line wakes the Bots and opens turns as usual, and it notes beside them, each line once (one it has read is not read again when filed again), one line at a time in order; what is still unnoted when the app quits or updates goes unnoted. It never reads a Bot's words. Questions about how it is going, which the app answers itself, never reach it, and once the engine level has reached holds, neither do lines that only stop or go on; below that level those are filed like any other line and reach it too.

Each item of the patch is checked on its own: its quote has to be found in your line (both compared with spaces, punctuation and symbols taken out and full-width folded), and what is kept is that stretch of your line as you wrote it; a new entry or a replacement to confirm needs at least 4 of those characters (or the whole line, when it is shorter), since a character or two of your line says nothing and the entry would stand on the restatement alone, except words that give one running time, resolution, aspect or frame rate (「90 秒」, "4K", "16:9"; 「片长 2 分钟」 inside a long line too); a raise or a change may name only an entry it was shown that is still in force; a new entry holds for the ticket it names (when it names exactly one; or the ticket your line was filed under, when it names none), for the session when the scribe reads that every job should (see [Requirements ledger](#requirements-ledger)), and otherwise for the job; the same words as an entry in force there that holds at least as far are a raise (so are those of one you set not to apply to this job, which then applies again): a ticket's entry counts only for a new entry of the same ticket, and one for the whole job or session is an entry of its own, or the words would stay held for one ticket and the next job would inherit nothing; a line adds at most 8. Every change is only kept as a replacement to confirm, and the old entry stays in force: one giving the old entry's number a new value ("make it 3 minutes" against "about 2 minutes") whatever its category; the old entry's own number again ("about 2 minutes after all") is a raise; any other when its category is the old entry's; one of another category is kept as a replacement to confirm too, in the category the scribe gave (until 2026-10-10 it was dropped, the new words with it: on the IG MV job "rebuild it with the League map, champion models and real player models" was lost that way, and the "original stylised characters" it replaced stayed in force), unless the same words already went to an old entry of their own category in that answer — then aiming them at another is the scribe's mistake, and not kept; an old entry with no category (written on the board, an old rule taken in) cannot tell, so it is kept. Each item that fails is dropped and logged in the work log with the reason, and the rest of the patch still lands. So whatever the model answers, the entries in force lose none.

With no default model set, a failed call, a cut-off or unreadable answer, no requirement found, or nothing landing, the line only goes through the fallback capture. Every raw answer is kept in the work log as it came. Its spend is billed as the "Organizer" kind with the scribe as its purpose, gets a line of its own, "Scribe", in the spend view (under "Other"), sits in the line's session, and belongs to no Bot. A line you changed after sending it has the clauses you changed kept as one more quote; the scribe reads every quote of the line, oldest first and each once, so it reads only what changed ([ADR 0063](adr/0063-edit-a-line-and-lines-in-order.md)).

<a id="line-reading"></a>
## Line reading

Before the app acts on a line, one tool-less short call reads what it means ([ADR 0055](adr/0055-lines-read-by-a-model.md), `reader.ts`). A stop, a go on and a send-back to rework are carried out only on a reading, and none of them when the line cannot be read (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)); otherwise a reading is only understanding: whether an ending is bounced or a card asked is still decided by the rules each of those always had.

**Your line**: the line, the one it answers and the three before it are read for four things — whether it tells the Bots to stop or go on now (stop, go on, both, neither); whether it is nothing but that; whether it only asks where the work stands; which of its clauses object to delivered work as it stands (quoted). Used for:

- **Control lines**: by the reading alone; the fixed rules no longer read first or carry anything out (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)). Nothing but a stop (「停下」, 「先别搞了，收手吧」 "drop it for now", and "you haven't stopped" too) makes holds over the scopes the rules give (@ names, you, all of you, every Bot, this job), undoable from the receipt; a go on (「继续」 alone, or with more in it like 「继续做第二集，……」) is not carried out by the app: it is filed first, lifts the stops that keep the Bots it is said to from that job, and then goes to the Bots like any line (see [Hold](#hold)); a stop beside other words, or both, only carries the buttons and goes on to the Bots; one read as no control carries none. Asking whether it stopped or can stop ("has it stopped?", 「怎么还在跑」 "why is it still running?", "can it stop?") is a line for the Bot. A line sent on again after you undid its stop is not read for control again.
- **Status questions**: the app no longer answers them (its status line went on 2026-10-09); the line goes to the Bot, which answers. When its filing reads it as new work it counts as done in place, so what the Bot looks up or runs for it opens no job (「怎么样了」 "how's it going" once opened a job of that name that ran 79 turns by itself).
- **Complaints**: whether the work goes straight back to rework goes by the quoted clauses objecting to work handed over, in review or approved (see [Review](#review)); which parts go back is which parts the line is filed under (see "Where a line belongs" below), with no shot numbers picked from the clauses by pattern; the fallback capture when the scribe noted nothing goes by the clauses too. The word lists' reading sends nothing back.

**Where a line belongs** ([ADR 0057](adr/0057-the-job-a-line-is-about-is-read.md)): a line of yours with no reference (your choice, an annotation, a quoted reply, an attachment or a written path, bound work) is read once more, alongside the reading above: the line, where it was said, the six lines before it (who, how long ago, which job each was on) and the jobs it may be about — the conversation's candidates and the jobs of those lines, each with its name, goal, stage, home, last move, tickets (state, owner, declared parts), recent files and what you last said about it. The answer is which of them (with a ticket and parts, by the refs it was shown), new work (something to hand over, or work that takes a while and is followed up), something done in place (a greeting, chat, a question, a look-up, a change to the app's settings, a one-off small action — the reply is the result), or no telling which. A line is read with no job open too, choosing between new work and done in place. It is checked against those refs, a ref it was not shown dropped; read as about some jobs, the line is filed there (a default, correctable), and anything else is the Bot's to choose at its [desk](#desk-segment). A line read as new work is marked, and the desk's first call with an effect opens a job for it; one read as done in place is marked too, and its effects open none. This one has no word lists: a line no model read goes to the desk, never guessed from how it opens or the numbers in it.

**A Bot's line**: the message is read, with what its segment answers (the line that woke it, unless its own, and the mail it read there), first for whether it mostly translates, restates, rewrites, polishes or summarises what it answers (`restates`), then for the sentence in which the Bot itself says the work is still going or more will follow (quoted; a sentence of the translation or restatement is not one, only what the Bot says beside it); whether it claims something was run, tested or verified and passed; whether it only says there is nothing to do or no reply needed; whether it is only an acknowledgement, a claim of being done, a wait or "let me look"; and whether a question to the user only asks their OK to go on with what they asked for (a sign-off on work in progress, whether it may start the next step — not something only they can give, a choice between options that is theirs, or what blocks the work). No word list stands in for that last one: with no model, or no reading, the question goes to you as before. Used for the [closing check](#closing-check)'s promise and claimed run; the [ending contract](#delegation), which reads the segment's last word for `promised_later`; whether a `send_message` or a closing reply is a no-work closer (one that is empty, long, asks, names someone or carries a path or code is ruled out by shape first, unread); whether `end_turn(done, answer)` words are a bare status; whether a blocked ending's `needs_from_user` or an `ask_user` question only asks your OK (see [Work question card](#work-question)).

**Craft entries** take no extra reading: the scribe marks each new entry's nature (craft, a choice of look, a series' constant, other), kept on the ledger, which the conversation-wide default and the standing offer go by; older entries without one still go by the word lists.

**Checks**: the answer must be JSON and the control one of its four values; quoted clauses and sentences must be words of the line (spaces, punctuation and width aside). A clause not found is dropped, and when none is the whole line stands as one; a "still going" not found falls back to the sentence the lists find, else the start of the message. A "still going" sentence (the lists' too) found word for word, at least 20 characters of it (spaces and punctuation aside), in what the Bot answers is that line's own, not a promise of the Bot's: on 2026-10-08 you gave 专业翻译官 an English paragraph in your direct, it put the paragraph back above its translation, and the model read the paragraph's "The community-site search is still running. Once it's back, I'll compare the two…" as the translator's promise; the ending bounced once (it went on idly for three minutes), and a line after it told you to @ it in your direct. A phrase of a few words both use (「结论随后」) is no repeat. A cut-off or unreadable answer is no reading.

**When it cannot read**: with no model, a failed call, more than 20 s (waiting for a slot included), an unreadable answer or a draining daemon, the old fixed word lists read the line, and the work log says why — a stop, go on or complaint the lists find is not carried out, and the line goes to the Bot (for a stop that cannot wait, the composer's Stop button); where a line belongs has no lists, and the line goes to the desk. The lists are not widened to fix a miss any more.

**Speed and cost**: your line wakes no Bot, and no stop is carried out, until it is read, and the Bots it would wake show as thinking meanwhile; what it says and where it belongs are read at once, so it waits on the slower. Readings have their own slots per endpoint instead of queueing behind the Bots' long streams, ask for the lightest thinking level the model lists, and run on the model chosen for Reading under Settings → Roles (the default model otherwise). That can also be a Claude model of Claude Agent (haiku first, the fastest): each reading is then one tool-less, single-turn call to your own Claude Code on the Claude account you pick (when more than one is listed), spending that Claude plan's usage, shown on the spend page as Claude Agent at an estimated price; it takes none of the Bots' Claude Agent slots, and at most two run at once. Measured, haiku reads a line in 3–5 s, about 1–2 s of it starting `claude`, slower than a model like deepseek. With Claude Code missing or that account signed out a reading is logged `claude_unavailable`, an error `claude_failed`, and both are read by the word lists. Only you can pick a Claude model for reading: a Bot can only set an endpoint's model or the default; an account lines are read on must be swapped out before it can be removed from the list. The phone offers them as the computer does, asking this computer's Claude Code over the relay ([ADR 0061](adr/0061-claude-agent-runner.md), 2026-10-10 addendum). Each line is read once: yours cached by message, a Bot's by its text and what it answers (every place in a segment that reads it passes the same, so they share one reading). Every reading is logged as `reader.answer` (what was read — your line, a Bot's, or where a line belongs, `filing` — the model, why it failed, the reading, the raw answer); spend bills as `organize` with purpose `reader`, its own "Line reading" line in the spend view.

**Lines in a row, and changed lines**: Lines you send in a row in one conversation are filed and routed one after another: each waits for the one before it to be read, filed and routed (see [Turn](#turn)); what a line means is read as it arrives, and one read as nothing but a stop goes ahead. A line you changed is not read again: its cached readings are dropped, and whatever reads it later reads the new words.

**Still rules**: who a stop or a go on is for (who "you" is, every Bot, this job) and whether a stop's receipt also offers to drop the job (「算了，停吧」) — neither carries anything out; numbers read from your words (running time, resolution, aspect, frame rate); the one-time import of old rules on a new build; whether a line heard by a working Bot is a question or a change; an endpoint's canned refusal.

<a id="organizer"></a>
## Organizer

**Version boundary**: `engine_level >= 2` does not call `organizeMessage`, and a new segment does not use `resolveTurnTask` inheritance/fallback opening or `ownOpenTicket` to choose its job. Deterministic signals or the Bot's desk choice replace that pre-turn guess; no signal does not silently openTask. Levels 0/1 still use the old paths: their functions, message-mode prompts and run logs are not physically deleted. Settle after 30 quiet seconds still files only the handover; the organizer is not claimed fully retired before P4c/P4e replace its progression and delivery judgement. Quotes, the ledger and user confirmation still protect Rules and Done when (ADR 0042).

**The following details are retained legacy message paths and settle behavior; pre-turn message mode applies only at levels 0/1:**

A short, tool-free call that organizes the plan and tickets on a session's behalf, running on the model chosen for the Organizer under Settings → Roles, else on the default model of the default endpoint (thinking at `high` when one is chosen). It runs at two points. First, after your message enters the session and before any turn opens, it judges whether the message continues the current plan (continue), moves on to a different thing (new), returns to an earlier one (resume), or is about a job the Bots here are pushing forward in another session (join: say you tell a Bot in your direct about the job it is doing in a group; a job opened elsewhere stays there, and this session's current plan is unchanged), updates the plan and tickets, and marks which ticket the message is about; once the message is stamped with a plan and ticket, every turn it opens (mentions, joining in after a judgement, forks) lands in the same place. Second, 30 seconds after all of the plan's live turns have ended and it has gone quiet, it organizes again (a turn you Stop does not lead to this: a Stop asks the work to stop, and the call-back after organizing would have it carry on; a Stop also cancels the organizing an earlier turn of the same plan had lined up but not started, and the plan's own wait before looking at it again, and an organizing already under way calls nobody back when it ends; other plans are not affected): it files what those turns handed over under tickets and updates ticket status, who is on it, and Progress. It reads the current plan's fields and tickets (each ticket with the files that landed in its folder and have been referenced), what has happened since the last revision (who said what, what was handed over, the trace, and the commands those turns actually ran with their exit codes), the plans this session moved forward earlier, the jobs the Bots here are pushing forward elsewhere (the whole plan, its tickets, where it was opened, who is working on it where, and the lines of this session filed under it earlier), and the existing Kind tags; precedents of the same Kind are gone, and it no longer reads them. It also reads every line you said about the job: the earlier ones beyond what happened since the last revision, and your answers to questions (a repeated line counts once; when there are too many it keeps the first few and the latest, within a length cap), and whether the goal and each ticket description are ones you typed on the board yourself (these count as your words too, and cleanup leaves them alone). Every line is marked as said by you, by a Bot, or by the app, and an answer to a question counts as yours. It does not write Done when or rules: the prompt no longer asks for them, and any in its answer are not taken; a plan it opens has neither, and an existing plan keeps its own, what you wrote on the board and what earlier filings left; what your lines ask goes into the requirements ledger through the scribe (see [Scribe](#scribe)). What a Bot decides goes into neither the Goal nor a ticket's description, but one of three ways: a hold it put on itself (pausing, freezing, no retries, doing only part of it) goes under Process with who decided it, and what is held back because of it goes under Progress as stuck, with what would unblock it; something that waits on you (an authorization, a key, a go-ahead to spend) goes under Progress as stuck, with what you need to do; a working constraint (don't overwrite the original file, where the delivery goes) goes under Process. Once you say keep going or don't stop, a hold a Bot put on itself is lifted wherever it was written (Process, Progress, or a ticket's description) and what it held back moves back to to do; a hold that waits on you is lifted only once you give what it waits for, and a working constraint stays. Only on a line of yours, and only when it can see every line you said, does it widen a goal a Bot narrowed back to what you asked for. Under Progress, to do lists only what is not done yet; something handed over and only waiting for you to look at it does not count. A ticket's description says what to hand over, not progress. When the plan goes quiet, organizing only files the handover: what was handed over, where each ticket stands, who is on what, Progress and Process, whether the plan is done, and any checks it proposes; it leaves the Goal, the Kind and each existing ticket's title and description exactly as they were (an empty description may still be filled), and does not park the plan; the app sends such changes back and logs a line for each. This holds whether or not you said anything since the last revision: your lines were filed, and handed to the scribe, when you said them, so the quiet pass has nothing new to go on. If the plan changes while that call is running (a line of yours was filed into it, or you edited it), nothing from that call is applied. A Bot saying "done" or "tests pass" does not count as evidence: marking a ticket In review requires files handed over by that ticket, and marking it Done also requires a record of a run. When several parties each build a part that only works once combined, a separate "Integrate the parts and give the start instructions" ticket is opened. If the organizer judges the plan done while tickets are still To do or In progress, the plan does not count as done: its other changes this time are still accepted, and the plan stays in progress (marking it done yourself on the flow board is not subject to this limit). A failure changes nothing: if no default model is configured, the endpoint refuses, the answer is malformed, or the database rejects the change, the plan stays as it was and turns open as usual. An answer that returns to an earlier plan or joins a job elsewhere, when by the time it is read that plan is no longer one it could pick (say the turn on it elsewhere ended while the call was out), files nothing either, and the message stays as it was: the answer was written for that plan, so landing it on the current plan would write over the current plan's fields, and with no current plan it would open a copy nobody asked for. An answer that names this session's current plan is the exception: that is a continue in other words, and it lands as one. No turn runs during the few seconds of organizing, but the Bots this message will wake (in a direct, that one Bot; in a group, those mentioned, or every present member if no one is mentioned) already show "Thinking" under it, and in the list and the header as well; once organizing finishes, each hands over to its own turn or judgement without a gap. Its spend is a separate category, "Organizer", under decision routing, and it belongs to no Bot.

**What came of hand-overs (2026-10-10)**: the organizer reads each ticket's stage (handed over, in review, rework, approved, dropped) and how its newest hand-over came out — who decided it (you, a reviewer or the app), your words when you sent it back, and whether it waits on your approval — and every approval, send-back, and line of yours that put a ticket back to rework since the last version (with that line). Your send-back outweighs a Bot's word and the checks: a ticket in rework, or whose newest hand-over you sent back, is never written as passed, fine or "matches the sample" in the progress, and what you want changed goes under to do; a passing sample check only says it matches the sample, it is not your approval; one handed over again and waiting on you is not written as passed either. In the trace it reads, a turn that said nothing reads "said nothing this turn" and one that only handed over files reads "handed over N files", no longer as the line that woke it. Before, it saw only the ticket's status, and your send-back on a card lived only in the work record and a Bot-only notice it never read: one job's progress four times called a sample shot you had just sent back "matches the sample". A Bot's card on the trace also shows the turn's last part with words in it: a reply often ends on a part with files only, and the card used to take that one and draw blank. The trace also draws your decisions: your answers to a Bot's questions (with the question), your approvals and send-backs on cards (a send-back with your words; an approval whose checks then failed, with what came of it), and a line of yours that put a ticket back to rework are each a card of yours under the turn it answers, marked Answered, Approved, Sent back or Back to rework, and "only what matters" keeps them. Before, the trace drew only the lines that woke a turn: the IG MV job's 4 answers and 14 approvals and send-backs were nowhere on it.

<a id="work-dir"></a>
## Work dir

**Level-2 attribution**: the directory comes from the current work item's plan/ticket, not the waking turn's ticket or an organizer stamp. An unbound desk may not act; the one-change bound and protected Continue are under [Desk segment](behavior.en.md#desk-segment). Directory names, reserved files and previews below still apply; inheritance, the single current plan and organizing first describe level-0/1 legacy paths only. This change neither makes the work directory a sandbox nor claims every proposed cross-ticket write restriction is enforced.

A plan's folder, under the workspace's `work/`, named after the first 12 code points of the Goal (or of the opening request) plus a short id, with no date and no `@` (it becomes `-`, so citing a file inside does not read as a mention; older folders with a date or an `@` are left as they are); under it, each ticket has its own `NN-<title>/`, with any `@` in the title replaced the same way. A turn's work dir is the folder of the ticket it is filed under, or the plan folder if it is not filed under a ticket; the shell runs in it by default, while file tool paths stay relative to the workspace root. Who inherits it: handoffs, mentions, joining in after a judgement, Bot↔Bot directs, resuming an interrupted run, and check-backs all use the plan and ticket of the waking turn, so one piece of work stays in one place across Bots and sessions; your message is stamped with a plan and ticket by the organizer, and every turn it opens lands there. Folders are not switched by the clock: a session's current plan stays open until the organizer judges that the work has moved on to a different thing, returned to an earlier one, or is done or parked. A routine has its own standing plan and opens a new ticket each time it fires. The folder is created only when it is first actually used, so turns that only talk leave no empty folder behind. Deliverables are not forced into it: if you named a path, they are written there; if you did not, they land in this turn's ticket folder. Both levels have reserved subfolders: `tool-results/` holds large tool results saved by the daemon (cleared automatically once the plan has been closed long enough), and `scratch/` holds purely intermediate files; neither is ever attached as an artifact. `map.md` and `ticket.md` are the app's mirror files and likewise do not count as artifacts. Deleting a work dir is up to you; the app only clears `tool-results/`. In the app, you read the work's trace from top to bottom along who woke whom; each card shows which ticket it worked on, artifacts hang on the turn that handed them over, and clicking one opens it in the preview.

<a id="closing-check"></a>
## Closing check

**P4c checkpoint**: the legacy regex/run-evidence checks below still run. Adding level-3 ending obligations does not remove “later” or pre-delivery checks; retaining claimsVerification and extending coverage to pair directs still need final-cutover verification. This round does not claim identical closing-check coverage in Bot↔Bot directs or treat this reminder as real implicit submission. Current ending facts and gaps are under [Delegation](behavior.en.md#delegation).

A check made before a turn hands its deliverables to you. When it happens is unchanged: the message a Bot is about to send cites workspace files, or says it is still doing something unfinished ("working on it", "results to follow"; a start announced as under way counts too — 正在编写, "I'll put the storyboard together and post it", 我将按照…, "I'm now drafting" — while 正在 in a scene, 埼玉正在超市买菜, does not; whether a message promises more and whether it claims a run is read by [line reading](#line-reading), by the old word lists when it cannot be), you are in the target session, and the turn belongs to a job. Since 2026-09-29 (ADR 0037) no model guesses what is missing; it applies three fixed rules, and if any of them fails the Bot gets an app note back: one, the job has acceptance checks (ADR 0036) that are not passing — file checks are judged again on the spot, so a file fixed in this very turn is not bounced on an old result, a check from your words is measured again with ffprobe (one you have not confirmed, or not bound to a file yet, is left out), and command checks use their latest run, skipping one this turn already ran successfully; at most three are listed, with the reminder to fix the deliverable rather than the check, or to say in the closing why it cannot be done now; this rule applies only to replies that deliver (cite files). Two, it says the rest comes later but has neither booked a check-back here nor handed the rest to someone by name — once the turn ends nobody picks it up, so finish now, book a check-back, or name who takes it. Three, it says something was run, tested or verified while this turn ran no command at all (unless it says it was not verified). Through send_message the call does not run and returns the note; a direct reply gets the note as a line in the loop. A turn is checked once and not stopped a second time; a send-back is not a tool error and the chain review does not count it as a failure; the check itself makes no model call, and reading the message is billed as line reading. A Bot↔Bot direct without you is not checked, nor a reply that cites no files and does not say it is still working. The earlier step that had the default model compare the delivery with the plan's spec line by line is gone: in the golden-path ablation switching it off cost no completion and saved about a quarter of the spend and time, and the machine-checkable part of the done-when now runs as acceptance checks.
<a id="acceptance-check"></a>
## Acceptance check

A plan's Done when is a list of results you could check yourself; acceptance checks hand the ones a machine can decide to the daemon, which runs them itself instead of trusting a Bot's claim or a model's reading of its closing. Four kinds: a file exists (and is not empty), a file contains some text (or, reversed, does not), a file matches a regex (or does not), or a command (an expected exit code, 0 by default, and optionally the expected output, compared without trailing blanks and trailing empty lines). File paths, and commands with no directory given, are relative to the workspace root, as the file tree shows it; paths outside the workspace and commands that reach outside cannot be saved, are judged again when they run, and are recorded as blocked and not run if they reach outside — the app never approves anything on your behalf. A command runs under `/bin/sh` in its own process group, killed whole when it ends or times out (120 seconds by default, 600 at most), with only the terminal's environment whitelist (without `SSH_AUTH_SOCK`, and nothing of the daemon's own). Each run records its outcome (passed, failed, blocked, error), a one-line detail in the app's language and the tail of its output; a check keeps its last 5 runs. When they run: when the plan goes quiet the stale ones run first (never run since defined, or the job has had turns since), and the ones the organizer just added run after it files; a check you create or change runs at once; Run checks on the flow board runs them all at any time. One run per plan at a time, one command check at a time on the whole Mac, at most 15 minutes of checks per quiet stretch. Who writes them: you add, edit and remove them on the flow board; editing one the organizer added makes it yours, and the organizer cannot touch it again; the organizer may add, change and remove its own in its answer (at most 3 new ones a time, 10 per plan), a command only if a turn of this job ran it successfully (exit code 0) in that directory or you wrote it yourself in a line filed under the job, and never one you removed; Bots have no tool to write checks and only see them in the situation block and in `map.md`. A check belongs to one line of the Done when, matched by its text: when you or the organizer reword that line the check follows it, and when the line goes, the check is listed under checks that match no line, still run and still counted. Where the results go: on the flow board each line carries its checks' results, which open to the detail, the last run and the output; the organizer sees every check's last result, cannot mark the plan done while one fails (or has not run since it was defined) and cannot mark its ticket done, and records blocked and errored ones under Progress · Blocked as no evidence either way; closing a plan by hand is not held back. Before a plan call-back posts, the app compares the Bot's last 40 replies in the same session and plan. An identical reply after Markdown removal ends silently. An English reply with at least 12 content words also counts as repeated when only articles or wording such as “run longer / be longer” change and the remaining word sequence is identical. Numbers, negations, question marks, code, link targets, mentions and file paths remain in the comparison; new content or newly delivered files still go out. Direct replies and send_message use the same check. Replies triggered by ordinary user messages and existing history are preserved. When a plan goes quiet with tickets open or checks failing, the app calls a Bot back once, quoting up to 3 failing checks and the tail of their output and asking it to fix the deliverable, not the check; a further call-back needs progress first (a ticket handed over or closed, or a check that existed before the last call-back passing for the first time), otherwise it notifies you once that the job stopped, and posts nothing in the session; between two of your lines there are no more call-backs than tickets plus checks. Every turn's situation block lists the job's checks and their results.

Seams (`continuity`) is the fifth kind, for work that several Bots make in parts and that only counts as one delivery once the parts come together: each Bot reviews its own part, and nobody sees the seams. The parts come, in order, from one of three places: a command that prints them one per line (the most precise); a glob (every match in natural order, C2 before C10; only when every match is a video and no command is given is it taken for a master that keeps being re-cut, and the newest one is used); or one file split up — Markdown and plain text at level-one and level-two headings, HTML at h1 and h2, a video at the cuts scene detection finds (it misses cuts between similar shots: 6 of 11 on a real episode). Each seam gets evidence from both sides: for video, the last frame of one shot and the first of the next; for images, the two images; put side by side in one picture under the plan folder's `checks/<check>/` (a reserved folder, never an artifact), which needs a model that reads images; for text, about 700 characters from the end of one part and the start of the next, which does not. Text also gets one whole-set pass: each part's heading, opening and the numbers-with-units and proper names in it, as one digest, to see whether the same thing is named and counted the same way in every part. The judge is the picture judge's model (chosen under Settings → Roles, else the default model of the default endpoint; for the calls that send pictures, the default model when the chosen endpoint model is not marked as taking pictures, while a chosen Claude model is always used), with the acceptance line, the plan's rules and one general list: agrees with the rules; follows on (a missing transition is named); the same thing stays the same (characters, products and proper names, terms, numbers and units, orientation and direction); the same style (for pictures, the look, rendering, palette and character design; for text, tone, format and numbering); repeats or gaps (not called when the evidence cannot show it). Nearly identical frames or images across a seam are good news: the seam is invisible. It answers in JSON only and is asked again once if the answer does not parse. It passes only when every seam and the whole set hold; otherwise the detail reads "K of N seams have problems: 02-market→03-channels: …" and the output lists every seam (with its image path when there is one), then the whole-set line. These calls are billed as their own spend kind, acceptance checks. Code does not use it: the seams between modules are checked by an integration or start-up command. The organizer may propose one too, but only on parts this job delivered or with a list command the job actually ran successfully.

The app also offers one kind itself, from the numbers you give, which counts once you confirm it: a video's running time, resolution, aspect or frame rate; see [Check from your words](#check-from-your-words).

<a id="check-from-your-words"></a>
## Check from your words

When you give a job a running time, a resolution, an aspect or a frame rate, the app offers an acceptance check from your words that measures the video the job finally delivers. As soon as your line is filed under the job (every turn it opens has opened), or you answer a question or change the goal, Done when, rules or a ticket description on the flow board, the app reads the numbers from the job's quotes by the same fixed rules as the requirements ledger (see [Requirements ledger](#requirements-ledger)): no model call, and no waiting for the scribe. A board edit counts only for the clauses you changed (adding 「，加字幕」 "add subtitles" to 「剪一支约 60 秒的宣传片」 counts only 「加字幕」); text the organizer wrote that you only kept is not your words. It reads narrowly: a misread costs an extra offer, a miss one offer fewer. A running time needs a word for a bound ("about", "around", "at least", "at most", "within", 约, 左右, 以上, 以内), a word setting the work to it right before it (剪成, 做成, 改成, 控制在, "make it", "cut it to"), or the word for a running time earlier in its clause (片长, 时长, "duration"); a resolution, an aspect ratio, portrait or landscape counts as it stands, a ratio only with a word for the picture's shape in the line (画幅, 比例, 屏, "aspect"; 「比分 2:1」, a score, is none), and a frame rate only written as one (帧率, "fps", 每秒 N 帧: 「淡出 24 帧」 is a count of frames); 最长, 上限, 顶多, 别超 and "max" read as at most, 最低, 最短, 下限, 打底, 起步 and "min" as at least; 「预算 2k」 ("budget 2k"), 「报价 4k」 and 「码率 8k」 are no resolution. A line that asks (吗, a question mark, 能不能, 怎么样, 你觉得, 试试, "can you"), supposes (如果, 会不会, "if", "would"), complains (a word for something being wrong, 短了 and 有点短 "a bit short" included), tells how a cut, or an interim one, came out (上一版, 这版, 这次, 第一版, 之前, 原来, 现在, 你交的 "the one you delivered", 只有, 才, 已经, 显示 "shows", 为什么, 怎么, 粗剪 "rough cut", 完整版, "was", "it's", "now", "only", a clause that only says 可以 "fine", or 了 after the number), describes a file on hand (我拍的是 "I shot it in", 相机 "camera", 客户给的 "the client's", 显示器 "monitor", ffprobe), asks for another version (横屏的也做一版, 竖版的也要, 抖音版 30 秒左右, 横屏版, "a landscape version too") or is about a still picture or a screen (a photo, a poster, a cover, a wallpaper, a KV, a GIF, an interface) reads no number at all: 「太长了，改成 2 分钟」 ("too long, make it 2 minutes"), 「这版改成 90 秒」 and 「能不能改成 90 秒？」 read nothing; to change it, say it on its own (「片长改成 2 分钟」) or confirm on the flow board. A number about one part of the work counts for no dimension: a shot, a segment, the opening or ending, titles, the logo, subtitles, the voice-over, the climax, a scene, an empty shot or each page named in its clause («开头控制在 5 秒以内», «logo 在 5 秒左右出现», «C09 做成竖屏»), or named in an earlier clause when its own does not name the whole film (片长, 总长, 成片: «C09 这个镜头，时长 8 秒» is the shot's; «加中英字幕，片长约 2 分钟» still counts); 这段 / 那段 ("this segment") and 采访 ("the interview") are parts too; a line filed under a shot's ticket or an annotation counts for none either (the job's own ticket is not one: it is named after the job, even when that name is cut short on a word like 片尾). In a clause about the footage, a reference, the music or a song, or after an earlier clause about one when its own does not name the whole film, only a number set by such a word counts («把素材剪成 90 秒», "cut the footage to 90 seconds"). A no before a setting word drops the number («不要剪成 90 秒» "don't cut it to 90 seconds», «别压到 60 秒以下»); in «帧率 24 改成 60» or «分辨率从 4K 降到 1080» ("from 4K down to 1080") only the new number can read, and the old one or either of two choices («24 或 30») never does. A number you said no to, a moment or a place in the picture («1 分钟左右切到产品», "cut to the product at about a minute"), a decade ("90s 港风", 「90s 那种感觉」) or how long something else takes ("done within 2 hours", "the task takes about 3 hours") is not read in the first place (see the ledger).

The numbers read are of two kinds. A target sets the number for the deliverable: the word for what it measures in its clause (片长, 时长, 总长, 成片时长, 分辨率, 帧率, 画幅, 比例; "running time", "length", "resolution", "frame rate", "aspect ratio": 「片长约 2 分钟」, 「画幅 9:16」, 「帧率 30」), or a word setting the work to it right before it (剪成, 做成, 改成, 控制在, 压到, 砍到, "make it": 「剪成 90 秒发给我」, 「做成竖屏」), outside the context of the footage or a reference. Any other reading («约 90 秒», «不要超过 2 分钟», «1080p 就行», «要竖屏», «片子差不多 107 秒» "the cut's about 107 seconds") becomes an offer only while that dimension has no target at all. Your words never make a gate by themselves: a number read is only an offer, listed on the flow board, with no card asking whether you want it (from 2026-10-04; until then each offer had a card, 「按你的话加检查：时长 108–132 秒？」 ("Add a check from what you said: running time 108–132 s?"), posted again 「你已经说了 N 次」 each time you said it again — asking about what you had just said); the offer follows the latest target you gave, or failing any, the latest reading. When another line of yours says the same number again (the same number and bound, "about" and a bare number alike: "about 2 minutes", "2 minutes or so" and 「片长 120 秒」 are the same, 「片长 115 秒」 and 「片长改成 130 秒」 are not; saving the same board field again is not another line), it is the same offer, with the count. Only your confirm, on the flow board or on the required-items card (when a requirement you said twice has nothing behind a hand-over), makes it a gate. In F-f you said 「片长约 2 分钟」 twice: the 107-second master was measured as soon as it was delivered and the result shown on the board and in the Bots' situation, holding nothing back; one confirm, and it holds the 107-second master back. A number you removed or turned down, and every other number you had said before one you confirmed, no longer counts from the words you had said by then (no card, no count), only from words said later; when your latest number is the one you removed, nothing is offered, and no older number takes its place. A check in force never changes by itself: a different target you give later makes a replacement card (which number is in force and which you just gave, with Use the new one, Change and Keep the old one), and the check in force stays in force, and holds back, until you choose; said again, the card is posted again beginning 「你已经说了 N 次」 ("You have said it N times"). It is the one time an offer gets a card: unasked, the Bots stay held to the old number. Change puts 「片长改成 」 ("make the running time ") in your composer, so the number you type is what the check stands on. Choosing the new one removes the old; keeping the old removes the offer; giving the old number again withdraws the replacement card. A reading that is no target makes no replacement card. Complaints never touch a check: 「上一版 107 秒太短了」 ("the last cut's 107 seconds is too short"), 「107 秒左右，短了」 and 「你剪成 107 秒了，太短」 read no number.

How much each allows: "about 2 minutes" and "2 minutes or so" are 108–132 seconds, "over 2 minutes" and "no less than 2 minutes" set only a minimum, "within 90 seconds" and "don't go over 90 seconds" only a maximum, and every other number read ("make it 3 minutes", "running time 3 minutes") is also allowed 10% either way; a resolution is the picture's short side (1080p and 1920×1080 are both 1080, 4K is 2160), a frame rate is allowed 10% either way, an aspect ratio is met within 1%, and portrait (landscape) alone means taller than wide (wider than tall). Each dimension has at most one check in force and one offer; a line giving one dimension two different numbers counts for neither.

It measures the job's final cut: of the files a Bot delivered (attached to its lines filed under the job and not under a shot's or another version's ticket — 抖音版, 竖版, 预告…, never the ticket named after the job itself — and still there), a video whose name has the word MASTER (母带 in Chinese) comes before one with the word final (成片, 终版), and those before a video under `deliverables/`, the newest within each; but names are compared only within the same kind of place: a file in a `deliverables/` folder or the job's own folder (its tickets' included) comes before one elsewhere, whatever the name. Whole words only (`master_shot_03.mp4` and `EP01finale.mp4` are not it); never a name that also names a shot, another version or format, titles, a preview or a proxy (`C09_final.mp4`, `EP01_MASTER_9x16.mp4`, `EP01_30s_MASTER.mp4`, `EP01_douyin_MASTER.mp4`, `EP01_trailer_MASTER.mp4`, `intro_MASTER.mp4`, `logo_sting_final.mp4`, `EP01_MASTER_720p_preview.mp4`, `EP01_MASTER_proxy.mp4`, one with 竖版, 片头 or 镜头); and nothing under footage/, raw/, ref/, 素材/ or 参考/. Newest by when it was first delivered, so a reviewer comparing the new master with `EP01_MASTER_v1.mp4` does not move the check back to the old one. A newer one moves it there, and the result on the last file does not carry over. An offer finds the file too and is measured at once, its result shown to you and the Bots: on the board as "To confirm · Fail", expanded as 「未确认的检查：107.00 秒，你说的是时长约 2 分钟（待你确认）」 ("Unconfirmed check: 107.00 s; you said running time about 2 min"), and as such a line in the Bots' situation block and `map.md`; but it holds back no delivery and calls nobody back, lets the organizer mark the plan done, and is left out of "N/M checks pass", so a misread number or a check pointed at the wrong file can never refuse a good delivery (whether reviewers must answer to it waits for submissions and reviews). Once you confirm it, it is read again at once with ffprobe (the picture as it plays: a phone's portrait clip stored as 1920×1080 and flagged to turn 90° is portrait; a running time, picture size or frame rate that cannot be read, or no ffprobe installed, is an error and no evidence either way), and from then on it is an acceptance check like any other: it runs when the plan goes quiet, the organizer cannot mark the plan done while it fails, the app calls the Bot back over it, and handing the file to you again gets it measured on the spot by the closing check. Holding a reviewer's approval to it waits for submissions and reviews (ADR 0040 P4e).

A replacement card goes to the session the job lives in (when you are in it), with its three buttons, and again with the count each time you say it again. Nothing else gets a card; the flow board shows it: a new offer, a check you confirmed first finding its file (moving to a newer cut only measures again), and the line you confirmed a check on being erased — the check goes back to being an offer, never quietly deleted; erasing a later line saying the same number, or filing the line under another job, does not touch it. An offer goes when the words it stands on are erased or filed elsewhere. Each card takes one press; Confirm on an offer a later number already withdrew is refused. The cards are for you and are not in the Bots' transcript. On the flow board these checks are listed under "Checks from what you said", after the words they read ("Running time about 2 min"): an offer can be confirmed, removed and, once bound, rerun; one in force can be removed and rerun; none can be edited, and the local API refuses an edit (`derived_check`); a confirm is `POST /v1/checks/:id/confirm`. They do not count toward the 10-check cap of a plan. Clearing or deleting a session together with what you said there removes the offers standing on those words at once, and turns the checks you confirmed on them back into offers.

In the checks table it is recorded with you as its source and "file exists" as its kind, with further columns for what it measures, the quote it stands on, how it found its file, and whether it is offered or in force: an older build sharing the data folder reads it as a check that the file exists, and while it has no file as a check with no path, which runs as an error and holds nothing back.

From level 5 ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-submissions-and-reviews.md)) it still holds nothing back: no [submission](#submission), no approval, however often you said it. When a same-model [review](#review) passes something you said twice in the ledger with no passing check behind it, what this check measured is written on the card that asks you, and the card can confirm it.

## Pane

One content area in the desktop window. A pane is a group of tabs; a tab can be a session (its artifact preview and flow board go with it; its own settings slide out from the right of the conversation in that tab, with an overlay that covers only this pane, and take no tab of their own), or a terminal (one shell session per tab), the routine calendar, the workspace, spend, or usage. The window can be split horizontally and vertically into any number of panes, and both the dividers and the points where they meet can be dragged; tabs can be dragged to reorder within their own tab bar, dragged between panes, dropped on an edge to split in place, or dragged out while holding ⌥ to become a pane floating on top. Right-clicking anywhere in a pane splits off an empty pane in place, up, down, left or right, or offers Float this tab: the tab right-clicked (on the content, the one showing) lifts out into a floating pane of its own, where it was and a little smaller, while the pane's other tabs stay put; a pane left with no tabs closes up, and the only tiled pane stays behind empty; a tab in a floating pane that holds others can be floated on its own too; a tab's ⋯ offers the same; things that already have their own context menu (messages, the code editor) and input boxes keep theirs, and ⌥-right-click gives the web view's native menu. A floating pane works like the original flow board window: drag the bare part of its tab bar to move it, and double-click there to dock it back into the layout; every edge and every corner can be dragged to resize it, the side across staying put, never smaller than what it shows needs and never outside the workbench; it does not split in place, and its right-click menu offers docking it back into the layout and closing the pane. When there are other panes, an empty pane can be closed directly from its top-right corner (not shown on the only tiled pane: closing it would just leave another empty one, and with nothing else on its tab strip the strip folds away); each pane's context menu can also close the whole pane and its tabs, asking first when the workspace or an artifact preview has unsaved edits. Closing the last tiled pane leaves one empty pane. **A window split into several panes is still the same window process**, and a floating pane is not a second system window either. Only one pane is the current pane at a time — whichever has the keyboard, and clicking or scrolling in a pane also makes it current: Stop hits that pane's turn, and clicking a notification first looks for a pane that already has that session open. When more than one pane is showing, the current one is outlined in the accent color so you can tell at a glance which it is; with only one pane there is no outline. Something already open is only brought forward when asked for again. Where it goes when nothing shows it yet depends on its kind (conversation, Bot ↔ Bot direct, trace, board, plan, artifacts, workspace, terminal, calendar, spend, usage) and is chosen in Settings › Behavior › Where windows open (the Behavior tab is there only with the desktop workbench); the choice is kept on this machine, and narrow windows and phones do not have it ([ADR 0074](adr/0074-where-windows-open.md)). The choices:
- New tab.
- Replace the same kind: the same kind of window in front in the current pane, else in the pane nearest the keyboard that shows one; a new tab when that one holds an unsaved edit or none does. "The same kind" is the same row in the settings: a conversation and a Bot ↔ Bot direct are two kinds, and the trace, board and plan are one each.
- New tab in the background: in the current pane, without bringing it forward.
- Split up, down, left or right: the current pane is divided that way; a new tab when there is no room or the current pane floats.
- The pane above, below, left or right: into the pane on that side; into the current pane when it is itself on that side; otherwise a pane is split off that way.
- Floating: a floating pane over the layout. Where this kind of window was last moved or resized while floating, it opens there again at that size (pulled inside a smaller window, and stepped off a float sitting exactly there); one never moved opens in the middle, stepped down and right past the floats already there, back to the middle after six. The places are kept per kind on this machine, and back to defaults forgets them.

The defaults:
- A conversation replaces the same kind. Going through conversations in the sidebar does not pile up tabs, and in a split window the one replaced is the conversation you were reading, not the artifacts column beside it. A terminal, the workspace and the like are never pushed out.
- A Bot ↔ Bot direct, the trace, board and plan, artifacts and the workspace open in the pane on the right.
- Terminals open in the pane below.
- The calendar and spend open in a new tab.

What you pick from a pane's + or an empty pane goes into that pane. Session settings are open on only one session at a time, and opening them elsewhere closes them where they were; there is only one routine calendar at a time. Each session has only one artifact preview; the flow board's trace, board and plan are each a tab of their own, one of each per session: opening another file or another job from this session, from its preview's file tree or from its flow board turns that one over to it rather than opening another. These tabs open straight from a right-click, without opening the trace first: a message's menu has "This job's trace", "This job's board" and "This job's plan" (the trace lands on the message's card, the board picks the message's ticket), and a session's row in the sidebar and a session tab's ⋯ and right-click menu have "Trace", "Board" and "Plan" (for that session's latest job); an open tab is brought forward, otherwise a new tab opens in the current pane. A tab shows its own view only, with no switch by the title; moving between views (a ticket number or state in the plan, "See it on the trace" and "See in the plan" on the board) opens the other view's tab, carrying the ticket or column to show; a message asking for its card turns the trace's tab. A flow tab saved before this is the trace. The names of these tabs are fixed and follow the session title, "<name> flow", "<name> board", "<name> plan" and "<name>'s artifacts"; they do not change with the open file or the job. Switch away from a flow board tab and back, and it stays where you left it: where you dragged it, the zoom, which turns are folded or unfolded, the filters and the highlighted tickets all stay as they were (the board's and the plan's tabs keep their own highlighted ticket); the same holds when you switch to another job in its switcher and back; it moves only when a message newly asks it to go to a card. A session pane no longer draws its own top bar: the avatar (with a status dot while a Bot is working, waiting on you or has failed, none when idle) and the name are always on the tab, and the top bar's second line has become the tab's tooltip; ⋯ appears only when the mouse is over the tab and holds pin, trace, board, plan and settings, and right-clicking a session tab also offers these items ahead of the split options. Right-clicking any tab (also found in a session tab's ⋯) lets you close that tab, the other tabs in the same pane, the tabs to its right, or all tabs; closing all is the same as closing the last tab: the pane closes with it, asking first when the workspace or an artifact preview has unsaved edits. Other tabs (terminal, workspace, routines, spend, flow, board, plan, artifacts) also always carry their own icon before the name. On narrow screens (≤680px) and phones there are no panes; it is still one screen at a time, stacked layer on layer.

## Model

A completion name that a given endpoint recognizes. Each endpoint has its own list and its own default, and a name on the list can carry a routing reference price, an optional billing price, the thinking levels it supports, and what it is good at. The routing reference price is a relative number the app uses when it picks a model. The billing price is in USD per million tokens: input and output are set together, cached input can be set separately, and when left blank it uses the input price. The billing price is used to estimate spend when no reported amount is available; probing the model list does not fill in prices automatically. When the user's message starts a turn, the app picks the model and thinking level only from the list of the endpoint the Bot is pinned to (the default endpoint if it has none pinned); adding another endpoint in Settings does not bring its models into other Bots' choices. If the Bot has pinned a name that is still on that list, the model name is held to it; a pinned name that exists only on another endpoint is used as pinned. A Bot can also pin a thinking level (the completion's `reasoning_effort`): the level names follow that model's list (commonly none / low / medium / high, plus xhigh, max and so on when the endpoint advertises them). When probing `/models`, if an object carries thinking levels they are written into that model's `thinking_levels` automatically, and you can still edit them by hand in Settings. The thinking level is pinned together with the model and never exists on its own: either neither is pinned and the app picks both the model and the level for each message, or a model is pinned and its level is set along with it. Pinning a model without a level falls back to that model's default level (the app's order of preference for ordinary messages); switching to a model that doesn't support the current level falls back to the new model's default level; clearing the model clears the level with it; giving a level without a pinned model is rejected. You can change these with one click in the Bot panel, and a Bot can change its own with a tool. An empty pin does not mean "always use the endpoint's default model"; it means the app may pick from that endpoint's list. To use another endpoint, you must pin the endpoint explicitly. Who picks (below level 7; from level 7 see Default models below): each time a turn starts, an agent first picks once. It looks at the message, this Bot's profile, the candidate list, this Bot's recent review conclusions (each with the message and category it came from, ignored when they don't match) and recent examples that finished cleanly, and answers with a model, a thinking level and a one-sentence reason. The agent itself always runs on the default endpoint's default model; if it can't answer, or answers with something that doesn't exist, the choice falls back to rules, so the user is never stuck on model routing. Everything the user says afterward is kept verbatim (not filtered by keyword) and attached to the quoted turn, to the latest turn of the Bot that was `@`-mentioned, or to the latest visible turn in the session. Consecutive turns about the same thing form one correction chain. When the chain ends (the topic changes or things go quiet for a while), it is reviewed once; a turn with no follow-up is also reviewed once if the endpoint refused it, its reply was incomplete, or it hit at least two tool errors. The review looks at the whole chain plus what the turn actually did: was it the model falling short, the task itself being hard, or the request being unclear? Only a verdict that the model fell short is kept as this Bot's experience, together with which way to adjust: stronger / lighter / faster / cheaper. What is kept is a conclusion, not a score: there are no weights, offsets or caps. A conclusion that has been followed twice and still hasn't led to a cleaner result drops out of model routing, though its record stays. The experience belongs to the Bot itself and is deleted along with the Bot. What each turn chose, how it ended, how much it did and what feedback it received are all recorded. The user does not pick for each turn. The judgement call and the turn's completions use the same resolved result.

**Default models (from level 7, and the default is level 8; [ADR 0048](adr/0048-default-models.md))**: no model call picks what a turn runs on any more. It goes by a fixed order: the model you pinned on the Bot; with no pin, the Bot's default model; with no default, the endpoint's default. The thinking level is the pinned one, or, when the model does not offer it or none is pinned, the nearest it offers for this kind of message; a Bot pinned to an endpoint only chooses from that endpoint's list; a pin no endpoint lists any more runs on the endpoint's default, and you are told once; the pin stays, and once the model is listed again it is the pin again. The Bot panel shows it as "not listed" and saves other edits as usual. Before, taking a model off an endpoint's list in Settings wiped every pin on it on the spot, with nothing said: on 2026-10-03 that moved both video Bots off claude-opus. Below level 7 the pin still goes with the list. A default is set the first time it is needed: what the Bot ran on most in the last 7 days among the models the endpoints still list (a renamed or removed one does not count), at the thinking level it ran that model on most, and it is used from then on with no card asking you: pin a model on the Bot to change it (from 2026-10-04; until then a card in your direct with it said so — "runs on X (thinking Y) from now on: what it ran on most in the last 7 days (N turns)" [Keep it] [Use the endpoint's default] — with a "waiting for your answer" notification, one for each of four Bots on 2026-10-03, the day level 7 went on: asking about nothing you had to decide, used whether you answered or not). The work log records `model.default_inferred`. Cards already out still take their buttons: declined, nothing is inferred again. On the board a ticket can also be given a model, ahead of the pin and the default (`ticket_override`), for its owner's turns only; the Bot reviewing it keeps its own. The model may be an endpoint's or one of your local agents' ([ADR 0079](adr/0079-local-agents.md)): the ticket's model decides who does it — an agent's runs those turns on that agent, as a ladder rung does; an endpoint's runs them in the app's own loop, even for a Bot that usually runs on an agent. Each turn's route record carries the reason: `ticket_override`, `pin`, `default`, `endpoint_default`, `pin_unlisted`, `capability_filter` or `escalation`. Reviews and learning go on as before, until level 8 replaces them with [quality events and lessons](#learning).

**Pictures and stepping up (from level 7; [ADR 0049](adr/0049-capability-filter-and-escalation.md))**: a model can be marked in the endpoint settings as taking pictures, taking none, or not sure (counted as taking them). When a turn is opened by a line with a picture, or the Bot reviews a ticket with a requirement about the picture, and the model picked is marked as taking none: a pin stays and you are told once; otherwise it moves to a model marked as taking pictures (`capability_filter`). A picture read mid-turn is not sent to a model that takes none; the tool result says so. A job whose hand-overs failed twice in a row since its last step runs one thinking level higher (`escalation`), up to its model's top, and resets once the ticket is approved; a thinking level you pinned is not raised, nor one on a model measured to gain nothing from it.

**The model ladder and stepping up inside a turn (from level 7; [ADR 0054](adr/0054-model-ladder-and-in-turn-triggers.md))**: in Settings → Models you can order a few models weaker to stronger as a ladder (at most 8). When the thinking levels are used up (or the model is measured to gain nothing from them) and a job needs more, its next turn moves one model up the ladder (`escalation_model`), past rungs no endpoint lists any more and, for work that needs pictures, rungs marked as taking none; asking for more past the last rung stays there and you are told once. Only a model the app chose for the Bot moves along the ladder; your pin and a ticket's model only step up their thinking level; with no ladder, or a model not on it, you are told once at the top. Besides failed hand-overs, a reply that fails again after its retry, tool arguments that are not JSON twice in a row, and the same tool called wrongly three times in a row (the model's own mistakes, like bad arguments or a refused call made anyway; a missing file, a timeout or an MCP server's error do not count) each step the job up — at most once between two hand-overs, and only in thinking level, never along the ladder; the last two switch the rest of that turn to the new thinking level. The route record keeps why the model was chosen first; the trace reads "Set on this ticket · one thinking level higher". Turns on a climbed model or a ticket's model do not count toward the Bot's inferred default.

**Claude models on the ladder ([ADR 0076](adr/0076-claude-models-on-the-model-ladder.md))**: with your Claude Code installed and signed in, the ladder can also hold the Claude models Claude Agent settings offer (sonnet, opus, haiku, fable), each rung with its own effort (default, low … max) and account (when several are listed); one model may take another rung at another effort, such as opus high and opus max. When a Bot on the app's own loop climbs to such a rung, its next turn is handed to your Claude Code on that rung's model, effort and account, run as a Claude Agent Bot's turns are; a rung above it on an endpoint takes it back to the app's own loop. Claude models see pictures, so picture work never skips them. A Claude Agent Bot's own Claude model is your pin and does not climb; trouble inside its turn is Claude Code's to handle and steps nothing up. Only you put a Claude model on the ladder: with `update_model_settings` a Bot can keep, move or drop the ones already there, never add one; an account a rung spends can be taken off the list only once that rung is gone or moved to another account.

**Built-in models ([ADR 0077](adr/0077-built-in-models.md))**: each model call the app makes on its own can be given a model, on the Roles page of Settings ([ADR 0082](adr/0082-roles-page.md); [ADR 0075](adr/0075-organizing-model.md) began this by giving the organizer, the scribe and the picture checks a model of their own, ADR 0077 gathered the nine calls on one page under Models, and ADR 0082 joined that page with these calls' prompts as Roles). At the top is a map, one lane for each thing that sets calls off: "A line of yours" is first [read](#line-reading), wakes a Bot's turn once read, and [the scribe](#scribe) writes it down beside that, holding nothing up; within the turn, compaction runs near the context window, the organizer ([the organizer](#organizer)) once the work goes quiet, and the picture judge when a part is handed in (the calls in the sample and seams checks that have a model compare); "A group line naming nobody" is first judged (joining in), and a Bot that takes it up goes on to its turn; "You accept a delivery" is followed by the retrospective; "You overturn an approval" by reflection; "You press ✨" asks the composer. Each call is a node naming its model (or what it follows when none is chosen), with a dot when a prompt of it is edited and a warning-coloured one for a conflict or unreadable answers. On a wide window the map runs down the left and the picked call sits on the right, each scrolling on its own, so a pick only changes the right side and nothing jumps; ↑ and ↓ move between calls. On a phone the map is a page of its own and a node opens as another. The call's side shows what it does, its model and the built-in prompts it runs with (four for reading, four for the picture judge, one each for the rest), each opening in the same prompt editor. A Bot's turn is a dashed node: it runs on the Bot's own model and moves up the model ladder, with every turn's prompts; neither is set here, so the node only links to the ladder and to the Prompts page. Not on the page: the route pick and the route review and learning (not run from level 7 and level 8), speech recognition (a service and key of its own) and the speed test. A call can be an endpoint's model, a Claude model run through your own Claude Code (the Claude Agent group; with an account to pick when more than one is listed), or unset. Unset runs as before: the first three groups on the default endpoint's default model, the composer on the default endpoint's model whose name has flash, mini, lite or fast in it, and the four as-a-Bot calls on the Bot's own model (compaction on the turn's own), none of them with a thinking level named. Only a chosen model is told how hard to think, by what the call wants: the ones you wait on (reading, the composer, judgement) the lightest level; the frequent ones (the scribe, compaction) `low`, else the lightest level above `none`; the ones that read the whole and must judge well (the organizer, the picture judge, reflection, retrospective) `high` (when the model does not list `high`, the highest it lists up to `high`; when it lists only higher ones, the lightest of those). A Claude model runs the call once on your Claude Code, tool-free, one turn, on the account you picked; the level becomes Claude Code's effort — none named for the lightest, `low` and `high` as they are. Calls other than reading get three places of their own, apart from reading's two, so a long settle cannot hold up the reading of your next line; a call may take its old first-byte limit plus 180 s; it spends that Claude plan's usage, billed under the call's own kind and purpose and marked Claude Agent, at an estimated price. With Claude Code missing or signed out, an error or a timeout, each call handles the failure as it did before. A call that sends pictures sends them as image blocks of its one message; every Claude model reads them, so the picture judge on a Claude model always runs on it; with an endpoint's model, a judge about to send pictures (the sample's frames, the two sides of a seam) needs it marked "takes pictures" in its attributes, and unmarked or marked as not it uses the default model for that call, while a judge that only compares words is not held to this. The organizer reads the whole plan and writes it back, so pick a strong model for it: on Opus one settle is about 20k tokens in and 6k out, far more than a reading, and each plan settles again once it goes quiet; nothing is spent until you choose, and unchosen, nothing changes. When the endpoint stops listing the chosen model, or is deleted, the call reads as unset; the model back on the list is the choice again. An organizing model chosen before is copied once, on upgrade, to the scribe and the picture judge, each now changeable on its own; `organizer_model` now means the organizer alone. The number on Roles in the settings sidebar is how many calls you changed (a model chosen or a prompt edited, each call once), warning-coloured when a prompt conflicts; the Prompts page keeps only the every-turn, Claude Agent and tool-description groups, with a line pointing to Roles (with an older Mac that does not yet tie prompts to calls, the app's own calls stay on Prompts as before). A Bot sees `builtin_models` in `list_endpoints` and can change it with `update_model_settings`, but only to an endpoint's model or none, never to your Claude model (that spends your plan), as with reading.

**Setting up on Claude Code alone ([ADR 0078](adr/0078-setup-on-claude-code.md))**: step 2 of the wizard can take "Claude Code on this computer" in place of an endpoint. It goes on only once a signed-in Claude Code is found; step 3 picks one Claude model (sonnet by default) and the account, and saving puts all nine built-in calls on it, creates no endpoint, and makes the first Bot a Claude Agent. A workspace plus the eight built-in calls other than compaction on Claude models completes the wizard; with no endpoint, putting one of them back on the default brings the wizard back. Set up this way and with no usable endpoint, a new Bot made without saying what runs it is a Claude Agent on reading's account, a teammate a Bot hires with `create_bot` too; the new-Bot form starts on Claude Agent; an explicit app runner is honored, and once an endpoint is added nothing switches by itself. When other agents are found and signed in on this computer (Codex, Grok, …, [ADR 0079](adr/0079-local-agents.md)), step 2 lists them beside Claude Code and picking one works the same way: step 3 picks its model, and the first Bot and the new Bots after it run on it. With no endpoint, judgements, reflections and retrospectives still run on the Claude model chosen; Bots on an endpoint, the model ladder, and learning memories and skills from a chain wait for an endpoint.

## Local agents (Codex, Grok, OpenCode, ZCode, Antigravity and ACP agents of your own)

**Turning it on**: besides Deskfolk and Claude Agent, the Bot panel's Runner offers Codex, Grok, OpenCode, ZCode, Antigravity and any ACP agent you added in Settings (by the name you gave it); also when creating a Bot, and only you can change it. Then set the model (as that agent spells it, e.g. `gpt-5.6-luna`, `grok-4.7`, `nvidia/z-ai/glm-5.3`; empty for its default, with suggestions where it lists models), the effort (only the levels it takes; OpenCode takes none) and the account (Codex can list several). One that is missing or signed out is greyed with why. The endpoint pin stays, and the app's own calls about the Bot still run on an endpoint (or, set up on an agent alone, on that agent).

**Settings › Agents**: one line per agent, for the whole picture first: its logo, whether it can run a Bot (Ready / Sign in / Not installed), a summary (the account or plan it is signed in with and how many models it lists; one signed in with many providers, like OpenCode, reads "OpenCode Go and 6 more") and how many Bots run on it; ready ones first, then those to sign in, the ones not installed folded at the foot ("N not installed"), with "Check all again" at the top. The list is there at once: each agent as last seen (kept in the database, across restarts), and one not asked in the last minute asked again behind it, its line catching up by itself; only one never asked, and "Check all again", are waited for. A line opens to the details: in place on a wide window, one at a time; on a phone as a page of its own, Back going to the list. The details are where it was found (as for Claude Code: the path you set, PATH, the usual install places — npm globals under nvm included — then your login shell), its version, its sign-in (Codex's plan, what Grok signs in with, the providers OpenCode has credentials for; ZCode's is known at the first turn), how many models it lists, and the network (proxy as for Claude); signed out, it shows the command to run in a terminal; "Set the path…" and "Add an account…" show their fields when asked (not found, the path field is there at once). The last line, "Your own ACP agents", opens to a name, the command's full path and its arguments — any program that speaks the Agent Client Protocol (e.g. `gemini --acp` with an API key); one a Bot, rung or built-in call uses cannot be removed. The phone shows and sets the same.

**A turn**: as for Claude Agent, a fresh session per turn in the turn's work dir, the first message the situation block and transcript window; the Bot's profile, skills, system text and memories go in Codex's developer instructions and at the head of the first message for ACP agents and Antigravity (they have no system prompt of their own). What you set up in these agents is kept out of a Bot's turn as far as each allows: Codex's hooks, memories, plugins, apps, AGENTS.md and your MCP servers are off for the turn, Grok does not join its shared leader, OpenCode runs its own private server — all passed at launch; your config files are never changed.

**Boundary and approvals**: Claude Agent's rules, and every call stopped before it runs where the agent allows: Grok has the app read, write and run everything, decided first; OpenCode asks before every command and write; Codex asks before every command and file change — inside the workspace they go ahead, out of it you get the approval card. What cannot be stopped — a call the agent made without asking and reported afterwards — is checked when reported, and one out of the workspace or on any agent's credentials (`~/.codex`, `~/.grok`, `~/.claude`, OpenCode's folders, `~/.dsh`, `~/.gemini`, the keychain…) stops the turn, saying what it did. Antigravity offers no callback at all: it works with the permission you set in `agy`, the app runs it sandboxed (its commands cannot read `~/.ssh` and the like) and checks each tool as it starts, stopping the turn out of bounds.

**Collaboration**: the app's own tools (posting, endings, hand-overs, reviews, delegation, asking you, check-backs…) reach ACP agents as an MCP server named deskfolk and Codex as tools of its own; Grok finds them through its tool search and OpenCode calls them from code in its `execute` tool, as the preface tells them. Antigravity gets none: its Bot speaks only through its closing reply, saying there what it hands over, passes on or needs to ask, for you to take up, and a segment ends after a second bounce. Lines you send meanwhile: Codex takes them at once; the others with the result of the next app tool they call, or at the next turn. Insert-now cuts Codex and ACP agents short; Antigravity cannot be, and the line waits for its step to end.

**Records and usage**: route rows say `agent_codex` and the like; spend is recorded under the agent's name (tokens from Codex and Antigravity; Grok and OpenCode report none), priced as an estimate. Once one of them is connected (or something runs on it), it shows in the usage widget (see "Usage" under Claude Agent below): Codex, Grok and Antigravity with what is left of their own plan windows (Codex's app-server; Grok's ACP extension `_x.ai/billing`, one weekly window; Antigravity's `agy -p "/quota"`, a 5-hour and a weekly window per model group, not asked of an `agy` older than 1.1.11, which would send it to the model as a prompt), none of it through a model or spending anything; OpenCode, ZCode and your own ACP agents with today's turns and tokens here only — never an invented percentage.

**Built-in calls and the ladder**: Settings › Models' built-in calls and model ladder may name these agents' models. A built-in call asks the agent once in a scratch folder with no tools and cuts it off if it tries one; a ladder rung may be any agent's model and effort, and a turn that climbs to it runs on that agent.

**Failures**: missing, signed out and out of usage name the agent and only you can clear them, so the supervisor does not retry; an agent exiting mid-turn is the app's side; "acted outside the workspace or on credentials without asking" is yours to look at before anything is retried. See [ADR 0079](adr/0079-local-agents.md).

<a id="claude-agent"></a>
## Claude Agent (run by your own Claude Code)

**Turning it on**: in the Bot panel set "Runs on" to "Claude Agent (your own Claude Code)", also when creating a Bot; only you can change it — a Bot changing its own or another's is refused. Then pick the Claude model (Claude Code's default, sonnet, opus, haiku, fable) and effort (default, low … max). The endpoint pin stays: the app's own judgements about the Bot (whether to join a group line and the like) still run on an endpoint, so one is still needed.

**Finding your Claude Code**: the path set in Settings, the daemon's PATH, the usual install places (`~/.local/bin`, `/opt/homebrew/bin`, `/usr/local/bin`, the npm global dir …), then your login shell. On Windows: PATH (the native `claude.exe` and npm's `claude.cmd` both count), `%USERPROFILE%\.local\bin\claude.exe` and `%APPDATA%\npm\claude.cmd`, with no login shell step. The "Claude Agent" card in Settings › Agents shows where it was found, its version, the account (a Claude subscription, a long-lived token, an API key, a third-party platform) and the network (direct, or which proxy), and warns when it is not signed in, bills per token (an API key), sends requests elsewhere (`ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL`), or is older than the Agent SDK the app ships with. The app only runs it and asks it `claude --version` and `claude auth status`; it offers no sign-in and never reads its credentials — sign in by running `claude` in a terminal. The card and its routes exist on this Mac only.

**Several accounts**: Claude Code keeps each account's sign-in in a config directory — `~/.claude` while `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` is unset, the directory it names otherwise — so one computer can have several accounts signed in at once. The Claude Agent card is grouped by account: location, version and network at the top, then under Accounts a block per account — its plan and email, the directory it reads and its usage (once a Bot runs on it), the default account tagged Default and the others with Remove; the card's and the accounts' explanations sit behind a "?" beside their headings, shown on hover or a click. Under Accounts, add the other accounts' directories (a full path, `~/` works; not the root, your home folder or anything above it): the card runs `claude auth status` with each and lists its plan and email, or the command that signs it in (such as `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude-b claude auth login`). Then pick a Bot's account under "Claude account" in its panel: a Bot left alone uses the default one above (the one Claude Code finds in the app's own environment, as before). With a directory picked, the Bot's turns, the sign-in check before each and its usage run Claude Code with that directory; for `~/.claude` itself `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` is unset rather than named, since naming it makes Claude Code look for another keychain item and read as signed out. When that account is signed out, the turn stops before it starts and names the directory. Only you change a Bot's account (a Bot doing so is refused), and the phone switches among the listed ones; a directory a Bot runs on can be removed only once the Bot is moved; listing or removing one works from the computer and the phone alike (`PUT /v1/runtime/claude-code/accounts`); the whole Claude Agent card is on the phone too, path and Check again included — only signing in needs a terminal on the computer. Every listed directory is as off limits to the Bots' file tools and commands as `~/.claude`. The app keeps the directory and reads nothing in it.

**Network**: an `HTTPS_PROXY` / `ALL_PROXY` in the daemon's environment goes to Claude Code as it is (`NO_PROXY` can take Anthropic out); with none, the system's HTTPS proxy (System Settings › Network › Proxies on a Mac, Settings › Network & internet › Proxy on Windows — the one browsers follow) is handed to it as `HTTPS_PROXY`. The app started from Finder, the Start menu or at login does not get your terminal's proxy variables, and some networks reach Anthropic only through a proxy — without this, every request is refused. Only `HTTPS_PROXY` is set, so a Bot's own requests to local `http://` addresses stay direct. A PAC file or a SOCKS-only proxy is not followed.

**How a turn runs**: each turn starts a fresh Claude Code session in the turn's work dir (the workspace root until the work belongs to a job), able to read and write the whole workspace. Its first message is the situation block and the transcript window (as for any Bot; its own lines marked as its own, pictures included; the work dir as a full path on this machine, the only kind Claude Code's file tools take); the system prompt is Claude Code's own plus the Bot's profile, skills, memories and the system text every Bot gets. Your `~/.claude` settings, hooks, plugins and CLAUDE.md stay out of it (the SDK's isolation mode); the keychain sign-in and an API key in the environment still work. Only Read, Write, Edit, NotebookEdit, Glob, Grep, Bash, WebFetch, WebSearch, Agent (subtasks) and TodoWrite are offered; nothing runs in the background, and `/commands` or `@paths` in the transcript are not expanded.

**Boundary and approvals**: reads, writes and commands inside the workspace run; outside the workspace, or a command reaching out, raises the same approval card any Bot raises (your always-allow rules count); a recursive search over your home folder is refused as always; a path under a top-level folder that does not exist (usually a workspace path written as `/work/…`) is refused with where the workspace is, with no card — on Windows, a file path with no drive; Claude Code's own settings and credentials (`~/.claude`, `~/.claude.json`, the keychain) are refused outright, with no card. Stop, holds and read-only turns apply as for any Bot; Stop ends Claude Code and every command it started (on Windows, the whole process tree with `taskkill /T`).

**Working with others**: progress, endings, hand-overs, reviews, delegation, questions to you, check-backs, laying out tickets, memory, skills and annotations go through the same tools as for any Bot (named `mcp__deskfolk__…` on its side), under the same rules; questions to you only through `ask_user`. Its last reply that calls no tool is the message it posts, and goes through the closing check, the hand-over of the files it cites and the end contract like any Bot's; a line sent back comes back into the same session to answer again. Text in the same message as a tool call is shown while it works and never sent. A segment on the whole job whose command or write lands in the folder of a ticket of its own moves onto that ticket at once, as any Bot's does, so a reply carrying those files goes out first and hands them over; it used to move only at `submit`, and a reply the end contract sent back was held back with it. When it reviews pictures it looks with its own Read, which the app records as seen.

**Records**: a route row per turn (no endpoint, reason `claude_code`; a turn that climbed onto a Claude rung keeps `escalation_model` as the reason it was chosen first); spend per model under the name "Claude Agent", priced as an estimate (a subscription is not billed per token); the commands it ran, files it wrote and effects it had are recorded as for any Bot.

**Usage** ([ADR 0080](adr/0080-one-usage-view.md)): every local agent's usage is in one place. A wide window has a **usage ball** floating top right: a small ball whose ring is as full as what is left of the tightest window of all (rounded down, so a window in use never reads 100%; amber with a quarter left, red with a tenth, the ball's outline too). Drag it anywhere; dropped within 24 px of the left or right edge it docks there half tucked away and slides out when pointed at. Pointed at, the ball **morphs** into a column of the agents (the one shape grows from the ball into a bar, the bubbles coming in one after another; upward from a ball in the lower half): each bubble wears the agent's mark, ringed by its tightest window over all its accounts, a dashed ring for an agent with today's records only. Point at an agent and **a card grows out of its bubble** toward the middle of the window: that agent's turns and tokens today here, then each account (the plan as a small label, the email muted after it, the directory when it has several, Codex's credits; with several accounts each sits in an outlined box of its own, its windows inside) with a line per window — its name, when it resets, what is left and a bar of what is left; an agent with today's records only says why there is no more. The card ends with when it was checked and Refresh. Another agent's card grows out of its own bubble; crossing from the bubble to the card keeps it, and it folds back into the ball a moment after the pointer leaves all of it. A click on the ball pins it open (how touch opens it), a click on an agent pins its card; Escape or a click outside folds it. With reduced motion asked for, nothing animates. The ball's context menu has "Hide the usage widget"; the usage tab's "Show the usage widget" brings it back. Tools › Usage and the menu bar's "Show all usage…" open a **Usage** tab on the workbench: a bar with when it was checked and Refresh (and "Show the usage widget" while the widget is hidden), then a card per account in a grid across the tab — an agent's accounts side by side under its name and day, several agents in a row on a wide tab (cards in a row one height, heads level), one column in a narrow pane; each window is a row: its name ("Fable · 7 days" for a model's window, on one line when it fits; a long model name like Antigravity's wraps in full, "· 7 days" kept whole), ↻ when it starts over and what is left, with a bar of what is left beneath; the agents with today's records only are a row of chips at the end. Like Spend there is only one: asked for again it is brought forward, and when it is not open it goes where Settings › Behavior › Where windows open says for Usage (a new tab by default). A wide window with no workbench (a tablet on a remote connection) still opens the widget. No local agent connected, no widget. A phone has no widget: Tools › Usage opens a page in the list laid out like the usage tab in one column (a head per agent, a card per account), but each window a dial with what is left inside, its name (a long model name wrapping in full) and ↻ beneath, a card's dials centred, up to three a row and two a row from four (Antigravity's 2 × 2), with when it was checked and Refresh at the end; Back at its top left; it and the archived list are pages in the URL like the calendar (`?o=usage`, `?o=archived`), so the phone's Back returns to the list, and a conversation opened from the archived list goes back to that list first. Settings › Agents shows one line under each account ("5h 62% · 7d 81%", the same ring). The menu bar icon's menu starts with a line per account: the agent's mark inside a thin ring for the tightest window (the mark alone for an account signed out or not read), the name and what is left of the 5-hour and 7-day windows (model windows only in the panel); agents with no plan share one line ("Grok 12 turns · OpenCode 0 turns"); eight lines at most, then "Show all usage…", which opens the window and the usage tab (menu icons do not follow the light or dark menu, so the colours read on both). Claude's numbers are fetched from claude.ai by your own Claude Code (the data behind its `/usage`, asked through the Agent SDK's `get_usage` in a session that sends no prompt and is closed right after) — no model, no quota spent, and the app never reads its credentials; Codex's come from its own app-server. Accounts listed are every connected one (found in Settings › Agents and not signed out) and every one in use (Bots, agent rungs on the model ladder, built-in calls), with or without a Bot on it; each asked once (two that are the same directory once). The daemon keeps each answer five minutes and the widget and the menu bar ask about once a minute, so at most one `claude` starts per five minutes; with nothing on Claude nothing is asked. "Today" is per agent (spend rows under its name since local midnight, turns counted as distinct turns), not per account. The SDK marks Claude's request experimental; if Claude Code changes it, that account says it could not be read (`GET /v1/usage`, readable from a phone over the relay; the older `GET /v1/claude-usage` and `GET /v1/agent-usage` stay for clients not updated yet).

**Failures**: Claude Code missing, unable to authenticate (not signed in, or its requests refused — with Claude Code's own error line), or out of usage (with when it resets; with extra usage on, going past the plan's limit is not a failure) are yours to clear, so the supervisor does not retry them; Claude Code exiting mid-turn is the app's side, also with its error line. When a request fails, the error line Claude Code writes is never posted as the Bot's reply. Past 200 steps the turn counts as stuck. See [ADR 0061](adr/0061-claude-agent-runner.md).

## Hold

**From 2026-10-09: a stop is only "stop for now"** ([ADR 0071](adr/0071-a-stop-is-only-stop-for-now.md)). Every stop of yours — Stop on a card, a typed "stop" / "stop X" / "stop every Bot", the group and tools menus, the flow board — does two things: it stops the work it covers now, and until you speak it holds what would start the Bot again by itself (its check-backs, other Bots' lines, job completions, the supervisor's resumes). Nothing you say or do to a Bot is ever held: a line said to it, a change to your line, Insert now, Send back on a hand-over's card, your answer to its question, Continue on an interrupted or restart notice, "continue", Lift in the list — it does it. A stop on that Bot alone is lifted; one over more Bots (everything, a group, a job, a ticket) releases that Bot (`effect.released_bots`, read by every held check, the database's own triggers included), and the rest stay stopped until you speak to them or lift it; once every Bot it covers is released it is lifted and the plans it parked go back. A line only asking where the work stands ("still at it?") is no go on: the Bot answers it, still stopped. Lift is going on: the stopped work opens again on a note. Where the text below says a stop stays until you lift it, or that your line only opens a read-only turn, that now holds only for the app's own holds (old parked plans taken over at boot) and the one that drops the old job when you open a new one; a line you sent before the stop and no Bot has read yet is held, and Insert now reads it.

**From 2026-10-10: a stop is just a stop** ([ADR 0081](adr/0081-a-stop-is-just-a-stop.md)). Every stop your word is about is lifted whole, however many Bots it covers: any line to any Bot after "Stop every Bot", any line in a group after its stop, any line about a job after its stop ends it; every covered Bot's check-backs, other Bots' lines and job completions wake again, and parked plans go back. `effect.released_bots` is no longer written (one an older build wrote is still read until your word lifts the hold). Only the Bots you speak to go on (for "continue", with the job they stopped); the other Bots' stopped turns are not reopened on a note. A stop leaves no state on screen: no stop for now above the conversation list, no "Stopped" on rows, no sleeping avatar; the bar above the list is left for stops you dropped a job with by a button, which no line ends. The flow board lists only its job's own parking and holds that wait to be lifted. Where the text below says "releases", "the rest stay stopped", "all bots continue", "Stopped" in the list or "All go on", this paragraph wins.

**P4b resume addition**: the scopes, user-only lifts and receipts below remain valid. At level 2 resumed segments obey Bot × plan uniqueness and work slots: an existing same-plan segment hears the line; another plan starts or queues its own. “One Bot works one turn at a time in a group or Bot↔Bot direct” below describes level-0/1 compatibility only and no longer merges another job into that segment. Protected Continue for an interrupted desk is under [Desk segment](behavior.en.md#desk-segment); this change activates no automatic restart supervision.

A stop of yours, recorded as a row. **Scope**: everything; one Bot (following it into every session it is in); one session (and the plans that session is home to, even where the work goes on in a Bot↔Bot direct — work in that direct is still the group's job); one plan (tickets included); one ticket; one Bot's work in one plan; or one turn (covering the check-backs that turn booked). It may also carry more it covers, fixed when it was made — the directs holding the work that Bot handed on, say — which does not change afterwards. Only you lift it: with a line of yours, or a button; no other caller has a way to lift one, neither a Bot nor the organizer. The record is never deleted: clearing a chat or deleting a group only blanks the line it points at, and the hold itself stays. A plan a hold names (a hold on that plan, on one Bot's work in it or on one of its tickets, or with any of those among its targets, lifted or not) stays when the chat is cleared too, so no hold points at a job that is gone. Deleting a group takes its surviving plans (the ones a turn or a line in another session still belongs to) out of it, and with that out of reach of a hold on the group; so each one that hold keeps parked first gets a plan hold of its own (source `migration`), carrying how to put it back. So does one stopped the old way before the hold (parked in its status and its spec): lifting its hold puts it back in progress, as the boot import of such plans does. One already under a hold on the plan itself needs none.

**When it is made**: in the same write that records it, every running turn it covers ends as stopped; once the write is in, the turn's model call, its MCP calls (the server is told the call is cancelled) and its commands' process groups (asked to exit, killed 3 seconds later) are aborted. Where each had got to — the files it wrote, its last few calls — is recorded on the hold (`effect.stopped_turns`), with the other Bots' turns still working in the same plans that it does not cover (`working_beside`) and anything it covers still running afterwards (`still_running`, empty unless something went wrong). A stop you say or press also covers the work the covered Bot handed on and has not had back: a turn of another Bot opened by a line of a covered turn — or by a line of such a turn, up to three handoffs down — that is still running or still has a check-back to come. Each becomes a target on that one turn, which covers the turn, what it booked and whom its lines would wake, and not the other Bot's own work; nothing upstream is stopped. A read-only turn a hold let open is left to finish its answer. The work log gets a `control.hold` row.

**Plan status**: a hold on a plan or a session sets the in-progress plans it covers to parked, in the status and in the spec's status both — the flow board sends the spec's status back with every edit, and the organizer revises the spec too, so changing only one of them would get the plan set back to In progress. The plan stays the session's current plan: it is stopped, not pushed aside by another job. A plan already pushed out of the current slot by a newer one (parked in its status, In progress in its spec) has its spec set to parked as well, and lifting puts it back to being pushed aside — In progress in its spec, parked in its status — rather than In progress, so that a plan out of the slot is not called back to work. While the hold stands, the organizer or a resume asking for In progress leaves it parked and only notes that lifting the hold should bring it back; one pushed out of the current slot meanwhile by a plan opened or resumed there goes back to being pushed aside when the hold is lifted; a plan opened in that session is parked from the start; marking it Done still goes through. Setting the spec to parked and setting it back on the lift are each a version of the spec: a flow board still on the version before has its edit refused (`409 revision_conflict`) instead of sending back the status the hold just changed — undoing the stop, or stopping again after the lift. Neither version counts as yours or as organizing: the organizer still reads what was said before the stop, and the revision history marks both `cause: "hold"`. Lifting the hold puts it back as it was, and back into the session's current slot if that is free; if another hold on the plan or its session still has it, it stays parked, and that one keeps the note of how to put it back. A hold on a Bot, a ticket or a turn leaves plan status alone, and the plan's other Bots carry on; one on everything leaves it alone too (it would otherwise rewrite every plan there is), and a plan's `held_by` lists the holds over the plan as a whole (on the plan, on its session, on everything).

**The old way of setting status**: once the engine level has reached holds, setting a plan's status to parked (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/spec`, as older clients and the phone do) is a hold on that plan; setting it back to In progress, or marking it Done, lifts the holds on that plan. With a hold on its session or on everything still standing, setting it back to In progress leaves it parked (the session's) or as it was (everything's), and `held_by` says which. Whether an edit sets the status is judged against the spec's status, which is what the flow board shows and sends back: an edit of only the goal sends it back unchanged and neither stops nor resumes anything. A plan pushed aside reads parked in its status column and In progress in its spec, so judging by the column would take such an edit for a resume. Below that level, parked is only the status.

**Check-backs**: a pending check-back a hold covers is set aside, and marked voided at the same time so that an older build that knows nothing of holds never fires it; one booked while the hold stands is set aside too. Once lifted, with no other hold over it, it is pending again, and one whose time has already passed fires once shortly after. If, while it is set aside, the session is cleared or deleted, the Bot is deleted, or — before holds are on — the turn that booked it is Stopped, it is voided for good and lifting does not bring it back; at levels 0–2 another booking in the same session replaces the old one; at level 3 only a timer of the same exact work-item/dedupe key replaces it, never an unrelated job or delegation event wait.

**Wakes and tools**: nothing a hold covers is woken. Your line (it opens only a read-only turn, below), another Bot's line (naming it, or any line in a Bot↔Bot direct), a check-back of its coming due, the report-back when a Bot↔Bot direct it opened goes quiet, a quiet plan's call-back, a routine coming due, Continue on an interrupted turn: none opens a turn, and a running turn a hold covers hears no new line either: one about work beside the hold opens a turn of its own next to it, without cutting the held one off, and one of yours in another conversation that is filed under the job does not reach it. A held Bot's own line wakes nobody, and a Bot↔Bot direct whose other side is held calls nobody back when it goes quiet; nothing is kept for later, since that side's next turn in the direct after the lift makes it go quiet again, and that calls the opener back. When you say something in a group without naming anyone, a held Bot is not asked whether it is its to answer (that judgement call is not made). Each wake a hold turns away is recorded in the work log (`wake.suppressed` in `work_events`), with what would have woken the Bot — your line, a Bot's line, a check-back, a plan call-back, a routine, a Continue — and which holds turned it away. A check-back turned away is set aside and comes back when the hold is lifted, and so is the report-back to a held Bot that opened the direct. Lines a running turn heard but ended without reading are put to the holds one by one: each one about held work is recorded as a wake turned away and stays in the transcript for after the lift, and the newest check-back among them is set aside to fire then; the rest open one more turn on the newest of them as usual, and only those ride along into it; a quiet plan's call-back is not kept, and the plan is looked at again the next time a turn on it ends and it goes quiet; a routine that comes due is recorded once, counts as not yet run, and runs once for its latest due time after the lift; Continue answers `409 held`, and the phone gets the same code. A turn a hold covers makes no call with an effect — each comes back `held` without running: the shell, writing or deleting files, sending a message, booking a check-back, asking you a question, changing a profile, skills, memories, routines, endpoints or MCP servers, and any MCP tool not marked read-only. Reading still works (reading files, listing folders, the `list_*` tools, `read_skill`, `end_turn`, and MCP tools their server marks `readOnlyHint`, such as looking up a video job). A call that was waiting on your approval does not run either if the hold came before you allowed it. The database adds a last layer: triggers on the turns table refuse to write a live turn a hold covers — including a row with no `mode` written by an older build that knows nothing of holds, since a stop comes before compatibility — and refuse moving a live turn into a hold's cover in place or changing its `mode`. The one turn they let through is the read-only one a line of yours opens (`mode = readonly`): a line of yours to a held Bot — in its direct, or naming it in a group — opens one beside whatever it was doing, bound to no plan, shown only the tools that change nothing, and opening on a line that says you stopped this work (with your words), that this turn can only answer, and, if you told it to go on, to say plainly that your stop still holds and is lifted on its receipt, not that it is going on. Its reply is posted as usual and wakes nobody; another Bot naming it meanwhile opens a turn beside it, not cutting it off. When such a turn ends without a word (an empty reply after reading files, or a bare `end_turn`), the app no longer answers in its place with a status line (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md): the app does not speak for a Bot). That line came in on 2026-10-03: after the group was stopped, "start over from scratch, scrap what came before" got a read-only turn that read files for nine hops and replied with nothing, and the group showed nothing at all.

**Your lines**: whether a line stops or goes on is the [reader](#line-reading)'s (a model's) to say (from 2026-10-09, [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md)). The control line's fixed rules (see Control line in CONTEXT) no longer read first or carry anything out; they keep two uses, neither of which carries anything out: the scope the words name (who "you" is, every Bot, this job), and whether a stop's receipt also offers to drop the job (「算了，停吧」). When the reader cannot read the line — none set, an error, a timeout, so the word lists read it — nothing is carried out and the line goes to the Bot as said; for a stop that cannot wait there is the composer's Stop button. The reading starts as the line arrives, not after the lines still being read before it, so a line read as nothing but a stop still goes ahead of them ([ADR 0063](adr/0063-edit-a-line-and-lines-in-order.md)'s order holds for the rest). The fixed rules used to read first: on the morning of 2026-10-09, 「继续」 in 视频导演's direct was taken for a pure go on and carried out before it was read or filed — lifting an old stop on 视频导演's 《一拳超人》 work from 16:59, which could not start again, while nothing moved on the MV and the line never reached the Bot.
- A stop: a line read as nothing but a stop ("you haven't stopped" too: a stop again, with its own receipt) goes nowhere else — no filing, no turn. It makes a hold per scope it names (source `user_text`, the line as its `source_message_id`), and the app posts a receipt in the conversation you said it in: a `system` line kept from the Bots, carrying `control: {kind: "receipt", verb: "stop", hold_ids, offer, scopes}` for its buttons (undo; stop every Bot; drop the job, when you said 算了 with it). Every fact in it comes from the holds' record: the turns ended, each with its plan, its conversation and its last step; the check-backs set aside; the plans parked; the other Bots still working in those plans; what is running now.
- A go on: a line read as one — 「继续」 alone, or with more in it like 「继续做第二集，……」 — is not carried out by the app and gets no receipt: the Bot that goes on says so. It is filed first, and then the stops that keep the Bots it is said to from the job it is filed under go: on the Bot, on its work in that job or on that ticket, on one of its turns; this conversation's own stop, and a job you stopped from here, only when the line is said to every Bot here (always, in a direct). A stop on any other job stays; only words that name everything (「所有 Bot」「全都」) lift every stop you made before the line, a Stop pressed anywhere included. A line filed under no job lifts only stops on the Bot as a whole. The line then wakes the Bot like any line, and the Bot goes on from it and answers you itself: said in your direct, it goes on there, and its stopped work on that job in another conversation does not open beside it (a Claude Code Bot starts a fresh session here and reads back the other one's work from the records). Stopped work of a lifted stop that the line did not wake the Bot to opens again where it was, on a note for that Bot alone (below). With nothing held, a go on is an ordinary line. A stop that still holds (one on everything; a whole group or job stopped elsewhere; the group or the job here, when the line is said to only some of the Bots) stays, for its buttons: the Bot can only read and answer the line, and is told the stop still holds, that it is lifted on its receipt, and not to say it is going on. A go on used to be carried out by the fixed rules, lifting the holds the words named — for a Bot, its holds on every job, its work a Stop held elsewhere included — with a receipt, or a status line when something wider held it; that is how 2026-10-09's 「继续」 landed on another job.
- Opening again: each turn a lifted stop ended (lifted by a line or a button) opens again in its conversation, on a system line with your words, its plan and ticket, the files it wrote and its last calls; the line says what happened and asks for nothing. It is the app's note to that Bot and, like a check-back's own line, for that Bot alone: only the turn it wakes, or is heard in, reads it, and the flow board still draws that turn picking up from the one the stop ended; the conversation, the phone, other Bots, the organizer, search, unread counts and the conversation list's last line never show it. In a direct with you it opens beside whatever the Bot took up there meanwhile; in a group or a Bot↔Bot direct, where a Bot works one turn at a time, a turn it already has there hears the line instead (a read-only one answering you does not). One the Bot has since taken up there does not open again. One another hold still covers does not either: it is handed to that hold (`held_over`) — to one that is not a Stop's, when there is one — and opens when that one is lifted. What you said to it while it was stopped got only a read-only answer, which cannot act: those lines are kept on the hold that held it (`effect.answered_lines`) and handed back when it is lifted — per Bot, conversation and job, the last of them opens the Bot's turn as it would have with nothing stopped, and the turn reads the others above it; a Bot whose stopped work opened again in that conversation reads them there; one another hold still covers keeps them on that hold until that one is lifted. Before, the read-only answer was the end of it: on 2026-10-04, 「片尾的 logo 再大一点」 ("make the logo at the end bigger") said under a stop got "I'll enlarge it and render again once you say continue", and the go on opened nothing (the job had been approved, with no stopped turn to open again), so the logo stayed small.
- "Has it stopped?", 「怎么还在跑」 ("why is it still running?"), "can it stop?" and a status question are lines for the Bot (from 2026-10-09): into its inbox, read at its next step without cutting it off, when it is working, or waking it when it is not, and it answers. The app used to answer them from what was running (`control.kind` `status`): each hold over what was asked about with the line that made it, what of it was running now, and a stop button when nothing was held; first, anything still running under one of those holds was ended and recorded as a violation (`effect.violations`, `hold.violation` in the work log) — a status line a button brings (see Buttons below) still makes that check, and there should never be any.
- A line read as a stop beside other words (「先停，把第三镜换成夜景」 "stop, and make shot 3 a night scene"), or as both (「今天先停，明天继续」 "stop for today, go on tomorrow"), is delivered as usual and carries `control: {kind: "possible_control", offer, scopes}` for the buttons; nothing in it is carried out: Stop on the first, Stop and Continue on the second; Continue is offered only while a stop it would lift holds what the line names, and once its stops are lifted the messenger stops showing it. A go on with more in it carries none and is a go on as above; one read as no control, or not read at all, carries none. 算了 / 不做了 / 取消 on its own no longer carries buttons: it is a line for the Bot.

**Stop on a turn's card**: once holds are on, it is a hold — in a direct it is the stop button in the composer (beside Send while its Bot has a turn going in that conversation; a direct has just this one stop, no menu and none on the reply's header). Stopping lives in the composer: a group's replies carry no Stop; to stop one Bot there, pick "Stop all of X’s work" in the stop menu beside Send, which stops only that Bot while the other Bots go on (see the stop menus below; ADR 0040 P2 overturns ADR 0016's no Stop in groups, and first put a Stop on each Bot's reply header, removed on 2026-10-06). A direct's stop button holds that Bot's work in the turn's plan (on the turn alone when it has none), source `user_button`, lifted by your next line (`lift_on_next_user_message`). The turn ends under it as above and the check-backs it booked are set aside instead of voided; no receipt goes into the conversation (from 2026-10-04, [ADR 0058](adr/0058-the-app-asks-only-when-it-needs-you.md)): it stopped the very turn you pressed it on, your next line lifts it, and the receipt with its Undo only repeated what you had just done. Where the turn stopped is recorded on the hold (`effect`), and the flow board marks the turn "Stopped". Your next line about that job — filed under that plan, or landing on it — lifts it before it wakes anyone, and the Bot goes on from that line; nothing opens again on a note, since Stop meant "not this". Said anywhere but its direct with you (in a group, say), only a line said to that Bot counts: one naming it (a reply to its line carries its name), naming everyone, or naming nobody in a group it is in; a line only to another Bot about the same job leaves the Stop, and the check-backs it set aside, where they are, so no check-back calls the Bot back into the job before you have said a word to it. Only a line said after the Stop counts: one sent just before, still being filed when you pressed it, does not. "Continue" on its own is such a line. A "continue" to that Bot said elsewhere lifts it too when it is filed under that job, and the Bot goes on from it where you said it; one filed under another job, or under none, does not (from 2026-10-09, above). A line that reads like control and carries the buttons, or a hold of any other scope, never lifts it that way.

**Buttons**: the buttons on a line the app read or wrote about your stops (`control`) are pressed once per line: the press is recorded in `control.acted`, the line then only says what was done, and pressing again (a double tap, a request the phone resends) does nothing. The lines the app writes itself — receipts, the status lines a button brings (`control.kind` `status`), the line after a restart — show in the conversation as the app's: the Deskfolk mark where a face would be and "Deskfolk" with an "App" tag, not the face and name of the Bot the line is filed under, and a screen reader reads them as "Message from the Deskfolk app"; their buttons stay under them. Its answer to a status question went on 2026-10-09; old ones still show this way. The buttons on a line of yours stay under your line.
- A stop's receipt: Undo lifts its holds by button and the work they ended starts again on a note (so did a Stop's, when a Stop still had a receipt) — undo means as if it had not been pressed; when the stop came from a line of yours, that line then goes on as any line (filed, waking whom it wakes, as if it had never been read as a stop, and not read for control again); when that line was said in your direct with a Bot, the work stopped there does not start again on a note, and the line is what reaches the Bot: a line of yours there always opens its turn, so starting the work again as well would have the Bot at it twice. In a group the line wakes only whom it names (with nobody named and nobody still at work, each Bot only makes a judgement on it), so the work stopped there starts again on a note all the same, and the line then goes to the Bots as any line of yours in the group would. "Stop every Bot" makes a hold on everything, its receipt in the same conversation. "Stop “…” too" makes a plan hold on the plan other Bots were still working in when the stop was made (the plans in `effect.working_beside`). "Only “…”" first makes, for each hold on a whole Bot in the receipt, a hold on that Bot's work in that plan (quoting the same line), then lifts the hold on the Bot: its work stopped elsewhere starts again on a note, its work in that plan is handed to the new hold, and the app posts another receipt saying what it lifted and what stays held. One button per plan, three at most; the same for "Stop … too". "Drop this job" records the receipt's holds in force as dropping the job (`action: cancel`): they hold as before, and lifting one reopens the job; really dropping a job waits for work items.
- "Only X goes on" on a status line or the receipt of a lift by button (only a button brings these lines now; a go on you say gets none): each wider hold in the way is first split into one per other Bot, quoting the line that made it — one on everything into a hold on each Bot; one on a plan into each Bot's work in that plan; one on a conversation into each Bot's work in the plans it parked and the conversation's current plan, plus a turn hold on each other Bot's turn it stopped off those plans — and then lifted along with X's own holds: X's work starts again on a note, the others' is handed to the split holds. "All go on" lifts everything in the way. Both post a receipt; the receipt names the holds these buttons are about in `held_ids`.
- On a line of yours: "Stop" makes the stop the line meant (source `user_button`, `source_message_id` the line, quoted by the receipt); "Drop" the same, recorded as dropping the job (no longer offered from 2026-10-09; it stays on lines like 算了 from before); "Continue" (offered only on a line read as both) still goes by the old rule: it lifts what it can over the scope the line names — for a Bot, its holds on every job — the stopped work opening again on a note, with a receipt, or posts a status line when it cannot; [ADR 0070](adr/0070-the-app-stays-out-of-the-conversation.md) has not changed this one yet. "Stop" on an older status line stops what was asked about.

**Stop menus and the list**: while a Bot is at work in a group — or in a Bot↔Bot direct opened from it (what the lead handed on, and on down), which the group's stop holds anyway — a stop menu sits beside Send: "Stop the work in this group", each Bot at work there, "Stop this job" and "Stop every Bot"; a choice a hold in force already covers is left out. The first three go, like a Stop on a card, with your next line about them (`lift_on_next_user_message`), and the Bots go on from that line with no note: for the group, any line you say in it afterwards; for a Bot, a line said to it, wherever (as for a Stop: anything in its direct, in a group naming it, everyone, or nobody); for the job, a line that lands on it. "@X continue" in the group is such a line: the group's hold goes, only X's stopped work starts again on a note, and the other Bots' stopped work stays stopped, as if you had said a line to X alone. A line naming nobody is said to the whole group: it wakes whom it always wakes (the lead, when there is one), and the work the lifted holds stopped that it does not reach — a Bot's part done in its direct with the lead, say — starts again too, on a note with your words, and gets your line as an item of its inbox to answer for. Before, such a line woke only the lead: on the 2026-10-04 walkthrough the group was stopped while 文案 wrote slogans in its direct with 设计师, and after 「宣传语改成英文的，海报改横版」 it stayed stopped, never heard the line, and was called back by the supervisor three minutes later to "answer" the old request. The receipt's last line says so by scope: "Say anything more in this group and the stop is over; the Bots you say it to go on from it." or "Say anything more to it and it goes on from what you say."; a stop on every Bot's: "Say anything to a Bot and the stop is over; it goes on from what you say." Before, a new instruction after the stop ("start over") got only a read-only answer, and the banner stayed up until you lifted it by hand long after the Bots had stopped. "Stop every Bot" works the same way since 2026-10-10 (ADR 0081): a line to any Bot ends it. A direct has no such menu, only the Stop above: it stops that turn and your next line lifts it; the menu bar's "Stop everything", the flow board's "Stop this job" and a typed "stop" are stops for now too — no stop waits for you to lift it any more. The flow board's title row offers "Stop this job" and "Stop every Bot". The tools menu has no stop (its "Stop everything" went on 2026-10-11, a repeat of "Stop every Bot" in the stop menus and on the flow board); the menu bar's "Stop everything" makes a hold on everything — on the turns that would start, not only those running, so it can be pressed with no Bot at work — and against a daemon that has no holds yet stops the latest live turn in a direct as before. A stop from a menu goes through `POST /v1/holds` with the menu's conversation, and its receipt goes there when you are in it. A lift the daemon refuses says "That did not work; try again" where it was pressed; with the daemon out of reach these buttons show but cannot be pressed. The stops you dropped a job with by a button, which no line ends, are listed above the conversation list: the scope, the time, "Dropped", each with a Lift (by button — going on; see API); a stop for now is not listed, and no row is marked (since 2026-10-10, ADR 0081). The flow board lists under its title the plan's own parking and the holds over its job that wait to be lifted — on a Bot's work in it, on its conversation, on everything — each with a Lift. When a hold is made or lifted, an open flow board reads its plan again, so the next edit starts from the version the hold wrote and is not refused with 409; on a 409 all the same it reads the plan again as before. The plan's footer and history mark the versions a hold wrote as "by a stop" (a revision's `cause: "hold"`, the plan's `revision_cause`). All of this needs a daemon at the holds level: it appears only when the snapshot has `holds`.

**At boot**: once the engine level has reached holds, every boot checks again. A plan parked before holds existed — status and spec's status both parked, which is how you or the organizer stopped one — becomes a plan hold of source `legacy`, so from then on only you can put it back in progress; a plan only pushed out of the current slot by a newer one (its spec still says In progress) is left alone. Then any plan a hold covers that is not parked, and any check-back it covers that is not set aside, is put right, and daemon.log says so.

**API**: `GET /v1/holds` (holds in force by default, `?status=all` for lifted ones too, newest first), `GET /v1/holds/:id`, `POST /v1/holds` (a stop you make from a button or a menu, recorded as `user_button`; the turns it covers end at once, as above), `POST /v1/holds/:id/lift` (the turns it ended open again as with a go on, a Stop's excepted; one already lifted comes back unchanged), `POST /v1/messages/:id/control` (the buttons above: `{action, task_id?}`, `task_id` naming the plan for "Stop … too" and "Only …"; returns `{made, lifted}`; 422 for a line with no buttons or without that one); the phone can do the same. `POST /v1/holds` takes an optional `session_id`: the receipt goes to that conversation when you are in it. The snapshot's `holds` are the holds in force, newest first, absent before the holds level; a hold's `plan_title` is the title of the plan it names. Parking a plan with `PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/spec` ends what runs in it the same way. `POST /v1/turns/stop` is the Stop above. A line the app read or wrote about your stops carries `control` (`MessageControl`). Making a hold, lifting it and recording what it did each reach clients as a `hold.upsert` event. A plan's `held_by` is as of when it was read: a hold that changes nothing on the plan's row, such as one on everything, sends only `hold.upsert` when it is made and when it is lifted, so a client that shows `held_by` reads the plan again on that event.

<a id="terminal"></a>
## Terminal

A shell session you open yourself on this Mac, held by the daemon. It has the same rights as Terminal.app: its cwd starts in the workspace by default, but it is not confined to the workspace and needs no approval. Approval governs Bots' dangerous actions, and you don't need to ask yourself for permission. It is for running your own work, including processes that stay up, such as starting a project. Closing the window doesn't stop it. Which directory it is in is whatever the shell itself reports (zsh does this out of the box, once before each prompt): after a `cd` the tab name follows; if it has never reported, it is the directory it was opened in. When you quit or the daemon itself stops, the processes inside stop with it, but its directory and what was on screen are kept, and the next launch opens a new one in that directory. Only "End session" forgets it. The screen lives in the daemon too: when you reattach, you get it as it is now, full-screen programs included. Questions a program asks the terminal are answered only by the daemon, never by panes, so even when several panes watch one session, each question is answered once. The shell's environment is set up the way Terminal.app sets it up, but only an allowlist gets through; the daemon's other environment variables (including keys) are not passed in. When the desktop and a phone watch one session at the same time, its rows and columns follow whichever end you most recently looked at or typed in. Paired devices have the same rights: you can see it and type into it on your phone. Bots can't see it, can't use it, and don't know it exists. On the desktop, one terminal tab is one session: opening a new terminal tab starts a new shell, and a tab no longer holds a row of sessions to switch between. Closing a tab only removes it from the window; the processes keep running. Sessions not shown in any tab can be brought back with "Reattach" from "+" or from an empty pane. Phones and narrow screens have no panes, and neither does a device on a remote connection at any width (a tablet included); all sessions are gathered on the Terminal page as its tabs. To stop one, you must explicitly choose "End session" and confirm. The daemon's session list is the real one; sessions opened in the same folder are numbered in the order they were opened, and they carry the same names in desktop tabs and on the phone.

<a id="remote-screen"></a>
## Remote screen

A paired device sees this Mac's screen and operates it under Tools → Mac screen. The picture and input both come from macOS's own Screen Sharing (local port 5900): Deskfolk captures nothing and injects nothing, so it needs no Screen Recording or Accessibility permission, and no update takes one away. The daemon only ever connects to that one local port; this is not a tunnel that can be pointed elsewhere.

**The switch**: off by default, and turned on only in the Mac window's Settings → Remote access; a paired device cannot change it. Turning it off ends a connection in progress there and then. Screen Sharing itself is turned on in System Settings → General → Sharing; the card says whether it is on right now, and one click opens that pane when it is not. While the remote screen is off, the phone page says where to turn it on.

**Signing in**: Screen Sharing asks for this Mac's account name and login password. You type them on the phone once; after they get in, the phone keeps them (where it keeps its pairing keys) and signs in by itself next time, unless you untick Remember on the sign-in form. When Screen Sharing refuses them the phone forgets them and asks again, and a new pairing or another Mac never uses them. The cost, stated: whoever can read this phone's pairing keys can already use your terminal; the kept password also lets them into the Mac's screen and sudo. That is what not typing it each time costs. It works at the lock screen too, where you can type the password to unlock: the lock screen turns the display off after half a minute idle, and Screen Sharing captures a display that is off as solid black, so the Mac turns its display on when a device connects, keeps it on while one is connected, and lets go when the last session ends. It does not work with the lid shut and no external display, asleep, or after a restart before anyone has logged in: the daemon runs per user.

**Two routes**: the phone tries a direct connection first — a WebRTC data channel whose non-trickle offer and answer travel over the encrypted remote link, so the direct peer is still this paired device; on the Mac `real-bot-rtc` takes it and connects it to Screen Sharing. The same network, IPv6, or STUN / TURN servers named in the Mac's settings let the two find each other; none are set by default and no third-party server is used. A phone with no candidate address at all goes straight to the relay; one that has some but has not connected within 8 seconds switches to it too: the daemon connects to Screen Sharing itself and the bytes ride type 9 frames on the remote link, neither side running more than 48 KiB ahead of what the other acknowledged. Over the relay the relay's bandwidth budget applies, and a full Retina refresh is slow. The page's top bar says "Direct" or "Via relay".

**Using it**: a tap is a left click, a two-finger tap a right click, a two-finger drag scrolls. The row at the bottom adds what a soft keyboard lacks: ⌘ ⌥ ⌃ ⇧ apply to the next key, plus esc, ⇥, the arrows and a keyboard toggle; text typed on the soft keyboard goes over character by character, Backspace and Return included. A pinch zooms the picture on the phone, never the Mac: the point between your fingers stays put, up to one Mac pixel across two of the phone's. Zoomed in, two fingers move the view instead of scrolling the Mac; one finger still clicks and drags on the Mac. "Actual size" in the header jumps to one Mac pixel per phone pixel, "Fit to screen" back to the whole page.

**Trackpad mode**: "Trackpad" in the header makes the whole screen a trackpad (the black bars above and below the picture included) and draws a pointer on the picture; the phone remembers the choice. Sliding one finger moves the pointer by how far the finger travels, not to where it lands: slowly, it moves less than the finger's travel across the picture, for fine positioning; quickly, it crosses the whole screen in one go. A tap clicks where the pointer is, a two-finger tap right-clicks, two fingers slide to scroll; holding still for about half a second and then sliding drags with the left button down, shown by a ring at the pointer's tip, until the finger lifts. A pinch still zooms the picture on the phone. Zoomed in, the pointer always stays where you can see it and the view follows it to the edge; at the picture's own edges there is no margin, so the menu bar and the screen's corners stay reachable. In this mode "Actual size" zooms around the pointer. The pointer starts in the middle of the screen, or where the last tap was, and the Mac's pointer jumps to it at the first slide or tap. noVNC has no way to put the pointer anywhere but under a finger, and its gestures need 50 pixels of travel before a slide counts as a drag, so in this mode the page reads the touches itself and writes RFB pointer events straight into the same channel (after an ARD sign-in the stream is plain RFB).

**Smooth mode**: "Smooth" in the header has the Mac lower its resolution while you are connected; the phone remembers the choice. Screen Sharing sends every pixel of the framebuffer: a 16" MacBook Pro at "More Space" is 4112×2658, a full-screen change with a photo wallpaper and one window is about 7.5 MB, and the phone decompresses it in JavaScript; at 2336×1510 it is about 2.8 MB, and decoding takes about a third of the time. The mode chosen is the smallest one on the main display with one pixel per point, the current aspect ratio and at least the current size in points, so no window has to shrink; the Mac's own screen just looks soft meanwhile. A screen already at one pixel per point is left alone, and the page says so. Pressing the switch reconnects the page: when the session starts, the daemon has `real-bot-rtc display-hold` switch the mode before it answers the phone, so Screen Sharing announces the new size when the phone connects; switching it off likewise restores first and answers after. Several devices share one lowering; it is restored 3 seconds after the last session that wanted it ends (room for a reconnect), and at once when you disconnect from the Mac window or turn the remote screen off. The mode is set per process (`kCGConfigureForAppOnly`): however the helper ends (the daemon closing it, the daemon dying, a crash), macOS puts the previous mode back, and on a clean end it restores first and says so. The Mac window's remote screen card says what the screen is lowered to.

**Who is on, and how it ends**: while a device is connected, the Mac window's settings card names it and says whether it is direct or via the relay, with a Disconnect button. Revoking that device, turning the switch off, the remote link dropping, the phone page going to the background, or the Mac not hearing the page's keepalive for 45 seconds all end the connection; coming back to the page reconnects.

**Windows**: Windows has no screen sharing of its own, so the picture and input come from a VNC server the user installs (TightVNC is the one to use, registered as a system service and allowing loopback connections only); the daemon still connects only to local port 5900 ([ADR 0059](adr/0059-windows-remote-access-and-screen.md)). The phone learns over the remote link that it reached Windows: the page is called "Computer screen", signing in takes only the VNC server's password, the key row reads Ctrl, Alt, Win and Shift, Ctrl+Alt+Del sits in the top bar where Smooth would be, and there is no smooth mode (TightVNC sends JPEG, small already). While a device is connected, `real-bot-rtc stay-awake` holds off the display and sleep timers, as `caffeinate` does on a Mac. While no VNC server answers, the computer window's card lists the steps to install and configure one, and its button opens TightVNC's download page. Not yet tried on a real Windows PC.

**Interfaces**: the phone calls `POST /remote/screen/start | offer | tunnel | status | stop` over the remote link; these exist only there and loopback cannot reach them. The phone shows the entry only when the `/remote/features` answer carries `screen: "rfb-v1"`, and `host` says whether it is a Mac or Windows. The Mac window calls `GET` / `PUT /v1/remote/screen` (the switch and ICE servers) and `POST /v1/remote/screen/disconnect` over the local API; none of these is on the remote allowlist. Protocol details: [remote protocol](remote-protocol.md#remote-screen); the trade-offs: [ADR 0056](adr/0056-remote-screen.md). Bots cannot use it.

<a id="interrupted"></a>
## Interrupted

When the daemon restarts, every turn in progress is marked interrupted, and that line goes into the conversation the turn ran in. A long job's turns mostly run in Bot↔Bot directs you are not in: on 2026-09-26 a video job cut off by a restart said so only in such a direct, and sat for 7.6 hours until you found it and pressed Continue. So after a restart, as soon as the engine is up and before the scheduler's first tick (so a check-back or routine that fell due while the daemon was down cannot wake a Bot first), each job the restart cut off that will not go on by itself gets one line. Work the [supervisor](#supervisor) picks up on its own from level 4 (at once after a deliberate stop; once after a minute of steady running after a crash; once after five minutes without starting again after a development restart), and work a stop of yours holds, gets no line and no notification: each turn's own 「中断」 line says enough, and their own notifications are voided and marked read (from 2026-10-04; until then every development restart posted a line and a notification, and on the morning of 2026-10-04 you pressed Leave it on both of the 《一拳超人》 job's lines, the second time setting aside work that would have gone on by itself a minute later). A line goes up for levels 3 and below, a last step with no known outcome, development restarts within five minutes of each other, and turns on no job (the supervisor does not take those up); work that was to go on by itself but was cut off again by another start before its minute (and so no longer will) is told by that later start, saying it was cut by an earlier development restart and the daemon started again before it could pick up. The line goes in the conversation the job belongs to where you are: a plan's turns go to where you last spoke about the plan (as the supervisor's own notices do, while you are still in it), else to the plan's conversation; when you are not in it, to the turn's own conversation, else the conversation up the line that opened the Bot↔Bot direct, else your direct with the Bot. Turns of one job in one place (a plan cut off in two Bot↔Bot directs, say) make one line. It used to go to the plan's conversation first: on 2026-10-04 a job opened in a group and taken up that morning in your direct with the video director was told of in the group at both restarts, while the 「中断」 lines were in the direct beside your last words. It is the app's system line, kept out of every Bot's context and shown as the app's own (see the buttons under [Hold](#hold)):
- **Why it restarted**: this is a development run (`bun --watch` / `--hot`, or `REAL_BOT_DEV=1`) — "The development daemon has restarted", however the last run ended: on every save `--watch` sends SIGTERM and restarts the process in place without waiting for it, and the stop that SIGTERM starts writes "stopped on purpose" first, so there that mark cannot tell a code change from a quit; otherwise, the last run was stopped on purpose (quit, update, a restart you asked for, which wrote `last_shutdown = clean`) — "The daemon was stopped and has started again"; otherwise "The daemon quit unexpectedly and has started again".
- **Where it stopped**: each turn's Bot, its ticket (or the plan, with none), where it ran when that is not this conversation, and its last step (the last command or MCP call in its record).
- **What now**: up to level 3 nothing picks up on its own; from level 4 the line says what the [supervisor](#supervisor) will do (after a deliberate stop, go on at once; after an unexpected exit, once after a minute of steady running; a development run, once after five minutes without starting again, however many restarts came before; a last step that was an external call with no known outcome, or work a stop covers, does not go on by itself), naming each turn's arrangement when they differ — and going on by itself is exactly the Continue below. Continue continues each turn of the job the way its own interrupted line's Continue would: a new turn whose trigger is that line, under your stops like any other — while a stop holds all of those turns the press does not count, and after you lift it you press again; while it holds only some, the rest go on, the row says how many went on and how many the stop still holds, and the line stays unanswered so that pressing again after the lift continues those; a turn already continued, one whose Bot is already at work there, or whose conversation or Bot is gone is skipped, and when every turn is already continued the press counts and does nothing more. Not now keeps it as it is, and the interrupted lines' own Continue comes back, to pick it up later from there (until 2026-10-04 the button read Leave it, in Chinese 「不续」, which read as closing the notice). Each line is pressed once.
- **Notification**: with a line, one per job (`interrupted`); the ones the turns' own interrupted lines made are voided, and in the same conversation an interrupted line the notice covers offers no Continue of its own until you press one of the notice's buttons. With none, the turns' own notifications are voided and marked read (`goes_on_by_itself`): no system banner, nothing on the Dock badge.

Which turns a restart cut off: whenever the daemon starts ending every turn at once (a quit, a forced drain before an update or a remote restart, the daemon stopping itself), and when a boot tidies up the turns the last run left live, it notes the turns live at that moment; the next boot takes only those still interrupted with their interrupted line not yet continued. Only the boot right after takes the record: when another run opened the database in between without taking it (it died during boot), or an older copy of the app that knows nothing of the record ran and opened turns, the record is stale and is dropped, rather than telling of that earlier shutdown under this restart's cause; those turns keep their own interrupted lines and notifications. An error while writing these lines only adds a daemon.log line, and the daemon starts all the same. A turn the stuck-turn sweep interrupted is not counted; a forced drain you call off is forgotten when you call it off. Every boot writes `daemon.restart` to the work log (the cause and the turns cut off), and one daemon.log line when anything was cut off. The notice itself does not depend on the engine level; going on by itself by cause is level 4's ([supervisor](#supervisor)), which checks the effect ledger first and does not go on after a last step with no known outcome, or a last step that submitted a review or a render would be paid for twice. Once every line has been continued (by the supervisor or by you), the notice reads as continued and its notification is resolved. Continue and Not now go through `POST /v1/messages/:id/control` (`{action: "resume" | "leave"}`, on the phone too); the line's `control` is `{kind: "restart", cause, notes, offer, acted?}`, `notes` being the turns' interrupted lines; when only some went on, the answer carries `partial: {continued, held}`.

<a id="completion"></a>
## Completion

Every hop's request carries `max_tokens`: the model's `max_output` in its endpoint's list, or 32,768 when unset, reasoning tokens included. Without a cap, on 2026-09-29 three hops each wrote one sentence up to the endpoint's default of 128,000 tokens (one of them for 17 minutes, the same sentence 3,457 times), and each was posted as the turn's closing line; in a Bot↔Bot direct it also woke the other Bot, whose next hop read the whole thing again. 32,768 comes from the spend records: a normal hop's output peaked a little over 30,000, and the only hops past 32,768 were those three repetitions, so anything lower would cut normal hops that write a large file. An endpoint that rejects `max_tokens` with a 400 naming `max_completion_tokens` instead (as OpenAI's reasoning models do) gets the request again at once with that field, and that model gets it from then on. A model that writes less than that (DeepSeek's deepseek-chat at most 8,192, OpenAI's gpt-4o at most 16,384) has its endpoint refuse the cap with a 400: when the error states the model's own limit, the request goes again at once with that number; when it states none, without a cap; and that model gets the same from then on. These requests went through before hops had a cap, so a refusal like this must not fail the turn, and the cap cannot be changed in Settings. When the prompt and the cap together are over the model's context (the error says "32768 in the completion", or names the cap while talking about the context length, as vLLM does), the request goes again without a cap, that time only: the next hop sends the cap first again.

While a reply streams, the app reads only its prose, not tool arguments, code blocks or tables: over the last 6,000 characters it splits sentences (at full stops, question and exclamation marks, semicolons, ellipses and line breaks, and at an English full stop followed by a space) and compares each by its letters and digits alone, ignoring case; digits still count ("shot 11" and "shot 12" are two sentences). When the same sentence (8 characters or more) comes a fifth time in a row, or fewer than a fifth of at least 20 recent sentences are distinct, the completion is cut off on the spot as a repetition, usually within seconds. "In a row" means nothing new came between two copies, only sentences seen before: a loop has nothing between its copies but more of itself, while a per-item verdict list ("Shot 03: passed. The frame is stable, no rework needed." on every line) has each item's own name between two copies of the comment, and is not a repetition. The share of distinct sentences counts sentences of any length, so twenty identical lines of "- [x] done" that never say which item each is still count as a repetition. A finished reply is checked by the same rules again before it is posted.

These completions have failed and are not replies:
- **Repetition**: as above.
- **Refusal**: the endpoint's finish reason is `content_filter` or one of Gemini's like `SAFETY`; or the whole reply is at most 300 characters with no tool call and is a canned refusal (speaking as a language model and saying it cannot, or only "I can't help with that" or "抱歉，我无法协助处理这个请求"). A Bot explaining which step it cannot do and why is not a refusal. Gemini refusals had gone out as a Bot's message twice before this.
- **Incomplete**: the connection closed before the reply was finished (with neither a finish reason nor `[DONE]`; this is what the endpoint or a proxy in between closing the connection partway looks like), the finish reason said it stopped to call tools and no whole tool call arrived, or a tool call's arguments were not whole JSON. The client first tries the same request up to three times, and only when it is still incomplete does it count as failed.
- **Overtime**: one request may take this long from being sent to the end of its stream: the larger of 10 minutes and `max_output` ÷ the model's measured 10th-percentile speed × 1.5. The speed is `stream_tps_p10` in the list; unmeasured, it is 10 minutes (a model server on this computer or network is taken to write 10 tokens a second until measured; see "Model servers on this computer or network" below). Time waiting for a free slot at the endpoint, time running tools and time asleep with the lid shut do not count.

A failed completion posts nothing, stays out of the loop and wakes nobody. The same model gets one more hop with a note saying why (for a repetition: do not restate it or write a sign-off, do the next step by calling the tool directly); if that fails too, the turn ends as failed, with a "This turn did not finish: …" line in the session and, when you are in it, a failure notification. Any normal hop in between earns the next failure another go. Not reaching the endpoint, a busy endpoint, an endpoint error and a stream that goes quiet partway (the connection stays open but no next piece arrives) are not in this list: the client has already resent the same request, and the turn ends at once. Going quiet only shows once the idle time (3 minutes) has run out, so three tries already take 9 minutes and one more hop would double that; a connection closed partway shows within seconds, so it counts as incomplete, and one more hop costs little. When a stream goes quiet or breaks off partway, its first half used to be posted as the reply or have its tool calls run; now none of it is used.

Finish reasons are read regardless of case, including Gemini's own ones that a proxy passes through as they are: `STOP` is `stop`, and `max_tokens`, `MAX_TOKENS` and Mistral's `model_length` all read as `length`. An empty finish reason is none at all: some endpoints send `""` on every chunk until the last. A finish reason the client has no name for (such as `eos`) means the model says it is done, so the stream is whole: it is not resent as half a stream, and the reply is judged by the rules above like any other. `error` is the exception: OpenRouter ends a stream with it when the model behind it failed partway, so what came is only half, and it counts as incomplete. A stream that goes quiet after its finish reason (the endpoint neither sends usage and `[DONE]` nor closes) is taken as the reply once the idle time runs out: the model has finished, and only the endpoint's last packets are missing.

A reply cut off at the output cap (finish reason `length`) without repeating is carried on for one hop: the cut part is not posted but stays in the loop to carry on from, with a note to do the next step by calling the tool directly and to put long content in files, written in parts; when the cut falls inside a tool call's arguments, that call is not run and the note asks for it in parts. If the carrying-on hop reaches the cap too, the turn ends as failed for hitting the output limit.

Streamed output no longer counts as the turn making progress: it used to refresh the turn's last activity as each piece arrived, so a hop could write for any length of time without looking stuck. A completion is now bounded by its own time limit, and the stale sweep (20 minutes without any activity) covers only the time outside completions.

`max_output` does not show in Settings; `stream_tps_p10` is written by **Test** in a model's attributes, and `context_window` (the context window, in tokens) can be seen and changed there. A Bot can change `context_window`, `input_image` and `max_output` with `update_endpoint`, time a model with `measure_model`, and change the default endpoint, the built-in models (`builtin_models`; an endpoint's model or none only) and the model ladder with `update_model_settings`; all run without approval ([ADR 0014](adr/0014-bot-catalog-tools.md)). Saving the list from Settings again keeps whichever of these it leaves out, and only a `null` sent through the API clears one (emptying the window field sends `null`). When one is invalid in the stored list (say 0, or a string), only that value is ignored: the model stays in the list, and the Bots using it are unaffected.

**Anthropic-compatible endpoints.** With an endpoint's format set to Anthropic-compatible ([ADR 0066](adr/0066-anthropic-format-endpoints.md)) the rules of this section hold as they are; only what goes over the wire differs:

- **Address and key**: requests go to `/v1/messages` under the base URL, or only `/messages` when the base already ends in `/v1`. So the address to give is the kind Claude Code takes as `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` (`https://api.anthropic.com`, `https://api.deepseek.com/anthropic`). The key goes in `x-api-key`, with `anthropic-version: 2023-06-01`; refused with a 401 or 403, it is sent once more as `Authorization: Bearer` (some vendors and proxies take only that), and if that goes through the endpoint gets it that way from then on. Refused both ways, the key is wrong.
- **Messages**: inside, the app keeps the loop in Chat Completions' shape and converts it on the way out: every system message joins the top-level `system`; tool results become `tool_result` blocks in a user turn, ahead of any text in it; neighbouring messages of one role merge into one turn; a history that opens with the Bot's own line gets a user turn of "…" before it; tool call ids keep only letters, digits, `_` and `-` (the loop may hold ids an OpenAI-compatible endpoint gave); an image's data URI becomes a base64 image block.
- **Thinking levels**: first in the form of Anthropic's newer models, `thinking: {type: "adaptive"}` with `output_config.effort` (`none` sends only `effort: low`: the newest models cannot stop thinking, and older ones without a `thinking` field do not think anyway). A 400 that names thinking, effort or budget_tokens steps down to `thinking: {type: "enabled", budget_tokens}` (low 2,048, medium 8,192, high 16,384, at most half the output cap, none when that is under 1,024); refused again, no thinking fields at all. Once a request in the stepped-down form goes through, that model on that endpoint keeps it; a step that was refused anyway is not kept. A 400 about thinking blocks in the history ("Expected `thinking` … but found `tool_use`", a signature that does not check out) is not a refusal of the fields and steps nothing down. A judgement call whose cap has no room for thinking (256 or 512 tokens) gets the effort alone in the new form, and no budget in the old one.
- **Thinking goes with the tool calls**: when the model thought before calling tools, its thinking blocks (signatures included) stay in the turn's loop with those calls, unchanged, and go back with the next hop, which would be refused without them. They go back only to the endpoint and model that wrote them: a hop that moved to another model (stepping up the ladder, or to one that sees pictures) goes without them.
- **The cap**: this format needs `max_tokens` on every request, so there is no "send it again without a cap". An error saying the context is full ("190000 + 32768 > 200000": used, cap, context) is sent again once with what room is left; one that states the model's own limit is handled as above, that number from then on. Still refused with the cap out of the way because the prompt itself is too long ("prompt is too long"), the context is full, not a bare refusal; a stream stopped at `model_context_window_exceeded` is a full context too (as is Mistral's `model_length`), not a reply cut at the output cap to carry on from. Both compact the loop first (see [Context window](#context-window)).
- **Judgement calls** carry no `temperature: 0`: Anthropic's newer models refuse any sampling setting.
- **Cache**: one cache mark at the end of the system prompt, and for a hop another at the end of the loop, so the next hop reads up to there from the cache. Anthropic caches nothing unmarked; proxies that cache on their own ignore the marks.
- **Usage**: tokens read from and written to the cache both count as input, and those read count as cached too, as Chat Completions counts them; spend is estimated from the list's rates as before.
- **Finish reasons**: `end_turn` is a normal end, `tool_use` asks for tools, `max_tokens` is the cap, `refusal` is a refusal. An `error` event in the stream (`overloaded_error`, most often) makes the attempt unfinished, tried again as above.
- **The model list** comes from `…/v1/models?limit=1000`, the key sent as above. Many compatible endpoints have no such route; then type the names in.

**Model servers on this computer or network.** An endpoint whose address is loopback, `localhost`, `*.local` or a private address (10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16, link-local) is taken as a model server on this computer or network (Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp's `llama-server`), and these rules come on top of this section's ([ADR 0067](adr/0067-local-model-servers.md)):

- **The key** is optional. Without one the endpoint is still picked to run on and completes the wizard, and requests carry no key header; when a Bot adds such an endpoint, the key field on the approval card may stay empty.
- **Cut prompts**: such a server does not refuse a prompt it cannot hold; it cuts the start and answers anyway (Ollama 0.40 keeps the last half once over its window, with HTTP 200). So after a local request the app compares the bytes sent with the tokens the server reports reading: over 6.5 bytes a token, or over 1.6 times what the model read its earlier whole requests (of 16 KB or more) at, the prompt was cut (requests under 16 KB are not judged, and pictures are not counted); so was it when, within one conversation (one Bot in one session), a prompt longer than the one before was read as fewer tokens — Ollama drops the oldest messages to fit once a prompt is just over its window, and the reading moves only a few percent (not judged when the number of pictures changed). That step is dropped and not sent again as it was: when the turn's loop has something to condense, it is compacted into a summary first and the step goes again (see [Context window](#context-window)); when it cannot be, or still does not fit, the turn fails with "The prompt is larger than the model's context window", followed by about how many tokens the step was, how many the endpoint read and the server's window; that window is also recorded on the model's entry. When the entry already has a window and the estimate is more than a tenth over it, the step is not sent at all. A 400 from any endpoint (an Anthropic-compatible one too) whose text says the prompt is over the context reads the same way.
- **Window and abilities**: fetching such an endpoint's model list also asks the server for each model's window (Ollama: the loaded window, else the Modelfile's `num_ctx`, else the model's limit; LM Studio and llama.cpp alike), whether it takes pictures and whether it calls tools, and fills them into the model's attributes (never over what you typed); a model that cannot call tools is tagged "No tools" in the list.
- **Time limits**: up to 15 minutes for the first token (loading the model, queueing behind another request, reading tens of thousands of tokens) and 5 minutes idle; one streamed request at a time per server, the rest queued in the app without a clock; short calls nobody waits on get at least 10 minutes, while a reading keeps its 20 seconds and falls back to the word lists.
- **Short calls do not think**: organizing, judgements, readings and other short calls that name no thinking level send `reasoning_effort: "none"`; otherwise a reasoning model spends all 256 tokens thinking and answers nothing (measured on qwen3:8b). A model that refuses the value is asked again without it, and remembered.
- **Thinking is not the reply**: a leading `<think>…</think>` is taken out of the reply text (on every endpoint), and a lone `</think>` takes everything before it along; a local model's separate thinking also goes through the repeat watch, on the same-long-sentence rule only.
- **Speed test**: **Test** in a model's attributes sends two short requests, timing the first token and the tokens per second, and checks whether it calls a tool it is given; 60% of the speed is recorded as `stream_tps_p10`. It does not queue behind a Bot's step in the app, but a server that handles one request at a time makes it wait for that step.

<a id="artifact"></a>
## Artifact

A workspace-relative path, or an `http`/`https` link, referenced in a message. The file itself lives in the workspace (or at the remote URL). Files inside the workspace written during a turn appear in its final message automatically; not those the turn deleted again, nor anything in a folder it deleted — on 2026-10-04's real-model run a Bot wrote slogans.md at the workspace root, wrote it again in its ticket's folder and deleted the first, and the group showed two slogans.md bubbles, one of them gone. Opening one shows it in the preview panel to the right of the chat (image / audio and video / PDF / text / single-file HTML / Word / Excel / PowerPoint); other kinds (subtitles, archives…) state that preview is not supported: the app window on this machine offers "Open with default app", and phones and browsers offer a download button. Every file opened remotely can be downloaded: on a phone from the right end of the preview's top bar, on wide screens from the row of buttons above the file, and on the flow board's file cards from the card header. Opening remotely, downloading and sending attachments have no file size limit; on desktop this preview can also be dragged out into a pane of its own. Opened from a message, the tree on the left of the panel lists only the files **that message** names — its attachments and the paths its text points to — not what other messages in the job referenced. Opened from a file card on the flow board, it lists every path **this job** has referenced — not every file in the work dir; "associated" means it has been referenced. That tree is rooted at the work dir, paths this job referenced outside that directory sit flat beside it, and the ones this step itself references are marked. The panel shares one column with the chat, with a drag bar between them to change the width; when one message has several paths, or when you open the workspace, the left side is a file tree, and the split between tree and content can be dragged. Preview column and file tree widths you have dragged are still there after you quit and reopen. Text is edited with Monaco + Shiki (line numbers, find / replace via ⌘F, bracket and tag matching, heading folding, line wrap); the preview itself no longer has find, wrap, copy, save or close buttons. Markdown and single-file HTML show the rendered result by default; a floating button at the top center of the content, which does not scroll with the body, switches between preview and source. PDFs are rendered with the built-in pdf.js (paging, zoom, fit width / whole page, in-page find), not the system PDF viewer. UTF-8 text inside the workspace can be written back to the same path with Cmd+S / save; switching files or closing the panel with unsaved changes asks for confirmation first. On desktop the preview has no title bar of its own: the file name and close are on the workbench tab; narrow screens and phones keep a strip with back and the file name. A device on a remote connection has no workbench tabs either, whatever its width (a tablet included): there the preview opens as a full screen of its own with that same strip, not a column beside the chat — before 2026-10-08 that column offered no way to close it at all on such screens. Right-clicking any item in the file tree offers "Show in Finder" and "Open with default app", which take effect only in the app window on this machine. Files and folders in the tree (several at once when picked with ⌘ or Shift) can be dragged straight onto the composer, which lights up under the pointer; dropped, they wait above the input as attachments and go out with the next message by their workspace path — nothing is uploaded or copied into `inbox/`, the Bot reads them as "附件：path" lines, and pictures still reach the model as pixels. Esc during the drag puts them back. The file-drop session, read-only conversations and a composer that is still sending take nothing; there is no dragging on phones or touch screens. In a handoff, the path goes into the message to the other party; there is no separate kind. The kind is inferred from the extension at preview time. Artifacts inside the workspace can be annotated (see "Annotation").

<a id="annotation"></a>
## Annotation

A remark you write about one spot in an artifact. It is attached to a workspace-relative path, an anchor, and the message that delivered the artifact. It starts as a draft the Bot cannot see; drafts collect into a batch that is sent all at once, combined into one reply that quotes the delivering message and @-mentions the Bot that delivered it. Once sent it is "Pending", and a Bot or you mark it "Resolved"; a resolved one can be reopened. As soon as it is marked resolved it comes off the file — the box on an image, the dot in the code margin, the mark in Markdown, the box on a PDF page, the number on HTML, the marker on a timeline — so what stays on the file is what is still unresolved; the list keeps listing them all. The list has tabs for All / Pending / Resolved / Drafts, each with its count, and each state has one colour — Pending teal (as the marks drawn on the file), Resolved green, Drafts grey and dashed — on the tab's dot, the row's left edge and its state label; an empty tab shows the app's usual empty state, saying what would appear there. They are drawn back only when "Show resolved" is checked in the preview column, and only for that preview; clicking a resolved one in the list to locate it draws that one while it is located, and clicking it again to deselect takes it down; clicking its card in the conversation only opens the list at that item and does not draw it back on the file. When the file has changed and the anchor no longer matches, it is marked "Text changed" and its status stays the same; when the file is gone, it is marked "File gone". An annotation is not a snapshot of the artifact: it records only the quote, position and file hash at the time and does not keep the old file (when the selection is longer than the quote limit, it also records the whole span's length and fingerprint, used to tell whether that span is still there and whether it has changed). What it attaches to: a preview opened from a message attaches to that message and the Bot that delivered it; when the delivering message is in a Bot↔Bot direct you are not in, the batch goes to your direct with the delivering Bot, and the message records the origin. Only files inside the workspace can be annotated; link artifacts cannot. Each Bot that an annotation message wakes keeps the work dir of the turn in which it delivered the artifact (its most recent delivery in this batch), even if the session has been quiet long enough for that job to have closed — it is changing the same deliverable; this turn only works in that directory: it does not reopen that job or take the session's current plan's place, likewise when that job belongs to another session (it was delivered in a Bot↔Bot direct), and what each of the two sessions is carrying on with is unaffected. It is an ordinary follow-up message: it lands on the delivering turn and joins the correction chain, with no extra weight. A batch holds at most 50 annotations, and each body at most 2000 characters. In a group, one batch can go to several Bots at once: in the context, every annotation states which Bot it is for (the Bot that delivered that artifact, or "a deleted Bot" if it has been deleted), and `list_annotations` includes this too; each Bot handles only the ones for itself and leaves the ones for other Bots to those Bots, though it may mention them; ones for a deleted Bot have no one to take them, so the Bots this batch woke handle them. What a Bot can do: work through the ones for itself one by one; mark the ones it changed as resolved with `resolve_annotation`, with one sentence on how it changed them (any Bot can mark one — a Bot is not a permission boundary — and who marked it is recorded); check what is still unresolved with `list_annotations` (paths resolve the same way as in `read_file`, and a path outside the workspace is an error right away); and it gets a reminder when `read_file` reads a file that still has pending annotations. What a Bot cannot do: reopen, delete or create annotations. Clearing history or deleting a session deletes the annotations attached to those messages along with them; deleting a file does not delete its annotations. Anchor kinds: text / code line range, a selection in rendered Markdown, a box drawn on an image, a region on a PDF page, an HTML element, an audio / video time point.

<a id="context-window"></a>
## Context window

What the model actually sees in a turn: the profile, the catalog of enabled skills, the system instructions, the "MCP this turn" section, this Bot's memory, the situation block (in a group it has the present members, what each of the others does (their duties, cut short), who the user confirmed as the group's lead — told to the lead itself as its job: lay out with plan_items what others should make, then delegate on those tickets — live turns, who woke the Bot and the user's latest message; on 2026-10-04's real-model runs a lead that saw only the others' names wrote the slogans itself in two of four jobs, and laid the job out once it was told; every turn has this job's plan (Goal, Process, Progress), what you said in the job (the first 4 and the latest 6 lines), the requirements ledger's entries that hold for it, its acceptance checks, the ticket list, this turn's ticket, the files already handed over, the trace, and the Bot's own pending check-back; when no plan has been organized yet it falls back to the original request, and the first turn is marked "This is the first turn of this job"; all of it is read from rows already in the database, and the turn itself does not ask the model for a summary), the most recent stretch of the transcript (including the pixels of raster attachments, only the newest ones within a single budget; a user message with annotations expands, after its body, each annotation's id, which Bot it is for, path, position, quote or cropped image, and remark (page text and text you wrote is folded onto one line or indented line by line, so it cannot pose as the next item); cropped images and raster attachments spend the same budget and are sent to the model as pixels, and once the budget is used up, a cropped image leaves only the annotation's text), and this turn's tool loop. The full history exists only in the database, for you to browse and for search; it is not stuffed into the model wholesale. Judgement calls use a separate, shorter window without images. For an oversized tool result, the full JSON is first saved in the workspace, and the window holds only a bounded preview and full_result_path; the Bot can go on to extract the fields it needs or decode the file with the shell, and a truncated preview is not treated as the original result being lost.

**Compacting a turn that no longer fits** ([ADR 0068](adr/0068-compact-a-turn-that-outgrows-the-context.md)): every hop sends the turn's whole tool loop again, so a long turn can outgrow the model's context. The loop is compacted at two moments: when a hop is refused as over the context (any endpoint's 400 saying the prompt is over the context, a local server's cut or pre-send stop, a stream stopped at a full context), after which the hop goes again; and when the model's entry names a context window (local servers fill it in; for a cloud model you can set it in the model's attributes) and this turn's hops reported usage, before a hop estimated at 75% of the window by this turn's bytes per token. A cloud model without a window gets no token estimate and is compacted only after a refusal. What is compacted is the loop's older hops: it splits at one of the Bot's own lines; the newest hops stay word for word (at most a fifth of what fits, each call with its results and thinking), as do the notes and messages read in after the last hop; the rest is written out — each entry labelled as the Bot's words, a call, a tool's result, or a note or message, results cut shorter tier by tier, keeping their start and end, and the oldest left out when even that does not fit, the previous summary first — and sent with the line that started the turn to the turn's own model (or the one chosen for Compaction under Settings → Roles, at `low` thinking; the split is still sized by the turn's own model's window), without tools. Its words are the built-in prompt "Context compaction" (`call.compact`), yours to edit; the answer is the summary, which replaces those hops inside an app note (the originals are gone from the context; read files again for details; large tool results are whole under `tool-results/`), and the Bot carries on from it; a later compaction folds the earlier summary in. What fits is the window at this turn's bytes per token, or without a window the largest request this turn got through (a floor). Refused again before a hop goes through, or when the instructions, transcript and tool definitions alone take three quarters of what fits or less than 8,000 bytes could be condensed, or when the summary call gives nothing back, the turn fails with "The prompt is larger than the model's context window" as before. The summary call is billed under the turn in spend ("Context compaction"), and from level 8 the work log gets a `turn.compacted`; nothing is posted in the chat. Hops count as before: compacting does not reset the 160-hop limit. Claude Agent turns are compacted by Claude Code itself, not here.

<a id="built-in-prompt"></a>
## Built-in prompt

The fixed text the app sends models, one slot each (`apps/daemon/src/prompts/registry.ts`): every turn's System section (`turn.system`), the notes opening the Skills, Memory and MCP sections (`turn.skills` / `turn.memory` / `turn.mcp`), the Claude Agent preface (`agent.preface`), each tool's description (`tool.<name>`, not its parameters), and the app's own calls (`call.*`). Unedited, a slot is its default rendered for the engine level and the shell, word for word what it was before; your version replaces that rendered text whole. The Chinese-only ones (organizer, line readings, scribe, join judgement, composer suggestions) are used whatever the app's language.

- **Where**: Settings › Prompts (after MCP). It is in sections as Models is: every turn, Claude Agent, the app's own calls, and the tool descriptions. A wide window shows them as tabs under the search, one page each, every tab counting what is edited in it (in the warning colour when one is in conflict with a newer default); a phone lists them first, each saying how many prompts it holds, how many are edited and whether one is in conflict, and opens one as a page of its own, with Back going to that list first. One line a prompt: the title and what it is; the tool descriptions are a grid of tool names on their own page. A default carries no mark; an edited one says who edited it (you / which Bot / the app merging a newer default), a conflict with a newer default, and how many answers did not read; while any is edited, an All / Edited filter sits beside the search. The search stays on top and looks through every section: while it holds text or Edited is on, there are no tabs, and what it finds is listed by section in one list. The editor has three views — Text, Diff vs default and History — and keeps the one you are on when you switch language: the text fills the page with the placeholders in a line under it and the answer format folded. It saves a second after you stop (at once when you leave Text); the saves of one sitting fold into one change in the history, but typing that change back out is a change of its own, and what comes after starts another (a space added and deleted is two). Each comparison marks the characters that changed within a line, a lone space included. Saves are guarded by the revision you saw; if it changed elsewhere the latest is loaded and what you typed can be copied. Undo the latest change, restore any version, restore the default after a confirm. The phone can do the same.
- **The answer format is locked**: in the app's own calls the format code reads is not in your text; your text holds one `{format}` (exactly once) where it goes. The context compaction's answer (`call.compact`) is the summary itself and no code parses it, so it has no `{format}` and all of it is yours to edit. The seams and standard checks also take `{item}` and `{rules}`, the Claude Agent preface `{workspace}` and `{cwd}`; a missing, repeated or unknown placeholder is not saved. Join judgement and composer answers are read from the first `{`, so those may hold none once filled.
- **A Bot proposes**: `list_prompts` / `read_prompt` are always available (reading works under a hold too). `edit_prompt` changes text the way the retrospective changes a skill — replace a passage that occurs exactly once, add after one, append a paragraph; `reset_prompt` restores the default. Both are applied to the current text and checked first, then wait on an approval card (`prompt-edit`) listing what is taken out and put in, the Bot's reason, and how far the change reaches ("Reach: every Bot, every step" and the like); there is no Always allow for it. A lesson about one kind of work or a few Bots belongs in their own skills, not here. When you allow it, it is applied again to the text as it is then; one that no longer fits goes back to the Bot. Once allowed, the card offers Undo (while it is still the latest change) and Open in settings, which opens that prompt's editor at its History with this change opened.
- **A newer default**: when an update brings one, at open (and when the engine level rises) it is merged into your version line by line: a clean merge is written as the app's change and can be undone; one that does not merge leaves yours in force, marked "New default · conflict", for you to keep yours or take the new default. The boot log says what was done to each, and which are edited.
- **Answers that do not read**: an answer to one of the app's own calls that came back and could not be read (not a timeout, an endpoint error or one cut off at its cap) is logged as `prompt.parse_failed` with the prompt and revision; settings show the count since your edit, or over the last 7 days on a default.
- **Where things belong**: what every Bot should do goes into a built-in prompt; how one Bot works into its skills; a fact it learned is a memory; your requirements stay in the requirements ledger in your words. A Bot proposes such a change only when you ask it to analyze or improve something — there is no background tuning; set up a routine to do it periodically.

<a id="records"></a>
## Records

Bots can read what the app keeps on this machine, read-only, for the evidence behind an analysis or an improvement ([ADR 0065](adr/0065-bots-read-this-machines-records.md)):

- **Three tools**: `describe_data` lists tables and row counts with a line on the main ones, then a few ready-made queries (`recipes`), and a table's columns and types for `table`; `query_data` runs one read-only SQLite query, or a ready-made one by name with `recipe` — a single SELECT or WITH … SELECT, `?` bound to `params` in order, 100 rows by default and 1000 at most, long cells cut, blobs shown by size, stopped at 10 seconds, a wrong column answered with the named tables' columns; `read_data_log` reads the end of `daemon.log` (or the development build's `daemon-dev.stderr.log`), with grep. None has a side effect: no approval card, and they work under a hold and in read-only segments. Claude Agent Bots have them too.
- **How it runs**: in a child process the daemon starts (`--query-data`), on a read-only `query_only` connection, two at most at once; one past its deadline is killed, and the daemon never waits on it.
- **What cannot be read**: `remote_*`, `pending_keys`, `request_receipts` (remote access's keys and receipts), `notification_push_config`, `notification_devices` (push), `terminals` (yours), `sqlite_*`. Such a table reads as "no such table"; reaching one through a view or an index, opening another database or writing is refused from the query plan. Of the data folder only the two logs are read, never through a link, with anything that looks like a key blanked out.
- **What can be read includes** other Bots' directs and memories (read-only, never into your context) and anything you ever pasted into a chat — keys belong on approval cards, not in chat.
- **When**: only when you ask a Bot to analyze or improve something; what it finds goes into its own skills and memories, or into a built-in prompt through the approval card.

<a id="spend"></a>
## Spend

An append-only ledger of calls on this machine. Eight kinds of endpoint call each record one row per call: turn completion, judgement, model routing before a turn opens, correction-chain review, each learning step after a review, input suggestions, organizing, and acceptance checks. A call that shares its kind with others also records its purpose (`purpose`): the scribe is billed under organizing with the scribe as its purpose, and the calls of a continuity check that send frames under acceptance checks as picture judgements; line reading, a reflection and a retrospective are billed under organizing too, each as a line of its own; the summary call of a context compaction is billed under turn completion as a line of its own, "Context compaction", on the turn it condensed, and counts with the turns. Model routing and organizing belong to decision routing; review, learning steps, reflections and retrospectives together make up feedback routing; judgement is listed on its own, and input suggestions, acceptance checks, the scribe and picture judgements fall under "Other"; input suggestions, organizing, the scribe and acceptance checks belong to no Bot. A built-in call set to a Claude model is billed under the same kind and purpose as before, marked Claude Agent, at Claude Code's estimated price. In the view's category breakdown a purpose is a line of its own, and "Organizer" is only the organizer's own calls; filtering by a category or a line splits them the same way, and a purpose nothing was billed with yet takes no line. Each row stores the actual model, endpoint and thinking level, plus the session and Bot names at the time it was written; rows are kept when you delete a session, clear history or delete a Bot, deleted objects show their name snapshot marked "Deleted", and rows whose model could not be found during migration show "Unrecorded model". Tokens come only from the endpoint's `usage`; when missing, they show "—". When the provider returns `cost_in_usd_ticks`, the call is recorded as reported; with no reported cost, it is estimated when both input and output tokens are present and a billing price is configured. Estimates follow the currently configured prices: setting, changing or clearing a model's price recomputes all of its calls on that endpoint that have no reported cost, and a model removed from the list keeps its existing estimates. Cached input uses the cache price (left blank, it falls back to the input price; missing cached tokens count as 0); reasoning tokens are already included in output and are not added again. Reported and estimated amounts are totaled separately, and incomplete coverage is noted. The app has a separate spend view: a pane tab on desktop, an overlay on narrow screens and phones; you can filter it by time, model, session, Bot and call category, and see trends and per-call details; a detail row can jump back to its trigger message. The session top bar, list rows, settings and the model choice log show no spend figures. The time range and dimensions are saved only on this machine.
